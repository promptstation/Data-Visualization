# Domain Content Map — Foundations: Disability, Standards, and the Case for Accessible Visualization

Read this during Phase 3 (architecture) and Phase 4 (writing) of the module
development workflow. Verify all factual claims and numbers against the
authoritative sources before publishing (Phase 2 evidence base); flag
anything disputed rather than repeating it.

## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Frame data-visualization accessibility through the inclusion mandate and disability spectrum (permanent-temporary-situational model with prevalence and CVD statistics) grounded in the social-model reframe, curb-cut evidence, and nothing-about-us-without-us principle, navigate the standards-law landscape (WCAG-POUR system, ADA-Section-508-EAA-EN-301-549 obligations, litigation reality, graphics-guidance gaps).
2. Map the assistive-technology partnership (screen readers, magnification, voice control, switch-keyboard, braille, sonification frontiers), decompose accessible visualizations into semantic-alternative-visual-interaction-cognitive-testing layers with the five-stage maturity model locating overlay-widget delusions.
3. Build the business-quality case (market expansion, usability spillover, legal-risk reduction, SEO-robustness dividends).
4. Adopt the inclusive-engineer identity treating accessibility as architecture and evidence over checklist compliance.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Introduces the discipline and its obligations: accessibility defined for data visualization — the inclusion mandate (the source framing: making visualizations accessible to all users including people with visual, motor, and cognitive disabilities — the perceivable-operable-understandable-robust scope applied to charts, dashboards, maps, and interactive graphics), the disability-spectrum reality (the permanent-temporary-situational model — the Microsoft inclusive-design lineage: the blind screen-reader user, the low-vision magnification user, the color-blind analyst, the motor-impaired keyboard-only user, the cognitively-disabled comprehension-sensitive user, plus the temporary and situational counterparts: the arm-in-a-sling, the bright-sunlight, the distracted-context users widening the beneficiary population), the prevalence evidence (the documented disability statistics — the global-billion-plus population, the color-vision-deficiency rates: the roughly-eight-percent-of-males deuteranopia-protanopia prevalence, the age-related vision and motor changes affecting most users eventually) establishing accessibility as mainstream engineering not niche accommodation; the social-and-medical-model context — the framing shift: the medical-model legacy (the disability-as-individual-deficit framing driving afterthought accommodation), the social-model reframe (the disability-as-environment-barrier insight: the inaccessible-visualization-creates-the-exclusion not the impairment — the design-responsibility consequence), the curb-cut-effect evidence (the accessible-design universal-benefit pattern — the captions-serving-airports-and-gyms lineage, the data-table-and-direct-labeling improvements benefiting all chart readers), and the nothing-about-us-without-us principle (the disabled-user participation requirement in design and testing — the lived-experience authority) as the ethical foundation; the standards-and-law landscape — the compliance frame: the WCAG system (the Web-Content-Accessibility-Guidelines as the dominant technical standard — the version lineage and conformance levels A-AA-AAA, the POUR principle organization previewed for section 2), the legal drivers (the ADA and Section-508 obligations in the US context — the Title-II-and-III web-application rulings, the European-Accessibility-Act and EN-301-549 requirements taking effect across the EU with the 2025 compliance deadline, the public-sector procurement accessibility requirements globally), the litigation-and-enforcement reality (the documented web-accessibility lawsuit volumes and demand-letter patterns, the higher-education and e-commerce settlement precedents), and the standards-specific-to-graphics gap (the WCAG treatment of complex images and data visualization as an evolving area — the DIAGRAM-Center and W3C-task-force work filling the chart-specific guidance void) as the obligation landscape; the accessibility-principles primer — the POUR foundation: the perceivable principle (the information-presentable-in-multiple-modes — the text-alternatives, contrast, and reflow obligations applying to every chart), the operable principle (the functionality-reachable-via-multiple-inputs — the keyboard, switch, voice, and pointer paths to every interaction), the understandable principle (the content-and-operation-comprehensible — the plain-language, predictable, and error-tolerant requirements), the robust principle (the content-interpretable-by-assistive-technologies — the semantics-and-standards compliance enabling screen-reader access) as the four-pillar preview with the data-visualization-specific interpretation of each; the assistive-technology landscape — the partner systems: the screen-reader family (the NVDA-JAWS-VoiceOver-TalkBack ecosystem — the blind-and-low-vision primary tools whose interaction with SVG-canvas-HTML structures defines the semantic-access challenge), the magnification-and-zoom systems (the OS-and-browser magnifiers — the responsive-reflow-and-contrast requirements for enlarged viewing), the voice-control systems (the Dragon-and-native voice-control — the label-matching requirements for spoken commands), the switch-and-keyboard-only navigation (the motor-disability input paths — the focus-management-and-target-size obligations), the braille-displays and refreshable output (the tactile-data-access frontier), and the emerging-technologies (the sonification-and-haptic-display research traditions previewed for section 5) as the technology partnership map; the accessible-visualization-anatomy — the component view: the semantic-structure layer (the roles-labels-and-reading-order making graphics interpretable to assistive technology — the section-4 depth), the alternative-representation layer (the text-summaries, data-tables, and sonification providing non-visual access paths — the section-5 systems), the visual-accessibility layer (the color-contrast-CVD-and-typography provisions for low-vision and color-blind users — the section-6 craft), the interaction-accessibility layer (the keyboard-operability-and-focus-management for motor-disabled users — the section-7 systems), the cognitive-accessibility layer (the plain-language-motion-and-complexity provisions — the section-8 discipline), and the testing-verification layer (the audit-and-user-testing systems proving access — the section-12 practice) as the architecture preview; the accessibility-maturity-model — the growth path: the stage-one absence (the inaccessible-by-default graphics — the canvas-blob and decorative-SVG realities), the stage-two retrofit (the bolt-on fixes — the alt-text-and-overlay patches addressing symptoms), the stage-three systematic (the accessible-components-and-patterns — the design-system-integrated provisions), the stage-four architectural (the accessibility-by-default infrastructure — the generated-semantics-and-verified-equivalence systems), and the stage-five cultural (the disability-led-participation-and-universal-design — the inclusion-as-quality-identity maturity) with the overlay-widget-delusion pitfall located as the stage-two trap; the business-and-quality case — the value argument: the market-and-audience expansion (the billion-plus-user population and the aging-demographic trajectory — the accessible-products-reaching-more-people economics), the quality-and-usability spillover (the accessible-charts-as-better-charts evidence — the structure-clarity-and-redundant-encoding improvements benefiting everyone from the curb-cut tradition), the legal-and-reputational risk reduction (the litigation-cost-and-brand-damage avoidance, the public-sector-and-enterprise procurement-gate passage), the SEO-and-technical benefits (the semantic-structure-and-text-alternatives machine-readability dividends, the progressive-enhancement robustness), and the innovation-record (the accessibility-driven inventions — the typewriter-to-audiobook-to-predictive-text lineage) as the value case; the practitioner-identity — the inclusive-visualization engineer: the skill-composition (the standards fluency, the assistive-technology partnership, the inclusive-design craft, and the verification discipline as one professional capability), the mindset-shifts (the accessibility-as-architecture-not-accommodation conviction from the social model, the evidence-over-checklist discipline against the compliance-ceiling pitfall, the disability-community-partnership commitment), and the course-role framing (this discipline as the inclusion floor every other course's output must satisfy — the cross-curriculum obligation); and the course map from WCAG depth through assistive-technology mechanics, semantic graphics, text alternatives and sonification, color accessibility, keyboard-motor access, cognitive access, chart-type patterns, dashboard-table systems, interaction accessibility, testing methodology, library-framework realities, governance, and the capstone accessible-visualization system.
