# Domain Content Map — Dashboard Architecture: Designing Analytic Screen Systems

Read this during Phase 3 (architecture) and Phase 4 (writing) of the module
development workflow. Verify all factual claims and numbers against the
authoritative sources before publishing (Phase 2 evidence base); flag
anything disputed rather than repeating it.

## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Architect dashboards on the Few doctrine (importance curation, consolidation, single-screen glanceability) differentiated by purpose taxonomy (monitoring, analytic, reporting, operational), engineer glanceability through viewport budgets, five-second standards, panel restraint, and exception-based attention with CVD-safe status systems, compose through KPI-row-over-detail, overview-focus, and comparison arrangements with genre templates, craft metric displays (KPI-tile anatomy, trend semantics, sparklines, bullet graphs) under metric-honesty discipline.
2. Build filter-control architectures (global-local hierarchy, time navigation, comparison controls) with state visibility.
3. Design layered depth (glance-investigate-analyze-metadata) with mantra-continuous transitions and definition access.
4. Plan multi-dashboard suites with consistency systems and routing, engineer real-time operations (refresh architecture, wall displays, alert escalation, freshness trust).
5. Validate through NN/g-informed task testing and usage telemetry, and repair anti-patterns (anti-mantra dumps, metric graveyards, vanity dashboards, filter mazes) via the documented build workflow.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the flagship data-product structure: the dashboard defined precisely — the doctrine: the Few-lineage definition (the visual display of the most important information needed to achieve objectives, consolidated and arranged to fit a single screen — the importance-curation, consolidation, and glanceability triad), the dashboard-purpose taxonomy (the monitoring dashboards for state-awareness, the analytic dashboards for investigation and comparison, the reporting dashboards for periodic review, the operational dashboards for real-time action — the purpose-driven design differences), and the dashboard-is-not-a-report insight (the at-a-glance comprehension versus the sequential-reading consumption modes) as the definitional foundation; the glanceability engineering — the single-screen discipline: the one-screen principle (the consolidated-view requirement — the no-scroll ideal for monitoring contexts with the scrollable-analytic exceptions, the viewport-budget allocation across panels), the five-second-test standard (the key-status comprehension within seconds — the glanceability measurement, the headline-answer-first arrangement), the visual-simplicity management (the chart-count restraint — the five-to-nine panel cognitive guidance, the redundancy elimination — the one-metric-one-primary-view discipline), and the monitoring-specific engineering (the exception-based attention design — the normal-state-quiet, anomaly-loud pattern, the status-color and indicator conventions — the RAG traffic-light systems with the CVD-safe implementations) as the glanceability craft; the dashboard-composition patterns — the arrangement systems: the KPI-summary-row-over-detail convention (the headline-metrics band with the supporting-analysis grid beneath — the importance-descending arrangement), the overview-plus-focus compositions (the context-map or trend-panel anchoring the detailed breakdowns, the master-detail dashboard structures), the tabular-and-chart hybrids (the data-table panels with the visualization companions — the sort-filter-search table engineering), the comparison arrangements (the period-over-period, segment-versus-segment, and target-versus-actual panel structures — the delta-encoding conventions), and the dashboard-genre templates (the executive-summary, operational-monitoring, marketing-analytics, financial-review archetypes as starting structures requiring task validation) as the composition library; the metric-display craft — the number presentation: the big-number conventions (the KPI-tile anatomy — the value, label, trend-indicator, sparkline, and context-comparison components, the unit-and-precision formatting discipline), the trend-and-change encoding (the arrow-and-color direction indicators with the good-bad-direction semantics — the up-is-not-always-good caution, the period-comparison labeling clarity), the sparkline integration (the inline-trend miniatures — the Tufte-sparkline traditions with the endpoint-labeling and scale-consistency decisions), the target-and-threshold display (the goal-line and gauge patterns — the bullet-graph doctrine for compact target comparison, the progress-versus-pace distinctions), and the metric-honesty discipline (the axis-and-baseline integrity in tiles, the percentage-point-versus-percent clarity, the small-sample and estimate flagging) as the number craft; the filter-and-control architecture — the dashboard interactivity: the global-filter patterns (the date-range, segment, and geography controls affecting all panels — the control-placement conventions: the top-bar and sidebar positions, the filter-state visibility and reset discipline from the mantra section), the panel-local controls (the per-chart metric, breakdown, and view-type switchers — the control-discoverability balance), the time-navigation systems (the date-picker and period-selector design — the relative-versus-absolute range patterns, the time-granularity toggles: the day-week-month-quarter switching with the re-aggregation clarity), the comparison-controls (the period-offset and benchmark-selection mechanics), and the control-hierarchy design (the primary-controls-always-visible, secondary-controls-disclosed arrangement — the control-clutter prevention) as the interactivity architecture; the dashboard-information-layers — the depth structure: the layer model (the glance layer — the status and headlines; the investigate layer — the trends, breakdowns, and comparisons; the analyze layer — the drill-downs, filters, and raw-data access; the metadata layer — the sources, definitions, and freshness) with the progressive-disclosure mapping across layers (the section-9 integration), the layer-transition design (the drill-down and expansion mechanics preserving orientation — the mantra continuity, the in-place versus new-view detail decisions), and the layer-completeness audit (the every-metric-explainable requirement — the definition-and-source access from each display element) as the layered architecture; the multi-dashboard system design — the portfolio structure: the dashboard-suite planning (the audience-tiered suites — the executive, manager, and analyst dashboards as coordinated levels of the same data, the navigation-architecture connecting suite members), the consistency-systems across dashboards (the shared-metric definitions and visual conventions — the design-system integration from section 12, the cross-dashboard filter-and-time-state propagation patterns), the entry-point-and-routing design (the landing-dashboard decisions, the alert-driven deep-links into specific views, the personalized-versus-standard starting points), and the suite-maintenance realities (the metric-lifecycle management — the deprecated-metric handling, the version-and-rollout coordination across dashboard families) as the portfolio architecture; the real-time-and-operational dashboards — the live systems: the refresh-architecture decisions (the polling versus streaming update patterns — the refresh-rate-to-decision-need matching, the last-updated indication discipline), the live-display design (the wall-display and control-room layouts — the distance-legibility typography and contrast, the auto-rotation and cycling patterns for multi-view walls), the alert-and-escalation integration (the threshold-breach prominence systems — the alert-banner, panel-flash, and notification coordination with the salience-hygiene restraint, the alert-to-context navigation flows), and the stability-and-trust engineering (the data-freshness and lag indication from the streaming discipline, the degraded-data-state display — the partial-and-stale-data honesty) as the operational craft; the dashboard-usability evidence — the research layer: the documented usability findings (the NN/g dashboard research — the common failures: the meaningless-variety charts, the excessive-detail upfront, the poor-context provision, the inefficient-filtering patterns), the task-based evaluation methods (the representative-question testing — the can-users-answer-their-questions assessment, the time-to-insight and error measurement), the expert-review heuristics (the dashboard-review checklists — the glanceability, hierarchy, filter-architecture, and honesty audits), and the analytics-informed iteration (the dashboard-usage telemetry — the panel-attention and interaction analytics driving refinement) as the evidence practice; the dashboard-anti-patterns — the failure library: the anti-mantra dashboard (the raw-panel-dump requiring user-built overview from the pitfalls — the everything-shown-nothing-guided collapse), the metric-graveyard (the accumulated-KPI sprawl without curation — the importance-extinction through accumulation), the vanity-dashboard (the impressive-visuals-poor-decisions failure — the decoration-over-insight inversion), the filter-maze (the control-complexity overload — the choice-paralysis filtering), the stale-trust-killer (the outdated-or-unexplained data destroying adoption), and the one-size-fits-all failure (the single-dashboard-for-all-audiences compromise) with the systematic-repair approaches per pattern as the diagnostic discipline; the dashboard-design-process — the build workflow: the requirements-and-task elicitation (the audience-question interviews — the decisions-supported inventory, the metric-curation workshops), the information-architecture drafting (the zoning and panel planning from section 4 — the wireframe-first discipline), the data-and-metric specification (the definition, source, freshness, and calculation documentation — the metric-registry integration), the iterative-build-and-test cycles (the prototype-usability-refine loops with the five-second and task testing at each stage), and the launch-and-iteration operations (the adoption-telemetry monitoring, the feedback-channel establishment, the scheduled-review-and-retirement discipline) as the professional process; and the dashboard-architecture deliverable — the glanceable, layered, filtered, evidence-tested analytic-screen design capability.
