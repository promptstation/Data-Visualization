# Domain Content Map — Annotation, Labeling, and Explanatory Layers

Read this during Phase 3 (architecture) and Phase 4 (writing) of the module
development workflow. Verify all factual claims and numbers against the
authoritative sources before publishing (Phase 2 evidence base); flag
anything disputed rather than repeating it.

## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Design annotation systems through the function taxonomy (orientation, interpretation, evidence, provenance, action) with title-header systems spanning informative-takeaway versus descriptive spectrums under honesty discipline.
2. Execute in-chart labeling (direct labels with collision placement, axis-unit-tick conventions, selective value labeling, legend-reduction strategies) and callout vocabularies (leader lines, reference-threshold lines, event markers, shaded spans) with whitespace-seeking placement and toggleable layering.
3. Integrate narrative text layers (summary-detail placement, takeaway boxes, methodology caveats, inline glossaries) under text-visual hierarchy coordination.
4. Build dynamic annotation (state-driven updates, user-generated notes, rule-based automated callouts with review obligations).
5. Apply genre patterns (journalistic choreography, executive implications, operational status, scientific uncertainty), govern through terminology registries, style guides, localization workflows, and comprehension testing.
6. Diagnose clutter-orphan-stale-overreach-legend-dependency failures, and ensure accessibility through screen-reader exposure, alternatives, keyboard interaction, and text equivalents with comprehension-impact evaluation.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the explanation craft: the annotation-function taxonomy — the why-annotate analysis: the orientation annotations (the titles, subtitles, and context-setting text establishing what is shown and why — the chart-header anatomy), the interpretation annotations (the callouts, highlights, and explanatory notes directing reading — the this-matters-here signals), the evidence annotations (the reference lines, benchmarks, thresholds, and uncertainty displays adding comparison context — the meaning anchors), the provenance annotations (the source, date, method, and definition credits building trust — the metadata layer from the disclosure discipline), and the action annotations (the recommendation-and-next-step text in decision contexts) as the function taxonomy driving annotation-design decisions; the title-and-header system — the top-layer craft: the chart-title design (the informative-versus-descriptive title spectrum — the takeaway-titles stating the finding versus the neutral content labels, the audience-and-context-driven selection, the title-subtitle-footnote hierarchy from the typographic-hierarchy discipline), the dashboard-and-view header patterns (the page-title-and-context-subtitle conventions, the filter-and-time-range-state-in-header discipline — the active-context visibility), the question-title pattern (the interrogative titles framing the analytic question — the does-X-affect-Y headers guiding interpretation), and the title-honesty discipline (the claim-versus-evidence alignment — the overstated-takeaway prevention, the uncertainty-and-scope qualification in titles) as the header system;
