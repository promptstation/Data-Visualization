# Domain Content Map — Navigation, Wayfinding, and Orientation Systems

Read this during Phase 3 (architecture) and Phase 4 (writing) of the module
development workflow. Verify all factual claims and numbers against the
authoritative sources before publishing (Phase 2 evidence base); flag
anything disputed rather than repeating it.

## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Architect navigation systems (global-local-contextual-utility layers with breadth-favoring depth-breadth trade-offs) and wayfinding answering the four orientation questions through position indication, landmarks, maps-indexes, and transition continuity, engineer information scent via predictive labeling, previews, data-product terminology matching, and first-click verification.
2. Build analytic navigation patterns (workspaces, view sequences, dashboard suites, entity hierarchies, temporal movement).
3. Integrate search-as-navigation with scoped facets and no-result recovery.
4. Implement deep-linking state navigation with cold-start orientation and browser-history correctness.
5. Adapt to mobile-constrained contexts with thumb-zone patterns and responsive transformations, ensure keyboard-screen-reader-cognitive accessibility with AT auditing.
6. Optimize through path telemetry, lost-user detection, and A-B experiments, and repair maze-label-state-loss-proliferation failures systematically.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the movement-and-orientation engineering: the navigation-systems taxonomy — the movement architecture: the global-navigation patterns (the persistent top-level structure — the primary-menu, sidebar, and tab-bar architectures with the data-product adaptations: the workspace-and-view switchers), the local-navigation patterns (the within-section movement — the sub-menus, in-page anchors, and panel switching, the context-scoped navigation state), the contextual-navigation (the inline links and related-content paths — the drill-across and cross-reference movement, the breadcrumb-adjacent lateral navigation), the utility-navigation (the search, settings, help, and account access — the supporting-movement layer), and the navigation-hierarchy design (the depth-and-breadth trade-off — the wide-shallow versus narrow-deep structures: the breadth-favoring evidence for wayfinding, the seven-plus-minus grouping guidance at each level) as the movement architecture; the wayfinding-system design — the orientation engineering: the wayfinding-question framework (the where-am-I, where-can-I-go, how-do-I-get-there, how-do-I-get-back orientation questions — the systematic answer provision), the position-indication patterns (the active-state highlighting in menus and tabs, the breadcrumb trails with the hierarchy-path visibility, the page-and-section titles matching navigation labels — the label-consistency discipline), the landmark-and-reference-point design (the persistent-chrome elements anchoring orientation — the header, sidebar, and footer continuity, the visual-landmark distinctiveness per section), the map-and-index provisions (the sitemap-and-overview access, the search-as-wayfinding-recovery, the A-Z-and-catalog indexes for large content sets), and the transition-orientation support (the page-and-view-change continuity cues — the preserved-chrome and animated-transitions maintaining spatial context, the scroll-position-and-state restoration on return) as the orientation system; the information-scent engineering — the path-signaling craft: the scent-source design (the link-and-label wording predicting destination content — the descriptive-over-clever discipline, the partial-content previews and metadata signals — the thumbnails, counts, and date indicators), the scent-strength calibration (the high-scent primary paths versus ambient secondary options — the progressive-scent reduction with depth, the scent-consistency across navigation surfaces), the scent-for-data-products specifics (the metric-and-dimension naming as scent — the terminology-matching to user vocabulary from the labeling-systems tradition, the data-freshness and count signals in navigation items), and the scent-verification methods (the first-click testing for label effectiveness, the navigation-path analysis from telemetry — the expected-versus-actual path comparison) as the signaling craft; the navigation-patterns for analytic structures — the data-product specifics: the workspace-and-project navigation (the multi-analysis workspace switching — the saved-view and project-organization patterns, the recent-and-favorite access accelerators), the view-sequence navigation (the guided-analysis flows — the overview-to-detail-to-related progressions with the state carrying forward, the free-exploration versus structured-path balancing), the dashboard-suite navigation (the cross-dashboard movement with the filter-and-context propagation from section 5, the role-based navigation defaults), the data-entity navigation (the record-and-dimension movement — the entity-page hierarchies: the customer-product-region drill structures, the related-entity lateral links), and the temporal-navigation (the time-range and period movement — the previous-next-period conventions, the calendar-and-timeline navigation integration) as the analytic-navigation library; the search-as-navigation — the query movement: the search-placement-and-prominence (the global-search positioning conventions, the search-versus-browse balance by user-knowledge state — the known-item versus exploratory split from the mantra extensions), the search-scope-systems (the scoped-and-filtered search — the within-section and type-filtered queries, the faceted-search-result navigation with the refinement loops), the search-result-wayfinding (the result-context display — the location-and-hierarchy indication in results, the result-to-destination continuity), the autocomplete-and-suggestion navigation (the typeahead-as-shortcut patterns, the recent-and-popular accelerators), and the no-result-and-partial-result recovery (the suggestion-and-alternative provisions, the browse-fallback guidance, the search-analytics-driven improvement loops) as the query-navigation system; the deep-linking-and-state-navigation — the shareable movement: the URL-as-navigation-state architecture (the view, filter, and selection state encoded in URLs — the shareable-and-bookmarkable analysis states, the state-serialization discipline from the interaction frameworks), the deep-link-entry experiences (the cold-start orientation for link-arriving users — the context-reconstruction and wayfinding-restoration patterns, the landing-in-detail disorientation prevention), the browser-history integration (the back-and-forward button correctness — the history-state management for SPA data products, the navigation-interruption handling), the link-expiration-and-permission realities (the stale-link and access-denied recovery experiences), and the embedded-navigation contexts (the iframe-and-embed navigation constraints, the cross-application deep-linking patterns) as the state-navigation engineering; the mobile-and-constrained navigation — the adaptive movement: the mobile-navigation patterns (the bottom-tab and hamburger-menu architectures — the thumb-zone ergonomics, the progressive-menu disclosure, the gesture-navigation — the swipe-back and pull-refresh conventions), the constrained-context adaptations (the small-screen navigation simplification — the priority-based menu curation, the watch-and-kiosk navigation extremes at awareness), the touch-target-and-density engineering (the tap-target-size requirements, the navigation-density reduction for touch), and the responsive-navigation-transitions (the desktop-to-mobile navigation transformations — the sidebar-to-drawer and menu-collapse patterns with the state-preservation continuity) as the adaptive-navigation craft; the accessible-navigation — the inclusive movement: the keyboard-navigation architecture (the logical-tab-order engineering — the DOM-order-matches-visual-order discipline, the skip-link provisions for chrome bypass, the focus-trapping and management in overlays and menus, the keyboard-shortcut systems with the discoverability and conflict management), the screen-reader-navigation support (the landmark-regions and heading-structure navigation — the ARIA-landmark semantics, the navigation-state announcements — the current-page and expanded-collapsed communications), the cognitive-accessibility of navigation (the consistent-and-predictable structures, the plain-language labels, the orientation-support generosity), and the navigation-testing-with-assistive-technology (the keyboard-path and screen-reader-journey audits from the accessibility discipline) as the inclusive-navigation system; the navigation-analytics-and-optimization — the evidence loop: the navigation-telemetry instrumentation (the path-and-flow analytics — the view-sequence tracking, the navigation-element interaction rates, the search-query and result-click logging), the wayfinding-failure detection (the lost-user signals — the rapid-back-button, dead-end, and orphan-page patterns, the search-rescue frequency as the navigation-failure metric, the rage-click and wandering-session detection concepts), the A-B-and-iterative navigation optimization (the label, placement, and structure experiments — the task-success and time-to-destination metrics), and the persona-flow validation (the representative-journey testing per audience segment — the navigation-fit assessment) as the optimization system; the navigation-failure-modes — the diagnostic library: the maze failure (the disorienting-structure collapse — the deep-narrow hierarchies without landmarks or recovery, the dead-end accumulation), the label-failure (the terminology-mismatch scent collapse — the internal-jargon navigation labels, the ambiguous-and-overlapping categories), the state-loss failure (the context-destruction on movement — the filter-and-scroll resets, the back-button-breakage traps), the navigation-proliferation failure (the redundant-and-competing navigation systems — the multiple-menus-fighting confusion), and the mobile-navigation-afterthought failure (the desktop-structure-squeezed collapse) with the systematic-repair patterns as the diagnostic discipline; and the navigation deliverable — the orientation-complete, scent-rich, accessible, telemetry-optimized movement system.
