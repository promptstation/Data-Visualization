# Domain Content Map — Visual Hierarchy: Mechanics of Importance and Attention

Read this during Phase 3 (architecture) and Phase 4 (writing) of the module
development workflow. Verify all factual claims and numbers against the
authoritative sources before publishing (Phase 2 evidence base); flag
anything disputed rather than repeating it.

## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Engineer visual hierarchy through the complete emphasis toolkit (size, color-contrast.
2. Position, weight-density, restrained motion) organized into consistent three-to-four-level tier systems mapped to information priority.
3. Build typographic scales, emphasis-palette architectures with data-chrome color separation, and spatial zoning with proximity grouping, choreograph reading order through guided-scan design, inverted-pyramid narrative layering, and multi-audience progressive hierarchy.
4. Diagnose anti-hierarchy failures (flat emphasis, false hierarchy, inconsistency, over-hierarchy) with repair patterns.
5. Validate through eye-tracking-awareness methods, five-second and first-click testing, contrast measurement, and A-B experiments with accessibility parity verification.
6. Run the hierarchy process from priority elicitation through specification, iterative refinement.
7. Design-system governance.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the signaling craft: the hierarchy-mechanism inventory — the emphasis tools: the size-and-scale channel (the area and font-size differentials — the magnitude perception from the encoding-hierarchy tradition, the title-body-caption scale systems), the color-and-contrast channel (the saturation, brightness, and hue salience — the preattentive pop-out exploitation from course 2, the contrast-ratio ranges establishing dominance levels, the muted-versus-vivid figure-ground control), the position-and-order channel (the reading-pattern placement — the F-and-Z-pattern traditions, the top-left and center primacy, the serial-position effects in lists and grids), the weight-and-density channel (the bold, thickness, and ink-density differentials — the stroke-weight systems, the whitespace-as-emphasis — the isolation and spacing power), and the motion-and-change channel (the animation-attention capture with the restraint discipline — the vestibular and distraction costs from the accessibility tradition) as the complete emphasis toolkit; the hierarchy-level design — the tier system: the three-to-four-level convention (the primary-secondary-tertiary-mute tiers — the perceptible-level-count limits, the level-definition consistency across a product), the level-to-element mapping (the decisions-first hierarchy: the answers and anomalies at primary, the supporting evidence at secondary, the context and metadata at tertiary, the chrome and decoration at mute), the cross-level contrast requirements (the sufficient differential between adjacent levels — the near-miss ambiguity failure, the contrast-step verification), and the hierarchy audit method (the squint-test and grayscale-test techniques — the blur-and-desaturate verification that level structure survives, the five-second-test assessment of what-reads-first) as the tier-design craft; the typographic hierarchy — the text system: the type-scale architecture (the modular scales — the ratio-based size progressions, the heading-subheading-body-label-caption families with the line-height and letter-spacing adjustments per level), the weight-style-emphasis combinations (the bold-italic-case-color emphasis grammar — the limited-emphasis-styles discipline preventing dilution), the text-hierarchy in data contexts (the chart-title-subtitle-annotation layering, the axis-label and tick-label subordination, the legend and source-note quieting, the data-label prominence decisions), and the multilingual and responsive typography (the hierarchy preservation across scripts and sizes — the mobile-scale adjustments maintaining level relationships) as the typography system; the color-hierarchy engineering — the palette structure: the emphasis-palette architecture (the dominant-accent-neutral families — the single-accent-color discipline reserving salience for meaning, the gray-scale backbone carrying structure), the data-color versus chrome-color separation (the encoded-data colors protected from decoration competition — the interface-chrome neutrality), the sequential-importance gradients (the intensity ramps implying priority in lists and maps), and the dark-mode-and-theme hierarchy maintenance (the contrast-relationship preservation across themes — the level-structure invariance requirement) as the color structure; the layout-hierarchy integration — the spatial emphasis: the region-priority zoning (the page and screen region value maps — the prime-real-estate allocation to primary content, the fold-and-scroll hierarchy in long formats), the grouping-and-proximity signaling (the Gestalt grouping carrying structural relationships from course 1 — the proximity-implied-relatedness discipline, the border-and-background region definition), the alignment-and-grid authority (the aligned structure reading as organized and trustworthy — the misalignment-attention-capture caveat), and the density-rhythm patterns (the dense-versus-sparse region alternation creating scan landmarks — the visual-breathing-room function) as the spatial hierarchy; the dashboard-hierarchy application — the analytic emphasis: the KPI-hero patterns (the headline-metric prominence — the big-number conventions with the trend-and-context companions, the metric-importance ranking driving size and position), the chart-hierarchy within panels (the primary-chart-dominant layouts — the supporting-chart subordination, the panel-title and annotation prominence ladders), the alert-and-anomaly escalation (the exception-prominence patterns — the attention-capture for threshold breaches with the salience-hygiene discipline from course 2), and the scroll-and-tab hierarchy (the above-fold priority curation, the progressive-depth ordering in scrollable dashboards) as the analytic application; the hierarchy-for-comprehension-order — the reading choreography: the guided-scan design (the intended reading-path engineering — the entry-point, sequence, and exit management through hierarchy cues, the Z-and-F-pattern exploitation), the narrative-hierarchy in explanatory graphics (the headline-subhead-evidence-conclusion layering from the journalism tradition — the inverted-pyramid structure), the annotation-hierarchy (the callout, label, and footnote prominence tiers — the explanation-without-clutter balance), and the multi-audience hierarchy (the expert-versus-novice emphasis differences — the progressive-hierarchy patterns serving both through disclosure) as the reading-order craft; the anti-hierarchy failure modes — the diagnosis library: the flat-emphasis failure (the everything-important collapse from the pitfalls — the uniform-weight dashboards where nothing guides, the decorative-competition failure where chrome outshines data), the false-hierarchy failure (the emphasis mismatching importance — the visually-dominant elements carrying trivial content, the aesthetic-weight versus information-weight divergence), the hierarchy-inconsistency failure (the level-meaning drift across pages — the same-weight-different-importance confusion, the component-hierarchy contradiction), and the over-hierarchy failure (the excessive emphasis tiers fragmenting attention — the Christmas-tree effect) with the repair patterns per failure as the diagnostic discipline; the measurement-and-validation — the evidence layer: the attention-validation methods (the eye-tracking studies for scan-path verification at awareness, the first-click and five-second testing, the comprehension-order interviews), the quantitative-emphasis checks (the contrast-ratio and size-differential measurement against defined level thresholds, the visual-weight scoring heuristics), the A-B-hierarchy experiments (the emphasis-variant comparison on task performance — the finding-time and error-rate metrics), and the responsive-and-accessibility hierarchy verification (the level-structure survival across breakpoints, the screen-reader-and-keyboard hierarchy parity — the heading-structure and DOM-order correspondence) as the validation practice; the hierarchy-design-process — the workflow layer: the content-priority elicitation (the stakeholder-and-user importance ranking — the what-matters-first conversations, the data-driven priority evidence — the usage analytics informing emphasis), the hierarchy-specification artifacts (the level-definition documents, the emphasis-style guides, the wireframe-weight annotations), the iterative-hierarchy-refinement (the draft-audit-adjust cycles with the squint-and-five-second checks at each stage), and the hierarchy-governance (the design-system enforcement from section 12 — the token-and-component level consistency) as the process discipline; and the visual-hierarchy deliverable — the attention-engineering capability making structural importance visible and verifiable.
