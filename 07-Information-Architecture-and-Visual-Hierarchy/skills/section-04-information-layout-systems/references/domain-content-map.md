# Domain Content Map — Layout Systems: Grids, Zoning, and Spatial Organization

Read this during Phase 3 (architecture) and Phase 4 (writing) of the module
development workflow. Verify all factual claims and numbers against the
authoritative sources before publishing (Phase 2 evidence base); flag
anything disputed rather than repeating it.

## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Build layout systems on grid anatomy (columns, gutters, baseline grids) with intentional-break discipline.
2. Select structural archetypes (single-column narrative, panel-canvas, dashboard-grid, immersive) through functional zoning and prime-real-estate analysis, engineer panel-card container architectures with consistent anatomy and user-resizable state persistence, calibrate whitespace and density by audience and context with crowding-failure management.
3. Design responsive breakpoint and container-query systems preserving hierarchy, architect scroll-viewport depth (fold curation, sticky controls, pagination decisions) and multi-page view portfolios with cross-page consistency and state flow.
4. Apply alignment-proportion-optical-balance geometry systems.
5. Evaluate through scan-path testing, task-flow analysis, grid audits, and responsive matrices, and repair layout anti-patterns (accumulation, maze, rigidity, monotony, responsive afterthought) through re-zoning and template redesign.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the spatial-structure craft: the grid-system foundation — the structural skeleton: the grid anatomy (the columns, rows, gutters, margins, and modules — the baseline-grid typography integration, the column-grid conventions — the 12-column web tradition and its rationale, the modular-grid composition for complex layouts), the grid-function analysis (the alignment-consistency production reducing cognitive friction, the proportion-and-rhythm establishment, the responsive-breakpoint scaffolding), and the grid-flexibility discipline (the intentional-break patterns — the full-bleed and spanning exceptions with purpose, the grid-abandonment costs) as the skeleton foundation; the layout-pattern vocabulary — the structural archetypes: the single-column-flow (the narrative and report layouts — the scroll-driven linear consumption), the two-and-three-column patterns (the sidebar-content and content-sidebar-content arrangements — the navigation, filter, and detail panel placements), the dashboard-grid patterns (the card-and-panel matrix layouts — the KPI-row-over-chart-grid conventions, the bento-box compositions), the canvas-and-panel patterns (the main-visualization-with-floating-controls arrangements — the map-and-overlay layouts from the spatial discipline, the focus-context side-by-side structures), and the full-screen-immersive patterns (the presentation and kiosk layouts, the scrollytelling stage arrangements from the narrative discipline) as the archetype library; the zoning-methodology — the region-design process: the functional-zoning approach (the header-identity, navigation, control, content, detail, and footer-metadata zone taxonomy — the region-purpose assignment before element placement), the prime-real-estate analysis (the attention-value mapping by region — the above-fold and top-left primacy evidence, the zone-value-to-content-importance matching from the hierarchy discipline), the flow-and-adjacency planning (the task-sequence-driven zone arrangement — the filter-beside-results and overview-above-detail proximity logic, the eye-movement-path design), and the zoning-documentation artifacts (the wireframe zoning diagrams — the region-priority annotation conventions) as the zoning process; the panel-and-card architecture — the container system: the panel-anatomy conventions (the title, subtitle, control, content, and footnote regions within panels — the consistent container structure enabling scan efficiency), the card-system design (the uniform-card grids for comparable units — the metric-card and entity-card patterns, the card-information-density balance), the panel-grouping strategies (the related-panel clustering through proximity and shared headers — the group-boundary marking via whitespace, rules, and background shifts), and the resizable-and-collapsible panels (the user-layout-control patterns — the drag-resize, collapse, and rearrange capabilities with the state-persistence obligations) as the container craft; the whitespace-and-density engineering — the breathing system: the whitespace-function taxonomy (the separation-whitespace between unrelated groups, the association-whitespace tightness within groups — the proximity-principle quantification, the padding-and-margin systems), the density-calibration by audience and context (the information-dense expert layouts versus the comfortable consumer presentations — the density-per-persona discipline, the monitoring-wall versus desktop-laptop versus mobile density steps), the density-measurement approaches (the elements-per-viewport metrics, the ink-and-whitespace ratio assessments), and the crowding-failure management (the visual-noise diagnosis — the element-collision and scan-path-fragmentation symptoms, the progressive-density reduction sequence) as the density craft; the responsive-layout systems — the adaptive structure: the breakpoint architecture (the device-and-container breakpoint families — the layout-transformation rules per breakpoint: the column-reflow, panel-stacking, and navigation-collapse patterns), the container-query and component-level adaptation (the element-responsive layouts independent of viewport — the panel-internal rearrangement), the mobile-layout patterns for data products (the single-column-priority structures, the bottom-navigation and sheet patterns, the progressive-disclosure mobile adaptation from section 9, the touch-target and thumb-zone ergonomics), and the responsive-hierarchy preservation (the level-structure maintenance across breakpoints — the mobile-emphasis re-curation rather than blind stacking) as the adaptive system; the scroll-and-viewport architecture — the depth structure: the fold-management decisions (the above-fold curation discipline — the critical-content placement evidence and the fold-myth nuances, the scroll-past-fold justification for narrative contexts), the scroll-structure patterns (the sticky-header-and-control persistence, the section-anchoring and scroll-spy navigation, the infinite-scroll versus pagination decisions for data lists), the lazy-load-and-progressive-render coordination (the scroll-performance integration from the latency discipline — the placeholder and skeleton patterns), and the scroll-storytelling layouts (the staged-scroll arrangements from the narrative discipline — the pinned-and-stepped viewport choreography) as the depth architecture; the alignment-and-proportion systems — the relational geometry: the alignment-discipline (the shared-edge and center-alignment networks creating visual connection — the misalignment-as-signal intentionality, the optical-versus-mathematical alignment adjustments), the proportion-and-ratio systems (the golden-and-root ratios at awareness, the practical panel-aspect-ratio decisions by chart type — the time-series-width versus scatter-square conventions), the modular-scale consistency (the spacing-scale token systems — the 4-or-8-point spacing conventions, the size-and-space relationship families), and the optical-balance adjustments (the visual-weight distribution across layouts — the asymmetric-balance craft for dynamic compositions) as the geometry system; the multi-page-and-multi-view layout architecture — the product-level structure: the page-portfolio planning (the view-inventory and purpose mapping — the dashboard, detail, exploration, and settings page families, the navigation-graph design connecting views), the cross-page layout consistency (the shared-chrome and grid continuity — the persistent header, sidebar, and control-region architecture, the template-family design), the cross-view state and context flow (the filter-and-selection persistence across page transitions, the back-and-forward orientation maintenance), and the application-shell patterns (the frame-and-content architecture for analytics applications — the workspace, panel-docking, and multi-window patterns) as the product structure; the layout-evaluation methods — the structural QA: the scan-path-and-attention testing (the layout-guided eye-movement verification — the first-look and hotspot assessment, the five-second comprehension checks), the task-flow-through-layout analysis (the navigation-path efficiency measurement on layout variants — the click-and-scroll counting, the task-completion timing), the grid-and-alignment audits (the systematic alignment verification — the overlay-grid inspection, the spacing-token compliance checks), and the responsive-layout testing matrices (the breakpoint-and-device coverage testing — the reflow and overflow defect detection) as the evaluation toolkit; the layout-anti-patterns — the failure library: the layout-by-accumulation failure (the panel-growth-without-plan collapse from the pitfalls — the dead-grid and implied-false-grouping symptoms with the re-zoning remedy), the maze-layout failure (the navigation-structure confusion — the dead-end and orphan-view problems), the template-rigidity failure (the content-forced-into-fixed-slots distortion — the wrong-aspect-ratio and orphan-panel symptoms), the symmetric-monotony failure (the undifferentiated grid repetition flattening hierarchy), and the responsive-afterthought failure (the desktop-layout-squeezed-to-mobile degradation) with the structural-repair patterns as the diagnostic discipline; and the layout-systems deliverable — the grid-zoning-panel-density-responsive spatial-organization mastery.
