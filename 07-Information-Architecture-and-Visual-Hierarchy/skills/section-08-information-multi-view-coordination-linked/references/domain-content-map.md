# Domain Content Map — Multi-View Coordination and Linked Interaction

Read this during Phase 3 (architecture) and Phase 4 (writing) of the module
development workflow. Verify all factual claims and numbers against the
authoritative sources before publishing (Phase 2 evidence base); flag
anything disputed rather than repeating it.

## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Architect coordinated multi-view systems through view-role taxonomy (overview-detail, complementary representation, cross-dataset, context-focus) with task-fit and cognitive-cost decision analysis, deploy linking mechanisms (brushing-linking, hover highlighting, linked filtering-navigation, master-detail) on central-state architectures with star-mesh-hierarchy topologies, batched update propagation, and state serialization, arrange coordinated layouts with visual connection signaling and responsive stacking, enforce cross-view coherence (shared encodings, scale coordination, granularity alignment, terminology consistency).
2. Build comparison patterns (small multiples, scenario pairs.
3. Map-timeline-chart triads).
4. Manage interaction economics through focus clarity, optional coordination, and cascade-error prevention.
5. Implement via coordination engines, join-key management, and indexed performance with propagation testing.
6. Diagnose invisibility-cascade-inconsistency-over-coordination failures.
7. Evaluate through cross-view task measurement and coordination telemetry.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the coordinated-views discipline: the multi-view concept — the distributed-comprehension architecture: the coordinated-views principle (the multiple simultaneous representations of related data with linked interaction — the whole-greater-than-parts comprehension through complementary perspectives, the relate-task support from the mantra framework), the view-role taxonomy (the overview-and-detail pairings, the multiple-representation views — the same-data-different-encoding complements, the different-data-related-views — the cross-dataset connection displays, the context-and-focus arrangements), the single-view-versus-multi-view decision analysis (the complexity-versus-screen-budget trade-offs, the task-fit assessment — the comparison-and-correlation tasks favoring coordination, the cognitive-cost accounting — the attention-division and cross-view-integration burden from the load discipline) as the architectural foundation; the linking-mechanisms — the coordination vocabulary: the brushing-and-linking pattern (the selection-in-one-view highlighting-in-others — the canonical coordination mechanism, the brush-source and highlight-target roles, the multi-view brush propagation architectures), the linked-highlighting and hover-coordination (the hover-driven cross-view emphasis — the shared-identity highlighting via keys and dimensions, the highlight-latency and performance discipline from the interaction frameworks), the linked-filtering (the selection-as-filter propagation — the click-to-filter cross-view subsetting with the filter-state visibility obligations, the filter-scope decisions: the global versus pairwise coordination), the linked-navigation (the cross-view zoom, pan, and scroll synchronization — the spatial and temporal coordination for maps, timelines, and series), and the master-detail coordination (the selection-driven detail-panel updates — the list-chart-detail triads, the detail-view context preservation) as the linking toolkit; the coordination-architecture patterns — the system design: the central-state architectures (the shared-selection-and-filter state driving all views — the single-source-of-truth discipline from the framework-integration traditions, the state-store patterns and update propagation), the view-communication topologies (the star topology — the central-coordinator model, the mesh and pairwise topologies — the direct-view-linking for specific relationships, the hierarchy topologies for overview-detail-detail chains), the update-performance management (the coordinated-update batching and debouncing — the frame-budget discipline under multi-view propagation cascades, the partial-update targeting — the affected-view-only re-rendering), and the coordination-state-serialization (the multi-view state capture for sharing and bookmarking — the complete-view-configuration deep-linking from the navigation discipline) as the system architecture; the layout-for-coordination — the spatial arrangement: the coordinated-view arrangements (the side-by-side comparison layouts, the overview-anchored satellite arrangements — the central-map or timeline with the linked panels, the grid-matrix layouts for multi-dimensional comparison pairs — the scatterplot-matrix tradition), the visual-connection-signaling (the shared-color-and-symbol encoding across views indicating linkage — the consistent-identity-encoding discipline, the connection-line and highlight-animation cues for cross-view relationships, the shared-axis-and-scale alignment for comparison views), the proximity-and-grouping for related views (the coordinated-set visual containment — the border-and-background grouping from the layout discipline), and the responsive-coordination adaptations (the mobile multi-view stacking with the linkage-state preservation, the coordinated-view disclosure — the progressive revelation of satellite views) as the arrangement craft; the cross-view-encoding-consistency — the coherence system: the shared-encoding discipline (the same-dimension-same-encoding across views — the color, shape, and size consistency for entity identity, the encoding-legend unification), the scale-coordination decisions (the shared-versus-independent axis scales — the comparison-accuracy versus detail-visibility trade-offs, the synchronized-domain patterns for time and value axes), the aggregation-level-alignment (the consistent-granularity awareness across views — the mixed-granularity confusion prevention with the explicit-granularity labeling), and the terminology-and-label-consistency (the same-entity-same-name discipline across view titles, legends, and tooltips — the labeling-system integration) as the coherence engineering; the comparison-view-patterns — the analytic arrangements: the small-multiples coordination (the repeated-view matrices with the shared-encoding and aligned-scales — the Tufte tradition from section 10, the cross-panel brushing and highlighting integration), the before-after-and-scenario comparison (the paired-view synchronization — the aligned time-period and parameter comparisons, the difference-and-delta view derivations), the parallel-coordinates-and-matrix hybrids (the multi-dimensional comparison systems with the linked-selection propagation), the geographic-and-temporal coordination (the map-timeline-chart triads — the spatial-temporal-attribute linking patterns from the spatial discipline), and the comparison-interaction design (the view-pairing controls — the compare-mode activation, the swap-and-align affordances) as the comparison library; the multi-view-interaction-economics — the usability layer: the attention-management across views (the interaction-focus clarity — the active-view indication, the cross-view-action predictability, the coordination-discovery — the how-views-relate learnability through consistent affordances), the interaction-cost-benefit design (the coordination-complexity restraint — the linking-where-tasks-require discipline, the optional-coordination patterns — the user-controlled link-toggling), the error-and-confusion prevention (the circular-and-cascading-filter traps — the filter-loop prevention, the coordination-state-recovery — the reset-all-and-unlink mechanisms, the visual-state-clarity under complex coordination — the active-filter and selection summary panels), and the multi-view-usability-testing (the cross-view-task testing — the coordination-discovery and integration measurement, the cognitive-load assessment of coordinated layouts) as the usability engineering; the technical-implementation-patterns — the engineering layer: the coordination-frameworks-and-libraries (the crossfilter-family filtered-dataset coordination engines, the visualization-framework linking support — the shared-state patterns in D3-React-Vue ecosystems from the framework-integration discipline), the data-identity-and-key-management (the join-key discipline for cross-view entity matching — the stable-identifier requirements, the many-to-many relationship handling), the performance-under-coordination (the index-and-aggregation strategies for live linked-filtering at scale — the precomputation-versus-runtime trade-offs, the worker-offload patterns for heavy coordination updates), and the coordination-testing (the state-propagation unit-and-integration testing, the interaction-sequence regression testing for linked behaviors) as the implementation craft; the multi-view-failure-modes — the diagnostic library: the coordination-invisibility failure (the linked-views-without-discoverable-linkage — the user-unaware-coordination waste), the filter-cascade-confusion failure (the propagated-filters-losing-track — the invisible-global-filter-state disorientation), the encoding-inconsistency failure (the same-entity-different-color cross-view identity confusion), the over-coordination failure (the everything-linked-everything attention fragmentation and interaction amplification), and the performance-collapse failure (the coordination-cascades destroying responsiveness) with the systematic-repair patterns as the diagnostic discipline; the evaluation-of-coordinated-systems — the evidence layer: the task-effectiveness measurement (the cross-view-insight-task performance — the comparison and correlation task success versus single-view baselines), the coordination-usage analytics (the linked-interaction telemetry — the brush-propagation and filter-link usage patterns, the coordination-feature-adoption measurement), the cognitive-load-and-comprehension assessment (the integration-burden measurement — the cross-view-comprehension testing from the load discipline), and the iterative-coordination-refinement (the linkage-map optimization through usage evidence — the add-remove-link decisions) as the evaluation practice; and the multi-view deliverable — the coordinated-view architecture, linking, layout, coherence, and usability engineering capability.
