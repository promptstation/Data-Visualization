# Domain Content Map — Perceiving Latency: Psychophysics of Response and Motion

Read this during Phase 3 (architecture) and Phase 4 (writing) of the module
development workflow. Verify all factual claims and numbers against the
authoritative sources before publishing (Phase 2 evidence base); flag
anything disputed rather than repeating it.

## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Ground latency budgets in response-time psychophysics (Nielsen-Card lineage, just-noticeable-difference asymmetries, direct-manipulation immediacy, context-dependent tolerance) and motion-perception science (60fps coupled-motion standards, frame-time-variance sensitivity, jank classification, motion continuity, vestibular comfort).
2. Design multi-sensory feedback timing (100ms acknowledgment, progress thresholds, input echo, feedback-honesty balance) applying Maister wait-psychology principles, progress-indicator psychophysics, skeleton perception, and peak-end effects.
3. Derive differentiated budgets per interaction class (hover 50-100ms, click 100ms, drag frame-rate, filter 100-1000ms, load seconds) accounting for expertise, device, and accessibility variance.
4. Measure perception through discrimination tests, subjective ratings, and behavioral frustration indicators.
5. Integrate perception into design workflows (budget derivation, feedback layering.
6. Design-review criteria, heuristics documentation), and navigate myths (faster-always-better, fps obsession, single-number latency, lab-field gaps) with critical nuance.

the expertise-and-frequency effects — the user-population variance: the power-user latency sensitivity (the repeated-interaction amplification — the expert-users detecting-and-suffering small latencies through volume, the flow-state disruption costs for high-frequency tasks), the novice-perception differences (the cognitive-load masking latency for novices versus experts — the load-discipline integration, the learning-phase tolerance versus proficiency-phase intolerance), the device-and-context variance (the mobile-touch latency expectations versus desktop-pointer, the connectivity-context expectation calibration), and the accessibility-latency-interaction (the assistive-technology overhead compounding latency — the screen-reader-and-switch-access timing budgets, the cognitive-disability wait-tolerance considerations) as the population-awareness layer; the perception-measurement methods — the evidence toolkit: the latency-discrimination testing (the A-B perceptual-comparison protocols for interaction-timing decisions, the threshold-finding methods at practitioner depth), the subjective-responsiveness rating (the perceived-speed surveys and the system-usability-scale responsiveness items, the experience-sampling during task completion), the behavioral-latency-indicators (the rage-click-and-retry detection — the frustration-behavior signals, the abandonment-and-task-switching telemetry as latency-pain evidence, the interaction-hesitation patterns), and the psychophysics-informed-test-design (the stimulus-timing precision requirements, the order-and-adaptation effects in latency experiments) as the measurement practice;
