# Domain Content Map — Metrics, KPIs, and Measurement Systems: Designing What Gets Measured

Read this during Phase 3 (architecture) and Phase 4 (writing) of the module
development workflow. Verify all factual claims and numbers against the
authoritative sources before publishing (Phase 2 evidence base); flag
anything disputed rather than repeating it.

## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Design measurement systems through foundations (measurement-drives-attention governance principle, metric-as-model reading, metric anatomy with definition-metadata-denominator-window specifications, classification across outcome-driver and scorecard perspectives, systems thinking with metric trees and cascades).
2. Build KPIs with discipline (SMART-plus criteria with actionability-sensitivity-interpretability tests, target systems with gaming resistance, OKR-balanced-scorecard-north-star framework integration, portfolio governance with addition-retirement and change management, vanity metric identification and replacement), ensure computation quality (lineage documentation, formula verification with edge cases and reconciliation, limitation documentation with confidence communication, anomaly monitoring and drift review).
3. Communicate metrics (context-mandatory presentation with comparison sets and trend displays, narrative integration with exception commentary, visual standards with consistency, distribution systems with alert management).
4. Apply honest critique (Goodhart management portfolios, attribution-causality limits with experiment integration, coverage blindness with qualitative complements, privacy-fairness-transparency ethics), and avoid vanity portfolios, definition chaos, gaming blindness, bare numbers, stale drift, trust inheritance, and measurement totalism failures.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the measurement-design discipline determining what executives see, believe, and act upon: the measurement foundations — metrics as organizational instruments: the metric concept and power (the measurement-drives-attention principle where what gets measured gets managed, making metric selection a governance act with behavioral consequences; the metric-as-model reading where every indicator embodies a theory of the business reducing complex reality to trackable signals with inevitable information loss; the abstraction-ladder from strategic objectives through key results to operational measures, connecting measurement design to organizational strategy); the metric anatomy (the definition components requiring name, formula, unit, grain, filter, and source specification for unambiguous computation; the metric-metadata obligations including owner, refresh cadence, known limitations, and interpretation guidance; the numerator-denominator discipline where rate metrics demand explicit denominator definitions preventing the classic comparison failures; the time-window semantics covering trailing, rolling, and period-to-date constructions with their trend-reading consequences); the metric classification (the outcome versus driver distinction separating lagging results from leading indicators, with the predictive-relationship validation obligation; the quantitative versus qualitative balance where satisfaction surveys and sentiment measures complement operational counts; the financial versus operational versus customer versus capability categorization from the balanced-scorecard tradition providing portfolio coverage; the input-output-outcome-impact chain distinguishing activity measures from effect measures, critical in program and marketing contexts); and the measurement-systems thinking (the system-of-metrics concept where individual KPIs function within portfolios that must jointly cover objectives without redundant or conflicting signals; the hierarchy-cascade where organizational metrics decompose through business units to teams with alignment and aggregation logic; the metric-tree construction where driver relationships structure diagnostic navigation from outcomes through contributors, the analytic backbone of section 4's dashboard designs) as the foundations layer; the KPI design discipline — building good metrics: the SMART-and-beyond criteria (the specific-measurable-achievable-relevant-time-bound baseline conventions with their known limitations; the actionability test where a good KPI implies specific responses when it moves, failing metrics generate discussion without direction; the sensitivity-and-stability balance where metrics must respond to real change without thrashing on noise, the signal-to-noise design challenge; the interpretability requirement where metric movement direction and magnitude carry unambiguous meaning to the audience); the target-setting systems (the target-and-threshold design where goals, alert levels, and normal ranges give metrics decision meaning per the gauge design of section 4; the baseline-and-benchmark logic where historical performance, peer comparison, and theoretical limits inform target realism; the stretch-versus-commitment target distinction with their different motivational and planning functions from the OKR traditions; the target-gaming awareness where measured targets invite behavior distortion, the Goodhart's-law discipline requiring gaming-resistance review); the OKR and framework integration (the objective-and-key-result structure where qualitative ambition pairs with quantitative measurement, the modern goal-system convention; the balanced-scorecard heritage with its four-perspective portfolio logic and strategy-map causality chains from the Kaplan-Norton tradition; the north-star-metric concept from the product-analytics traditions where one indicator proxies sustainable value creation for a business model; the framework-selection judgment matching goal-system architecture to organizational maturity and culture rather than fashion); the metric-portfolio governance (the addition-and-retirement discipline where new metrics justify their attention cost and stale metrics get formally retired, preventing dashboard sprawl; the definition-change management where metric formula modifications carry version control, communication, and restatement obligations from the trust systems of section 12; the cross-functional-consistency requirements where the same business concept carries one definition across teams, the semantic-layer challenge of section 5); and the vanity-metric discipline (the vanity identification criteria including cumulative-only metrics that never decline, activity counts disconnected from outcomes, and cherry-picked indicators selected for flattery; the actionability-replacement practice converting vanity metrics into decision-relevant alternatives; the cultural-honesty requirement where organizations tolerate metrics showing unfavorable truths, previewing section 13's culture systems) as the design layer; the metric computation and quality — the data foundation: the data-source lineage (the source-system identification where metrics trace to operational systems of record with transformation documentation; the pipeline-reliability dependencies where metric trust inherits data-engineering quality, connecting to the BI systems of section 5; the freshness-and-latency semantics where as-of timestamps and update cadences shape interpretation and prevent stale-data decisions); the computation-correctness assurance (the formula-implementation verification where metric code matches metric definition through independent recomputation; the edge-case handling covering null values, zero denominators, partial periods, and retroactive data corrections with documented conventions; the reconciliation practice where metric totals cross-check against financial and operational systems of record, the tie-out discipline from the accounting traditions); the known-limitations documentation (the measurement-error sources including sampling, attribution, instrumentation gaps, and definition boundaries recorded with every metric; the interpretation-guidance provision where typical misreadings get preemptive documentation; the confidence-communication where metric quality differences inform presentation language per section 11's uncertainty systems); and the quality-monitoring operations (the anomaly-detection on metric pipelines where sudden shifts trigger data-quality investigation before business interpretation, connecting to the operational monitoring traditions; the drift-and-decay review where metric relevance degrades as business models evolve, motivating periodic portfolio audits; the user-feedback integration where metric consumers report confusion and misuse feeding definition improvement) as the quality layer; the metric communication systems — presenting measurements: the context-mandatory presentation (the comparison-set discipline where every metric displays against target, prior period, prior year, and peer or benchmark contexts as the minimum interpretive frame; the trend-and-seasonality display where sparklines and small multiples carry temporal context compactly per the charting traditions of the visualization courses; the magnitude-and-change separation where level and movement receive distinct visual treatment preventing conflation; the denominator-and-scope annotation where rates carry their bases and aggregates carry their filters, the honesty conventions from the semiographics course); the narrative-metric integration (the metric-story-craft where numbers anchor claims about business performance with the annotation and emphasis systems from the narrative traditions; the exception-commentary convention where significant movements receive explanation or explicit investigation-status notes; the metric-review-meeting formats where numbers structure discussion through prepared commentary and live drill-down, connecting to section 8's briefing skills); the visual presentation standards (the KPI-card design patterns combining value, change, trend, target status, and context in scannable modules per section 4's dashboard craft; the color-and-status conventions with their accessibility and cultural obligations from the semiographics traditions; the consistency requirements where the same metric displays identically across artifacts, the ecosystem-coherence discipline of section 1); and the distribution and access systems (the push-and-pull balance between scheduled metric delivery and on-demand exploration per section 14's channel systems; the role-based metric views where executives, managers, and operators see appropriate aggregations of one measurement system; the mobile-and-alert channels where threshold crossings reach decision makers through notification systems with alert-fatigue management) as the communication layer; the measurement critique — the honest limits: the Goodhart's-law management (the target-corruption dynamics where measures cease to be good measures once targets, with the mechanism analysis of gaming, selection, and distortion behaviors; the mitigation portfolio including metric rotation, audit practices, qualitative balance, and gaming-detection analytics; the incentive-system interaction where metric consequences in compensation amplify distortion pressures, requiring human-resources collaboration); the attribution-and-causality limits (the correlation-presentation-caution where metric relationships imply causation only with analytic support, applying the framing honesty from the rhetoric traditions; the external-factor confounding where market, seasonal, and competitive movements drive metrics independent of organizational action; the experiment-and-holdout integration where causal claims rest on designed comparison rather than observational movement); the coverage-blindness awareness (the unmeasured-dimensions inventory where important qualities escape metric systems including culture, innovation capacity, and long-term risk positions; the qualitative-complement requirements where interviews, observation, and narrative reporting cover metric blind spots; the metric-fixation diagnosis where organizations mistake the measured subset for business reality, a cultural condition addressed in section 13); and the ethical-measurement boundaries (the privacy-and-surveillance line where employee and customer measurement raises consent and dignity questions from the data-ethics traditions; the fairness-and-bias review where metrics and targets distribute consequences unevenly across groups requiring equity analysis; the transparency-obligations where measured parties understand how metrics work, the algorithmic-accountability principle applied to KPI systems) as the critique layer; and the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the vanity-metric portfolio, dashboards crowded with flattering indicators disconnected from decisions, remedied by the actionability test and portfolio governance; the undefined-KPI chaos, metrics with disputed formulas producing contradictory numbers across teams, remedied by the anatomy specification and semantic consistency systems; the target-gaming blindness, celebrating metric improvement produced by distortion rather than performance, remedied by the Goodhart mitigation portfolio; the bare-number reporting, metrics without comparison context forcing interpretation guesswork, remedied by the context-mandatory presentation discipline; the stale-definition drift, metrics unchanged while business models evolved until they measure nothing relevant, remedied by the drift-review and retirement discipline; the pipeline-trust-inheritance, accepting metric values without source and computation verification, remedied by the correctness assurance and reconciliation practice; and the measurement-totalism, treating unmeasured dimensions as nonexistent, remedied by the coverage-blindness inventory and qualitative complements with detection methods as the diagnostic.
