# Domain Content Map — Accessible SVG and Inclusive Visualization

Read this during Phase 3 (architecture) and Phase 4 (writing) of the module
development workflow. Verify all factual claims and numbers against the
authoritative sources before publishing (Phase 2 evidence base); flag
anything disputed rather than repeating it.

## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Build accessible SVG through role-title-desc semantics, graphics-module roles, focusability, and DOM-order reading discipline, author text alternatives on the summary-to-detailed spectrum answering what-data-shows with human-authored meaning, provide data-table and download equivalents through shared-source synchronization, enforce color inclusion (contrast ratios, CVD palettes, redundant encoding, dark-print parity) and keyboard interaction parity (roving tabindex, value announcements, hover-focus equivalence, dynamic focus management), gate motion through reduced-motion integration and flash safety.
2. Verify via screen-reader sessions, automated CI scanning, and disabled-user testing, scale through accessible component APIs and design-system tokens, and navigate complex-visualization frontiers with honest-limitation documentation within the curb-cut quality mindset.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the inclusion obligation and craft: the accessibility-problem statement — the exclusion reality: the default-inaccessibility of data graphics (the SVG-as-picture problem — screen readers announcing nothing or nonsense for unannotated charts, the color-only encodings excluding CVD viewers, the interaction-only information excluding keyboard users, the motion-triggering vestibular disorders) with the standards-and-law context (the WCAG success criteria applicable to non-text content and complex graphics, the EN-301-549 and ADA-EAA regulatory drivers) establishing accessibility as professional obligation not enhancement; the SVG-semantics foundation — the document accessibility: the role-and-title-description architecture (the role="img" with title-and-desc elements and aria-labelledby-describedby wiring — the basic-chart announcement pattern, the role="graphics-document" and WAI-ARIA Graphics Module roles — the graphics-symbol and graphics-object semantics for structured exploration), the focusability mechanics (the tabindex enabling keyboard traversal of mark groups, the focus-indicator visibility requirements), and the reading-order engineering (the DOM-order-as-reading-order discipline — the logical mark sequencing for sequential announcement) as the semantic foundation; the text-alternative strategy — the description craft: the alt-text-equivalent decisions (the concise-summary versus detailed-description spectrum by chart complexity and context, the W3C diagram-summary guidance — the description answering what-the-data-shows not what-the-chart-looks-like), the longdesc-and-linked-description patterns (the extended textual analysis accompanying complex graphics, the details-summary and modal alternatives), and the auto-generated-description cautions (the template-summary limitations — the data-driven 'the chart shows X' text versus analyst insight, the human-authored-summary requirement for meaning) as the description discipline; the data-table-and-structure fallback — the equivalent-access layer: the tabular-data companion patterns (the visually-hidden or togglable data tables mirroring chart content — the sortable-filterable table advantages for exploration, the table-caption-and-header semantics), the data-download provisions (the CSV-and-API access as the ultimate fallback), and the synchronization discipline (the table-chart data consistency through shared-source rendering — the single-data-source component architecture) as the equivalent-access architecture; the color-and-encoding accessibility — the perceptual inclusion: the contrast-requirement application (the WCAG contrast ratios for text, graphical objects, and UI components — the 3:1 non-text contrast criterion for chart elements), the CVD-inclusive palettes (the deuteranopia-protanopia-safe schemes — the Okabe-Ito and CVD-optimized palettes, the simulation-verification workflow from the perceptual disciplines), the redundant-encoding mandate (the direct-labeling over legend-dependence, the pattern-and-shape companions to color, the value-annotation availability) and the dark-mode-and-print parity as the encoding inclusion; the keyboard-and-focus interaction — the operable layer: the keyboard-navigable chart patterns (the roving-tabindex for mark-group traversal, the arrow-key mark-stepping with screen-reader announcements of values — the live-region integration, the keyboard-activation of interactive elements — the enter-space handling), the interaction-parity audit (every pointer interaction mapped to a keyboard equivalent — the hover-tooltip to focus-tooltip parity, the brush-and-zoom keyboard alternatives or documented-limitation honesty), and the focus-management in dynamic charts (the focus-preservation across data updates, the focus-restoration after modal and drill interactions) as the operable craft; the motion-and-vestibular inclusion — the temporal layer: the prefers-reduced-motion integration (the D3-transition gating from section 7, the CSS-animation suppression, the instant-state-change alternatives preserving information parity), the auto-playing-animation controls (the pause-stop provisions for continuous animations, the carousel-and-streaming controls), and the flash-and-seizure safety (the WCAG flash-threshold compliance for animated and streaming graphics) as the motion inclusion; the assistive-technology-testing practice — the verification layer: the screen-reader testing workflow (the NVDA-JAWS-VoiceOver chart-navigation sessions — the announcement-quality assessment, the reading-order and comprehension verification), the automated-scanning integration (the axe-core and Lighthouse SVG-a11y rules in CI — the role-title-contrast automation with the manual-testing-complement discipline), and the APU-user-testing inclusion (the disabled-user testing participation — the lived-experience validation beyond compliance checklists) as the evidence practice; the component-library accessibility — the scalable inclusion: the accessible-chart-component architecture (the accessibility props and slots — the title, description, and fallback-content APIs, the built-in keyboard and ARIA behaviors encapsulated per chart type), the documentation-as-accessibility (the component a11y-documentation — the supported interactions, keyboard maps, and screen-reader behaviors), and the design-system integration (the accessible-palette and typography tokens, the contrast-verified theme systems) as the institutionalized inclusion; the complex-visualization frontiers — the hard cases: the large-scatter-and-map accessibility (the thousands-of-marks announcement impossibility — the summary-statistics, region-navigation, and sonification alternatives at awareness), the dynamic-and-streaming display access (the live-update announcement strategies — the polite-live-region throttling, the snapshot-on-demand patterns), and the interactive-exploration parity (the filter-drush-brush analytical actions without pointer — the form-based control alternatives) as the frontier patterns with the honest-limitation documentation discipline; the inclusive-design mindset — the professional stance: the curb-cut-effect recognition (the accessible-chart improvements — the direct labels, the data tables, the keyboard navigation — benefiting all users, the accessibility-as-quality frame), the progressive-inclusion roadmap (the semantic-foundation first, the interaction-parity second, the frontier-experiments third — the maturity staging), and the accessibility-review integration (the a11y checks in the design-review and PR processes from the component-architecture discipline) as the practice culture; and the accessibility deliverable — the semantic, equivalent, operable, and tested inclusive visualization capability.
