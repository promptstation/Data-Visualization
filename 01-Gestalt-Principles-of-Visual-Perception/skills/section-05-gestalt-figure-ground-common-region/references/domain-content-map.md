## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Engineer figure-ground stratification with documented determinant knowledge (area, convexity, symmetry, enclosure, motion) making data figure and context boring ground.
2. Manage multi-figure layered graphics through luminance stepping, edge-quality conventions, and interactive layer promotion, deploy common-region containment with the weight-graded vocabulary (tint, hairline, border, shadow) under spacing-first and container-cost discipline, resolve cue-stack conflicts using documented precedence ordering (region over proximity over similarity) including table-banding and border decisions.
3. Diagnose figure-ground failures (bistability traps, ground competition, reversed emphasis, region leaks) through squint and extraction audits.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the depth-and-containment principles: figure-ground organization — the stratification principle: every visual field splitting into figure (object-like, shaped, in-front, attended) and ground (background-like, unshaped, behind, ignored) with the documented determinants (smaller area, convexity, symmetry, enclosure, lower position, and motion favoring figure status; the classic Rubin-vase bistability demonstrating assignment as construction not reading), making figure-ground the layering engine of all composition; the visualization application — the mark-versus-context split: data marks as figure against chart ground (the background, grid, and frame as the receding layer — the data-ink ratio argument restated as figure-ground engineering), the annotation hierarchy (callouts as secondary figures over data figures — the z-order craft), and the ground-quality requirements (grounds must be boring — texture, contrast, or structure in backgrounds stealing figure status, the wallpaper-versus-competition discipline); the layered-graphics reality — the multi-figure problem: complex displays requiring several simultaneous figure layers (data, reference lines, annotations, highlights, controls) with the layer-separation toolkit (the documented techniques: luminance and saturation stepping between layers, the blur-and-dim depth cues, edge-quality conventions — crisp figures over soft grounds, and the interactive layer promotion — hover raising elements to figure status) as the z-axis-of-attention craft; common region — the containment law: elements inside the same bounded area grouping regardless of proximity or similarity (the Palmer-Rock demonstrations establishing common region as a dominant modern addition — the documented finding that shared regions override proximity and even resist similarity conflicts), making containers the strongest explicit grouping device; region-engineering practice — the containment craft: the card-and-panel systems in dashboards (the container hierarchy — nested regions signaling nested membership), the background-tinting technique (the softest region signal — subtle fills grouping without borders, the weight-graded containment vocabulary from tint to hairline to border to shadow), and the region-cost discipline (every container adding non-data ink and fragmentation risk — the container-crutch pitfall with the spacing-first hierarchy: gaps before tints before lines before boxes); the frame-and-margin system — the canvas structure: chart frames, panel borders, and page margins as ground-defining regions (the framed-chart versus frameless-modern styles and their perceptual consequences — frames aiding ground-separation at data-ink cost), the padding grammar (region interior spacing signaling content relationship to container), and the bleed-and-edge behaviors (elements crossing region boundaries as deliberate figure-ground violation for emphasis); the interaction with proximity-similarity — the cue-stack analysis: the documented precedence ordering (common region over proximity over similarity in the classic conflicts) with the design exploitation (using the strongest cue for the most important grouping, the hierarchy-of-cues planning) and the conflict-audit practice (mapping which cue carries each intended group); the table-and-matrix application — the dense-structure case: cell grouping in tables and heatmaps (the zebra-striping debate read as common-region versus similarity conflict — the documented banding findings, the border-versus-spacing research confirming spacing superiority at low density and borders' return at high density) as the applied cue-selection; figure-ground failures — the diagnostic layer: the bistability traps (ambiguous layers flickering between readings — the annotation-that-becomes-data problem), ground-competition (busy backgrounds fragmenting figure coherence), the reversed-figure error (emphasis styling making the context louder than the data — the highlight-dimming inversion), and the region-leak (grouping crossing container boundaries through strong similarity — the cross-card color-echo problem); the validation practice — the layer audit: the squint-test reading (which layer holds figure status under blur), the element-extraction test (do intended groups survive isolation), and the naive-viewer layer-report protocol as the figure-ground verification routine; and the figure-ground deliverable — the layered, contained, depth-structured canvas engineering.
