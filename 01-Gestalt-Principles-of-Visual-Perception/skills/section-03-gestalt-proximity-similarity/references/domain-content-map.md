Engineer proximity structure through spacing ratios and white-space grammar (intra-group versus inter-group distances, table spacing-over-rules findings) with associative-field falloff intuition, deploy similarity grouping across distance using the feature-strength hierarchy (hue over shape over size) with cross-panel consistency discipline, resolve cue competitions by reinforcing rather than contradicting with density-compensation toolkits (common regions, tinting, interactive reveal).
2. Build multi-scale grouping hierarchies with consistent spacing ratios.
3. Run squint, grayscale, and first-glance audits preventing equidistant-grid, false-proximity, and similarity-smearing failures.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the two strongest classical laws: proximity — the distance law: elements close together binding into groups (the documented strength ranking as the most powerful grouping cue in most contexts), the spacing-grammar of layout (gaps as punctuation — intra-group spacing smaller than inter-group spacing as the structural requirement, the Gestalt version of typographic leading and paragraph spacing), and the quantitative intuition (grouping probability as a smooth function of relative distance — the associative-field falloff, not a threshold) making spacing ratios the design variable; proximity applications in graphics — the working cases: axis-label and tick association, legend-item binding (swatch-to-label distance), bar-pairing in grouped charts, table row-column structure (the classic spacing-versus-rules finding — spacing outperforming gridlines for cell grouping, the Tufte data-ink argument's perceptual basis), scatter-plot cluster reading, and white-space as the grouping instrument (the active-void craft); similarity — the attribute law: elements sharing visual properties (color, size, shape, orientation, texture) binding across distance (similarity grouping surviving spatial separation where proximity fails — the long-range color-grouping demonstrations), with the feature-specificity finding (color-hue similarity grouping strongest, then shape and size gradients) connecting to the preattentive channel hierarchy (course 2 integration); similarity applications in graphics — the working cases: series identity across a multi-line chart (the color-as-series-binding convention), categorical encoding consistency (same category same color everywhere — the cross-panel binding requirement), highlight-dimming structures (the desaturated-background plus saturated-focus pattern as deliberate similarity partitioning), and iconography families in dashboards; the competition and cooperation analysis — the interaction craft: proximity-versus-similarity conflicts (the documented crossover displays where color groups override spatial groups and vice versa — the strength-by-context findings), the cooperation patterns (aligned cues producing fast unambiguous parses), and the design rule (never spend two strong cues contradicting each other; spend them reinforcing); the density trade-off — the dashboard reality: proximity grouping requiring whitespace that dense displays cannot afford (the space-versus-structure tension), with the compensation toolkit (common regions replacing gaps — section 5, subtle background tinting, hairline separators used sparingly against the data-ink cost, and the interactive-reveal alternative — hover expansion deferring structure to interaction); the scale-nesting property — the hierarchy craft: grouping at multiple spatial scales (points into bars, bars into clusters, clusters into panels, panels into pages) with the consistent-ratio discipline (each grouping level using a distinguishable spacing ratio — the typographic-scale analogy for spatial structure) as the multi-level-organization skill; the quantitative-evidence layer — the measurement status: documented experimental findings on grouping strength (the relative-distance effects, the similarity-channel rankings, the cue-conflict resolutions) with the effect-modulator awareness (element size, count, and regularity shifting strengths) as the calibration knowledge; failure patterns — the anti-craft: the equidistant-grid trap (uniform spacing producing no groups — the monotone-layout failure), the false-proximity error (label drift binding to the wrong mark — the orphaned-tick and floating-legend failures), the similarity-smearing error (too many categories sharing near-identical colors producing phantom groups), and the container-crutch (boxes substituting for spacing discipline); the audit practice — the perceptual check: the squint-test and blur-test for proximity structure (does grouping survive defocus — the low-spatial-frequency check), the grayscale-test for similarity structure (does color grouping survive luminance flattening — the CVD-anticipation check, the course-9 integration), and the first-glance protocol (naive-viewer reports of what grouped) as the verification routine; and the proximity-similarity deliverable — the spacing-and-attribute grammar engineering first-parse structure.
