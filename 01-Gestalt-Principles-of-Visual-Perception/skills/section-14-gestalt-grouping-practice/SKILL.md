# Grouping in Practice: The Audit, Critique, and Design-System Workflow — Professional Training

## Mission

Produce professional-grade learning modules and training materials on **grouping in Practice: The Audit, Critique, and Design-System Workflow** within Perceptual psychology applied to data visualization — how the brain automatically groups shapes, identifies patterns, and recognizes anomalies, and how designers engineer those automatic processes deliberately.

Subject scope: Integrates the discipline into professional workflow: the perceptual-audit method — the systematic review: the grouping-audit protocol (the composition walk-through: intended groups enumerated, cue-mapping per group — which law carries it, conflict-detection across cues, accidental-pattern scan, first-parse verification) as the structured design-review instrument derived from all preceding sections; the critique-vocabulary — the professional language: the precise grouping-feedback lexicon (the law-names as shared terms — 'the legend relies on similarity but the proximity to the wrong panel dominates', 'common region here beats your intended proximity grouping', 'this crossing breaks continuation') replacing taste-language (the craft-course 16 critique discipline specialized to perception), making grouping decisions discussable and teachable; the design-workflow integration — the process embedding: the grouping-first layout phase (the structural sketch before styling — the wireframe-as-grouping-plan practice), the cue-allocation planning (the hierarchy-of-importance to hierarchy-of-cue-strength mapping from section 6 — the strongest cues to the most important groups), the styling-phase grouping-check (every color, weight, and container decision reviewed against the grouping plan), and the pre-publication audit (the section-13 validation tier matched to stakes) as the four-touchpoint process; the design-system encoding — the institutionalization layer: the grouping-grammar in design tokens (the spacing-scale as proximity system — the documented ratio-based spacing scales encoding grouping hierarchy, the color-system categorical-sequential-family structures as similarity architecture, the elevation-and-border systems as common-region vocabulary) making correct grouping the default construction (the template-baked-in discipline); the component-library practice — the reusable grouping: the chart-component standards (the axis, legend, annotation components with grouping rules pre-solved), the layout-component families (the card, panel, and zone components with containment hierarchy built in), and the component-audit cadence (the periodic review against new evidence and failure-library entries) as the scalable craft; the failure-library operation — the learning system: the documented-bug catalog (the grouping failures with root-cause tags and fix patterns — the section-12 library operationalized), the post-incident reviews (the deployed-dashboard grouping problems traced to process gaps), and the library-to-checklist pipeline (recurring failures promoted into the audit protocol) as the compounding quality system; the team-education practice — the capability spread: the grouping-literacy training for adjacent roles (the developers implementing layouts, the analysts building ad-hoc charts, the stakeholders reviewing designs — the shared-vocabulary investment), the demonstration-set teaching (the conflict-display library from section 6 as training material), and the review-participation model (junior designers running audits against the protocol — the learning-through-checking pattern); the cross-discipline interfaces — the collaboration map: the grouping handoffs (to the preattentive-attribute work — course 2's salience layer, to the cognitive-load management — course 3's complexity budget, to the accessibility verification — course 9's redundant-encoding requirements, to the IA and hierarchy — course 7's navigation structures) as the curriculum-integration points where grouping decisions meet adjacent disciplines; the tooling workflow — the implementation bridge: the grouping-aware tooling practice (the design-tool grid and spacing systems, the code-level spacing tokens and layout primitives — the CSS-grid and flexbox grouping grammar, the visualization-library defaults audit — which grouping decisions libraries bake in and which they leave broken) as the design-to-production continuity; the portfolio-artifact standard — the demonstration piece: the grouping-case-study format (the before-after audit trail: the original composition, the cue-map analysis, the failure diagnosis, the redesign, the validation evidence) as the professional-portfolio demonstration (the craft-course 16 portfolio practice specialized) and the client-communication asset; the practice-maturity model — the development arc: stage one (law recognition — seeing grouping in others' work), stage two (deliberate application — planning cues in own work), stage three (audit fluency — systematic review with vocabulary), stage four (system design — encoding grouping grammar into design systems and teams) as the capability roadmap; and the practice deliverable — the audit-protocol, critique-vocabulary, design-system, and team-education workflow institutionalizing perceptual engineering.

Write as an experienced practitioner, not as a summarizer of popular content. Every framework taught must be something a real team or professional could run: procedures they can execute, criteria they can judge with, and artifacts they can hand to a colleague. Do not present claims as settled when the field treats them as contested — the training must model evidence discipline.

The module deepens this section's capabilities for a learner progressing from competent beginner toward expert practitioner, and connects them to the surrounding discipline rather than teaching them in isolation.

## Core Output Requirements

- Deliverables are Markdown documents: the module, exercise sets with model solutions, and checklists. No placeholders, no "TODO" sections.
- Ground content in authoritative sources:
- Wertheimer, Koffka, and Kohler's classical Gestalt psychology (laws of perceptual organization, Pragnanz) as the founding tradition
- Palmer, Rock, and Ehrenstein's modern re-analysis of gestalt principles (the new gestalt grouping factors: convexity, symmetry, parallelism, common-region research)
- Ware, Information Visualization: Perception for Design — the applied-perception canon bridging vision science and interface design
- Treisman's feature-integration theory and the visual-search literature (pop-out, conjunction search, anomaly detection)
- Cleveland and McGill's graphical-perception experiments (the elementary-task accuracy hierarchy) as the quantitative complement to grouping theory
- Tufte's information-design doctrine (data-ink, chartjunk, small multiples) read through perceptual-grouping lenses
- Nielsen Norman Group and applied-UX research on proximity, similarity, and common-region effects in interface layouts
- Maintain an evidence ledger while writing: every factual claim or number is either sourced, flagged as disputed/popular account, or omitted. Never invent statistics, studies, or citations.
- Distinguish established research findings from professional conventions and informed recommendations, and say which is which.
- Explain each specialized term in clear language on first use.

## Writing Standards

Throughout the material, prioritize language that is:

* Precise without becoming jargon-heavy
* Practical without discarding rigor
* Honest about limitations and contested findings without being defeatist
* Concrete — anchored in real cases, real artifacts, and verifiable numbers
* Progressive from fundamentals to expert judgment

Where a recommendation depends on context, explain the trade-off rather than presenting an absolute rule.

## Anti-Patterns

Avoid: Teaching the laws as a memorized list (proximity, similarity, closure) without the underlying perceptual-organization problem they solve, producing decoration-by-checklist instead of engineered perception; Ignoring accidental grouping — unintended alignments, adjacency, and shared styling creating false patterns and phantom relationships viewers cannot unsee; Over-grouping dense dashboards into cards and boxes until the containers carry more ink and attention than the data; Confusing grouping salience with importance — strong gestalt structure on the wrong element directing attention away from the decision-relevant signal; Assuming universality — grouping strengths vary with expertise, culture, and task; what pops for the designer who knows the answer may not pop for a first-time viewer; exercises without evaluation criteria; modules that stop at inspiration without a single decision the learner can now make better; and any content that overstates certainty beyond what the evidence supports.

## Decision Heuristic

When allocating depth under time or length limits, ask:

1. Where will this audience actually stall in practice?
2. Which unit protects the most value if taught well?
3. Can the learner run the framework tomorrow without me?
4. Is every example doing work a plain sentence could not?
5. What would a skeptical domain expert say about this material?

If two topics compete for space, keep the one that changes a decision.

## Final Principle

The goal is not more content about Gestalt perception. The goal is that a practitioner could take this material and perform: Run systematic grouping audits (intended-group enumeration, cue mapping, conflict detection, first-parse verification) with precise critique vocabulary replacing taste language, operate failure libraries with checklist-promotion pipelines and team-education practices, and climb the four-stage practice maturity from law recognition to system design with portfolio case-study documentation — with the same evidence discipline the field's best practitioners use. The module must function simultaneously as a learning resource, a practical reference, and a working methodology.
