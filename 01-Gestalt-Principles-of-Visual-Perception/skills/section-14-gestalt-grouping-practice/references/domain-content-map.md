# Domain Content Map — Grouping in Practice: The Audit, Critique, and Design-System Workflow

Read this during Phase 3 (architecture) and Phase 4 (writing) of the module
development workflow. Verify all factual claims and numbers against the
authoritative sources before publishing (Phase 2 evidence base); flag

## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Run systematic grouping audits (intended-group enumeration, cue mapping, conflict detection, first-parse verification) with precise critique vocabulary replacing taste language.
2. Integrate grouping into design workflow (structure-first sketching, cue-allocation planning, styling checks, staged validation), encode grouping grammar into design systems (spacing scales, color architectures, elevation vocabularies) and component libraries.
3. Operate failure libraries with checklist-promotion pipelines and team-education practices, and climb the four-stage practice maturity from law recognition to system design with portfolio case-study documentation.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Integrates the discipline into professional workflow: the perceptual-audit method — the systematic review: the grouping-audit protocol (the composition walk-through: intended groups enumerated, cue-mapping per group — which law carries it, conflict-detection across cues, accidental-pattern scan, first-parse verification) as the structured design-review instrument derived from all preceding sections; the critique-vocabulary — the professional language: the precise grouping-feedback lexicon (the law-names as shared terms — 'the legend relies on similarity but the proximity to the wrong panel dominates', 'common region here beats your intended proximity grouping', 'this crossing breaks continuation') replacing taste-language (the craft-course 16 critique discipline specialized to perception), making grouping decisions discussable and teachable; the design-workflow integration — the process embedding: the grouping-first layout phase (the structural sketch before styling — the wireframe-as-grouping-plan practice), the cue-allocation planning (the hierarchy-of-importance to hierarchy-of-cue-strength mapping from section 6 — the strongest cues to the most important groups), the styling-phase grouping-check (every color, weight, and container decision reviewed against the grouping plan), and the pre-publication audit (the section-13 validation tier matched to stakes) as the four-touchpoint process; the design-system encoding — the institutionalization layer: the grouping-grammar in design tokens (the spacing-scale as proximity system — the documented ratio-based spacing scales encoding grouping hierarchy, the color-system categorical-sequential-family structures as similarity architecture, the elevation-and-border systems as common-region vocabulary) making correct grouping the default construction (the template-baked-in discipline); the component-library practice — the reusable grouping: the chart-component standards (the axis, legend, annotation components with grouping rules pre-solved), the layout-component families (the card, panel, and zone components with containment hierarchy built in), and the component-audit cadence (the periodic review against new evidence and failure-library entries) as the scalable craft; the failure-library operation — the learning system: the documented-bug catalog (the grouping failures with root-cause tags and fix patterns — the section-12 library operationalized), the post-incident reviews (the deployed-dashboard grouping problems traced to process gaps), and the library-to-checklist pipeline (recurring failures promoted into the audit protocol) as the compounding quality system; the team-education practice — the capability spread: the grouping-literacy training for adjacent roles (the developers implementing layouts, the analysts building ad-hoc charts, the stakeholders reviewing designs — the shared-vocabulary investment), the demonstration-set teaching (the conflict-display library from section 6 as training material), and the review-participation model (junior designers running audits against the protocol — the learning-through-checking pattern); the cross-discipline interfaces — the collaboration map: the grouping handoffs (to the preattentive-attribute work — course 2's salience layer, to the cognitive-load management — course 3's complexity budget, to the accessibility verification — course 9's redundant-encoding requirements, to the IA and hierarchy — course 7's navigation structures) as the curriculum-integration points where grouping decisions meet adjacent disciplines; the tooling workflow — the implementation bridge: the grouping-aware tooling practice (the design-tool grid and spacing systems, the code-level spacing tokens and layout primitives — the CSS-grid and flexbox grouping grammar, the visualization-library defaults audit — which grouping decisions libraries bake in and which they leave broken) as the design-to-production continuity; the portfolio-artifact standard — the demonstration piece: the grouping-case-study format (the before-after audit trail: the original composition, the cue-map analysis, the failure diagnosis, the redesign, the validation evidence) as the professional-portfolio demonstration (the craft-course 16 portfolio practice specialized) and the client-communication asset; the practice-maturity model — the development arc: stage one (law recognition — seeing grouping in others' work), stage two (deliberate application — planning cues in own work), stage three (audit fluency — systematic review with vocabulary), stage four (system design — encoding grouping grammar into design systems and teams) as the capability roadmap; and the practice deliverable — the audit-protocol, critique-vocabulary, design-system, and team-education workflow institutionalizing perceptual engineering.
