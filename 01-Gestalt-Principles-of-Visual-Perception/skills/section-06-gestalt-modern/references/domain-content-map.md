## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the post-classical expansion: the revision history — the canon's modernization: the classical law list (proximity, similarity, continuation, closure, figure-ground, Pragnanz) proven incomplete and imprecise by the late-century experimental re-analysis (the Palmer-Rock-Ehrenstein program systematically testing candidate factors, isolating new grouping principles, and quantifying relative strengths) establishing the modern multi-factor view with measured hierarchies; symmetry (the mirror law) — the form principle: symmetric configurations binding into single figures (the documented strength as a powerful grouping factor — symmetric-pair displays overriding proximity in conflicts), the symmetry-detection speed findings (the near-preattentive character of mirror symmetry — the course-2 boundary case), and the applications (paired-comparison layouts, butterfly and tornado charts reading as unity, the symmetric-dashboard balance as organizational signal) with the rarity caveat (real data rarely symmetric — the design-imposed symmetry as grouping tool); parallelism — the orientation-family principle: parallel elements and contours grouping (the documented finding of parallelism as a distinct strong factor — parallel lines binding over mere proximity), with the applications (aligned bar families, the parallel-coordinates readability basis, consistent slant and angle families signaling relatedness, the hairline-grid parallel structure) and the violation-use (the single non-parallel element as the anomaly signal — the emphasis exploitation); convexity — the good-shape principle: convex configurations preferred as figure organizations (the documented convexity-over-concavity findings in ambiguous-contour displays) informing shape-design choices (convex hulls for cluster enclosure, the convex-panel preference, corner-radius psychology) as the modern Pragnanz refinement; common-fate's modern treatment — the motion factor: elements moving together grouping (the classical law's experimental revalidation, the motion-grouping dominance findings — common fate overriding static cues when motion present) previewed for section 7's dynamic depth; the measured-strength hierarchy — the quantitative canon: the documented approximate ordering from the experimental literature (connectedness and common region strongest, then proximity, common fate and continuity, similarity, symmetry, parallelism — with context modulation) as the cue-selection decision table replacing the flat classical list; connectedness — the explicit-link factor: elements joined by lines grouping most strongly (the documented finding that connected elements override all other cues — the line-versus-proximity demonstrations), explaining why line charts bind series absolutely and why adding connector lines transforms scatter perception (the dotted-line-trend-effect — the connection-implies-relationship warning) making connectivity the most deliberate design decision; uniform-connectedness applications — the network and flow craft: the connected-element logic in node-link diagrams, sankey and flow maps, dendrogram and tree reading, and the graph-drawing quality standards (edge-crossing minimization, bend reduction as continuation-respecting layout) as the connectedness-native visualizations; the factor-interaction research — the combination science: the documented pairwise-conflict experiments (which factor wins when two disagree — the precedence matrix) with the three-plus-cue complexity acknowledgment (interaction non-additivity — the combinatorial caution) informing the conflict-resolution playbook; the revision's design lesson — the meta-principle: the classical laws as useful vocabulary but the modern factors-plus-strengths as engineering knowledge (the difference between naming proximity and knowing connectedness beats it, region beats proximity, and motion beats both when present) as the practitioner upgrade; the demonstration-practice — the learning instrument: building and testing the conflict displays (the classic ambiguous figures where factors compete) as the calibration exercise developing cue-strength intuition through direct perception; and the modern-canon deliverable — the factor-hierarchy-fluent grouping engineering with conflict-resolution playbooks.
