## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the continuity-and-completion family: good continuation (the continuity law) — the alignment principle: elements arranged on a smooth path or line binding into a single continuing structure (the documented preference for collinear and co-circular groupings, the cusp-versus-smooth-intersection demonstrations), with the visualization applications (line-chart series crossing legibility — continued lines winning over switched ones at intersections, the direct-labeling placement preserving path continuity, aligned small-multiples reading as one system, the scattered-points-on-a-curve binding into pattern); occlusion and amodal completion — the behind-the-object effect: continued contours completing behind occluders (the classic overlapping-shape demonstrations) as the mechanism making layered graphics readable (annotations over data, overlapping scatter points, legend intrusions) with the design obligations (occluder clarity — clean edges signaling overlap rather than merging, the contrast and outline conventions preserving figure status); closure — the gap-completion principle: incomplete contours and shapes perceived as whole (the fragmented-circle and dotted-polygon demonstrations) with the applications (implied boundaries replacing drawn borders — the data-ink economy of closure: letting aligned element edges imply containers, dashed and dotted enclosures completing mentally, the partial-grid table designs where closure supplies missing rules) and the load-limits (closure weakening with gap size and fragment count — the completion-failure conditions); Pragnanz (the good-figure law) — the master principle: organizations tending toward the simplest, most regular, symmetric, stable interpretation available (the historical umbrella law), with the modern reading as the economy principle of perceptual inference (the minimum-complexity parse winning) explaining why ambiguous displays resolve predictably and why simple structures parse instantly; the simplicity trade-off — the design tension: Pragnanz favoring simple parses that may be wrong parses (the regularity-bias errors — viewers completing patterns the data does not have: implied trend-lines through noisy scatter, phantom gridlines from aligned points, the completion-confabulation risk) requiring deliberate disruption of false simplicity (jitter, honest irregularity, the anti-closure techniques for noisy data); the continuation-versus-proximity interaction — the competition case: the documented crossing displays where alignment and distance disagree (the X-pattern readings) with the resolution patterns (good continuation typically dominating at intersections) informing line-crossing and label-placement decisions; smoothness engineering — the craft layer: the curvature-continuity practices (the bezier smoothness conventions in line rendering, the corner-radius consistency signaling family membership, the alignment-laser discipline — sub-pixel misalignments breaking continuation), with the tooling reality (vector rendering, grid systems, optical-alignment correction); the pattern-completion connection — the anomaly link: Pragnanz-driven completion establishing the expected regularity whose violation triggers anomaly detection (the section-8 preview: the surprising-element effect depending on the strength of the completed pattern) as the pattern-and-anomaly mechanics; the evidence layer — the experimental status: the classical demonstrations' modern quantifications (the contour-integration psychophysics — the association-field findings, the closure-threshold measurements, the simplicity-preference rankings) with the computational-model connections (the field-theory mathematics — the course-10 geometry preview) as the depth literacy; failure patterns — the anti-craft: the broken-continuity error (misaligned series segments reading as separate objects, the disconnected-legend syndrome), the over-closure error (gaps so large viewers complete wrongly — the misleading-implied-boundary), and the false-Pragnanz error (regularized smoothing hiding real irregularity — the beautification-of-data trap); and the completion deliverable — the continuity-engineered, closure-economical, simplicity-aware structural design.
