# Domain Content Map — Color, Form, and Texture as Grouping Carriers

## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Architect grouping carrier systems across color (hue families, lightness-chroma nesting, consistency contracts), shape-size-orientation forms, texture fills, and typography with channel-strength and discriminability-limit knowledge, enforce redundancy principles under CVD constraints with simulation-verification workflows.
2. Combine carriers through redundant and orthogonal grammars within channel-capacity budgets, calibrate carriers against context interactions (background contrast, density, medium).
3. Run the four-carrier audit (grayscale, CVD, squint, legend-independence) preventing phantom groups, carrier starvation, and accessibility collapse.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the attribute layer of grouping: the carrier-channel concept — similarity needs properties: similarity grouping operating through shared visual attributes, making the attribute systems (color, shape, size, orientation, texture, typography) the carriers of grouping structure with channel-specific strengths and costs (the feature-hierarchy from section 3 refined into a working palette); the color-grouping system — the strongest carrier: the hue-family binding (categorical palettes as similarity-group systems — the documented discriminability limits capping categorical counts at the working-memory and channel-resolution boundaries, the palette-design discipline: equal-perceptual-weight hues preventing unintended figure dominance), the lightness-chroma dimensions (the tint-and-shade families grouping as related variants — the sequential-palette logic as nested grouping, the saturation-dimming highlight systems as the figure-ground partition), and the color-consistency contract (the cross-view binding requirement — one meaning one color everywhere, the violation cost as false-grouping creation); the colorblindness constraint — the carrier-reliability limit: the CVD prevalence reality (the significant population fraction with color-vision deficiency — the course-9 depth previewed) making red-green hue grouping unreliable for a substantial audience, the redundant-encoding principle (grouping carried by two channels — hue plus position, hue plus shape — the belt-and-suspenders standard), and the CVD-simulation verification workflow (the deuteranopia-protanopia checks as the grouping-integrity audit); the shape-and-form grouping — the geometric carriers: the shape-family similarity (the icon-shape sets binding categories, the marker-shape conventions in scatter overlays), the size-graded families (the proportional-symbol hierarchies grouping by magnitude class), the orientation families (the slant-consistency binding from section 6 parallelism), and the form-complexity budget (the discriminability floor — shapes must differ enough to group differently, the simple-form advantage); the texture-and-pattern carriers — the fill systems: the area-fill textures as grouping signals (the pattern-fill conventions in stacked and choropleth contexts, the hatching families for related regions), the texture-gradient depth cues (the density gradients suggesting surface continuity), and the fill-versus-outline weight (the filled-figure dominance over outlined — the figure-strength hierarchy) with the print-and-monochrome robustness advantage (texture surviving grayscale where hue fails — the redundancy synergy); the typographic grouping — the text-carrier system: the font-family and weight as similarity channels (the type-scale hierarchy binding heading levels, the weight-and-style families grouping related labels, the monospace-proportional contrast for data-versus-chrome text), the case-and-spacing conventions (the small-caps and letter-spacing treatments as group markers), and the text-figure-ground (the label legibility over data backgrounds — the halo and knockout techniques as ground-suppression for text figures); the carrier-combination grammar — the multi-channel encoding: the redundant-carrier patterns (two channels carrying one grouping — the robustness standard), the orthogonal-carrier patterns (independent channels carrying independent groupings — the crossed-dimension encoding with the conjunction-search cost from section 8 acknowledged), and the channel-capacity discipline (the total carrier budget — every additional grouping dimension consuming discriminability resources, the Miller-limits connection to course 3); the carrier-context interactions — the environment effects: the background-interaction findings (carrier strengths shifting with ground color and texture — the simultaneous-contrast effects on hue grouping, the size-contrast context effects), the density interactions (carrier discriminability degrading with mark density — the overplotting interference from section 9), and the medium differences (screen-versus-print carrier behavior — the luminous-color versus reflective-ink distinctions, the gamut and resolution constraints) as the context-calibration knowledge; the carrier-failure patterns — the diagnostic layer: the phantom-group creation (accidental color echoes binding unrelated elements — the region-leak from section 5 at carrier level), the carrier-starvation (too many categories in too-weak channels — the rainbow-palette mush), the emphasis-collision (highlight carriers competing with grouping carriers — the two-jobs-one-channel conflict), and the accessibility-collapse (grouping carried solely by a channel the viewer cannot perceive — the single-carrier fragility); the carrier-system deliverable — the palette, form, texture, and type architectures specified as grouping systems with redundancy and context calibration; and the carrier audit — the verification routine: the grayscale-test (lightness-only grouping integrity), the CVD-simulation (hue-grouping survival), the squint-test (carrier-salience hierarchy), and the legend-independence check (do groups read without the key) as the four-carrier-checks practice.
