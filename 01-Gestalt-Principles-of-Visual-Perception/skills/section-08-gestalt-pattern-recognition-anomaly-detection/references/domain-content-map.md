## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Analyze chart pattern recognition through the preattentive-to-deliberate comprehension continuum with documented slope and aspect-ratio bias awareness.
2. Apply feature-integration theory (parallel pop-out versus serial conjunction search with set-size cost slopes) to convert search tasks into pop-out tasks, engineer both anomaly types (feature-singleton and pattern-violation) through pattern-strength-first salience design.
3. Manage complex scenes (search asymmetries, cluster contexts, small-multiple strategies) with novice-expert dual-audience calibration, and prevent false anomalies and highlight fatigue through signal-density budgets verified by timed search experiments and scanpath analysis.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the source's pattern-and-anomaly thesis in depth: pattern recognition in graphics — the binding-to-meaning arc: grouping outputs (clusters, lines, regions) feeding pattern interpretation (trend, cycle, outlier, correlation readings) with the documented chart-reading research (the graph-comprehension literature — the preattentive-to-deliberate continuum of pattern extraction, the slope-and-trend perception findings including the systematic aspect-ratio bias in slope judgment) as the pattern-perception evidence; visual-search theory — the detection machinery: Treisman's feature-integration theory (single features detected in parallel across the field — the pop-out phenomenon with search time flat versus display size; feature conjunctions requiring serial attention — the linear search-time cost) as the quantitative model of finding things in displays; the pop-out conditions — the salience-map science: which differences pop (the documented preattentive features — color hue, orientation, size, motion, with the singleton-detection findings) and the salience-map concept (the competition architecture where feature contrasts vote for attention priority) connecting to course 2's attribute mastery; anomaly detection as search — the outlier-finding mechanics: the two anomaly types (the feature-singleton anomaly — the red point among gray ones popping preattentively, versus the pattern-violation anomaly — the point breaking a trend or cluster requiring the completed-pattern expectation from sections 4-6 plus local deviation detection), with the design implication (engineer anomalies into whichever detection mode the decision requires); the expected-versus-actual frame — the anomaly psychology: anomalies as expectation violations (the schema-disconfirmation mechanism — the documented surprise-and-memory advantages of violated expectations), making anomaly salience a function of pattern strength first (strong regularity, strong violation signal — the Pragnanz-completion link) and feature contrast second; the search-effort economics — the attention-budget view: serial-search costs in dense displays (the item-count and distractor-similarity effects on search time — the documented slopes), the set-size discipline (every non-popping element the viewer must serially inspect is a cost), and the design target (converting serial-search tasks into parallel pop-out tasks through encoding choices) as the quantified attention engineering; multiple-anomaly and cluster scenes — the complex cases: the documented search asymmetries (finding absence-of-anomaly harder than presence — the clean-scan cost), the cluster-context effects (anomalies within groups versus between groups — the grouping-structure modulation of detection), and the small-multiples search strategy (the parallel-panel comparison as distributed search) as the dense-scene literacy; the expertise modulation — the trained-eye reality: chunking and schema effects in expert search (the documented radiologist and analyst gaze patterns — experts fixating informatively, novices scanning broadly), the training-trajectory implication (displays should support novice search patterns while not slowing experts — the dual-audience design) and the dashboard-literacy development; the false-anomaly problem — the noise-vulnerability: phantom anomalies from grouping artifacts (the section-12 material previewed: accidental clusters, moiré patterns, aliasing producing illusory violations), the over-highlighting fatigue (too many engineered pop-outs diluting each — the cry-wolf effect on salience), and the signal-density calibration (the anomaly-emphasis budget) as the reliability discipline; the measurement connection — the search-validated design: the visual-search experiment as design test (the timed detection tasks, the accuracy-under-distraction protocols, the eye-tracking scanpath analysis — the section-13 preview) with the pop-out verification standard (if the intended signal requires serial search, the design has failed its job or the task is genuinely deliberative) as the accountability link; and the search-science deliverable — the pattern-and-anomaly engineering with quantified detection-mode control.
