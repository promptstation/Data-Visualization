# Domain Content Map — Validating Perception: Eye-Tracking, Experiments, and Evidence-Based Grouping

Read this during Phase 3 (architecture) and Phase 4 (writing) of the module
development workflow. Verify all factual claims and numbers against the
authoritative sources before publishing (Phase 2 evidence base); flag
anything disputed rather than repeating it.

## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Validate grouping designs through eye-tracking (AOI analysis, metric selection by question type, inference limits), controlled display experiments (A/B variants, within-subjects counterbalancing, single-cue isolation), and task batteries (comprehension, timed performance, recall, workload).
2. Operate rapid-validation tiers (five-second tests, blur checks, hallway protocols) with naive-viewer recruitment overcoming designer blindness.
3. Integrate production telemetry as deployed evidence within privacy discipline, leverage published graphical-perception findings with rigor assessment and context-transfer caution, and tier evidence effort to decision stakes avoiding methodology pitfalls and measurement theater.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Builds the empirical validation capability: the evidence-culture principle — the discipline standard: grouping claims as empirical claims requiring testable evidence (the psychology lineage from section 1 — the demonstration-and-experiment tradition) making validation the difference between applied science and folklore, with the designer-as-experimenter identity; eye-tracking methods — the gaze instrumentation: the measurement primitives (fixations, saccades, scanpaths, the AOI interest-area analysis — the panel-and-element dwell metrics), the equipment spectrum (the remote trackers, the wearables, the webcam-estimation tools with their accuracy trade-offs), and the visualization-study applications (the documented dashboard-scan studies, the chart-reading gaze patterns, the expertise-difference findings from section 8) as the grouping-behavior observation system; the eye-tracking analysis craft — the interpretation layer: the metric-selection discipline (the first-fixation and time-to-first-fixation for salience questions, the dwell and revisit counts for engagement questions, the transition matrices for path questions), the statistical treatment (the fixation-data noise, the participant-variability handling, the sample-size realities of gaze studies), and the inference limits (gaze showing where attention went, not what was understood — the comprehension-measurement complement requirement); controlled grouping experiments — the causal method: the A/B display testing (variant compositions with task-performance measurement — the detection-time and accuracy protocols from section 8's search tasks), the within-subjects demonstration designs (the counterbalanced presentation controlling order effects), and the effect-isolation practice (changing one grouping cue at a time — the variable-control discipline the course-19 experiment science applied to perception); the task-based evaluation battery — the measurement toolkit: the comprehension-question protocol (the answer-accuracy on targeted questions — the first-parse report, the comparison tasks, the anomaly-detection tasks), the timed-performance measures (the search-time and comparison-time benchmarks), the recall-and-recognition tests (the memory-persistence of grouping structure), and the subjective-workload ratings (the perceived-effort scales connecting to course 3's load theory) as the multi-measure validation standard; the rapid-validation practices — the lightweight methods: the five-second and first-glance tests (the section-10 protocols as structured methods with the naive-participant recruitment practice), the squint-and-blur checks (the low-frequency-structure verification), the click-or-point protocols (where-does-the-eye-go-first participatory tests), and the hallway-usability discipline (the quick naive-viewer intercepts) as the low-cost evidence tier; the naive-viewer principle — the participant standard: the designer-blindness problem (the creator's knowledge contaminating perception — the curse-of-knowledge in grouping judgment) requiring naive participants (first-time viewers matching the real audience) with the recruitment pragmatism (colleagues-from-other-teams, the user-panel access, the minimum-viable participant counts for directional evidence); the analytics-integration layer — the in-situ evidence: the production-telemetry signals (the hover and click patterns on dashboard elements as revealed-attention data, the scroll-depth and panel-engagement metrics, the filter-usage sequences revealing actual navigation paths) with the privacy-compliant collection discipline (the course-9-adjacent consent considerations for behavioral telemetry) as the deployed-validation stream; the literature-leverage practice — the standing-on-evidence discipline: the published-findings utilization (the graphical-perception research base — the Cleveland-McGill hierarchy replications and extensions, the documented grouping-strength studies, the dashboard-research literature) as the prior-knowledge layer reducing required local testing, with the source-quality assessment (the experimental-rigor appraisal, the effect-size attention, the context-transfer caution from section 2's universality limits); the evidence-tiering strategy — the proportionality principle: matching validation effort to decision stakes (the literature-informed defaults for routine choices, the rapid-tests for novel layouts, the full experiments for high-stakes or contested designs — the evidence-budget allocation), with the documentation practice (the decision-evidence record — what was tested, what was found, what was decided) as the institutional memory; the validation-failure patterns — the methodology pitfalls: the designer-as-participant error (self-testing contamination), the single-viewer anecdote (n-of-1 evidence over-reading), the task-mismatch (testing comprehension when the design question is salience), and the measurement-theater (eye-tracking heatmaps as decoration without hypothesis — the pretty-picture trap) with the corrections; and the validation deliverable — the multi-method evidence system making grouping decisions empirically accountable.
