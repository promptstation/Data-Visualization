## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Introduces the discipline and its governing bottleneck: cognitive load theory defined — the Sweller-lineage instructional-design framework formalizing that learning and comprehension are constrained by working-memory capacity, with the design task restated as load management: allocating the viewer's finite processing capacity to the elements that carry meaning rather than the elements that carry noise; the source thesis operationalized — complex dashboards must inform without overwhelming or fatiguing, which translates to an engineering target: keep total load under capacity, minimize the wasted fraction, and sustain the remaining budget across the session (the overwhelm-fatigue pair naming the acute and chronic failure modes respectively); the bottleneck evidence — why capacity binds: the documented working-memory limits (the Cowan four-chunk consensus for novel information, the Miller seven-plus-or-minus-two historical frame with its chunking caveat, the seconds-long decay without refresh) versus the effectively unlimited long-term memory, making every dashboard reading a threading of dense displays through a four-slot needle; the element-interactivity principle — the load unit: cognitive load driven not by element count but by the number of interacting elements that must be processed simultaneously (the documented distinction: forty independent bars impose low interacting load — each readable alone; four cross-referenced series with shared legends and annotations impose high interacting load), establishing interactivity management as the core design lever over quantity reduction; the information-design translation — the dashboard stakes: monitoring displays demanding the glanceable-extract (the preattentive course-2 layer feeding working memory), analysis displays demanding the multi-element hold (the comparison-and-integration work consuming slots), and the design failure modes (the overwhelmed user's abandonment, the fatigued user's error-rate climb — the documented vigilance and workload findings) as the cost of load mismanagement; the three-load taxonomy previewed — the framework's core structure: intrinsic load (the inherent interactivity of the information itself — irreducible but sequenceable), extraneous load (the processing wasted on poor presentation — the designer's responsibility to eliminate), and germane load (the productive effort building understanding and schemas — the investment to protect and cultivate) as the accounting system every section applies; the schema concept introduced — the capacity escape hatch: organized knowledge patterns in long-term memory (the expert's chunked dashboard-reading structures — the chart-literacy schemas) allowing effective capacity expansion (a schema counts as one chunk regardless of internal complexity), making schema-building the long-term load-reduction strategy and expertise the accumulated load-immunity; the measurement tradition previewed — load as measurable quantity: the subjective workload scales (the NASA-TLX and Paas-scale instruments), the performance indicators (error rates, time-on-task, the dual-task interference paradigm), and the physiological measures (the pupillometry and heart-rate-variability research tools) as the evidence culture detailed in section 11; the relationship to sibling sciences — the curriculum position: gestalt grouping (course 1) as the structure that reduces interacting elements into chunks, preattentive channels (course 2) as the pre-working-memory filtering, information architecture (course 7) as the navigation-scale load management, and accessibility (course 9) as the capacity-variance inclusion — making load theory the economics uniting the perception courses; the designer's ethics frame — the responsibility position: load as a cost charged to the viewer (the attention-tax concept — every extraneous element billing the user's finite budget for the designer's convenience or decoration), making load-honesty (the design-review question: what does this element cost, what does it earn?) the professional standard; and the course map from memory architecture through the load taxonomy, the effect catalog, visual working memory, dashboard complexity management, progressive disclosure, foraging load, measurement, fatigue, interaction load, expertise adaptation, and the capstone load-balanced dashboard system.
