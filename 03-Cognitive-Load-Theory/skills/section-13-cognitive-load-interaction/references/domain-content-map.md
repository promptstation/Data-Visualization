# Domain Content Map — Interaction Load: Animation, Direct Manipulation, and Responsive Feedback

Read this during Phase 3 (architecture) and Phase 4 (writing) of the module
development workflow. Verify all factual claims and numbers against the
authoritative sources before publishing (Phase 2 evidence base); flag

## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Balance interaction load sheets (relief versus charge per feature) with feedback-latency engineering against 100ms-300ms-second perception thresholds and wait-load mitigation.
2. Manage animation load through transition-continuity benefits versus duration-capture-vestibular costs within purpose-required budgets, engineer direct manipulation with Fitts-law motor-load awareness and modeless preference, reduce filter-query load through state visibility, incremental feedback, and reversible exploration, choreograph interaction sequences with task-level click budgets and anticipatory affordances, ensure assistive-technology parity.
3. Validate through telemetry, controlled comparisons, and discovery testing preventing interaction theater, latency debt, gesture games, and modal mazes.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the load dynamics of interactive displays: the interaction-load concept — the two-way cost: interactions as load-relievers (the progressive disclosure, filtering, and exploration reducing simultaneous working-set — the sections 9-10 benefits) and load-chargers (the motor, attentional, and memory costs per interaction event — the executive-load accounting from section 2) making every interactive feature a load-balance-sheet item with the net-effect question; the feedback-latency load — the responsiveness factor: the documented latency-perception thresholds (the sub-100ms instant-feel requirement, the 100-300ms perceptible-response window, the second-scale wait requiring progress-communication — the Nielsen-response-time tradition), the wait-load mechanics (the unacknowledged-input anxiety and re-clicking, the uncertain-wait duration overestimation — the occupancy-load: waiting-without-feedback costing more working memory than the interaction itself) with the feedback-design responses (the immediate acknowledgment states, the progress-indicator grammar, the optimistic-UI patterns and their error-handling costs) as the responsiveness-load craft; the animation-load balance — the motion accounting: the transition-continuity benefits (the object-identity preservation reducing re-orientation load — the course-1 and 2 animation principles in load service: the change-comprehension without animation versus the tracking-with-animation trade-off findings), the animation-cost cases (the duration-imposed waits — the unskippable-transition tax, the motion-competing capture — the section-5 signaling collision, the vestibular and comfort load — the course-9 accessibility obligations) with the duration-budget discipline (the 150-300ms transition standard, the skip-and-reduce-motion provisions, the purpose-required animation rule) as the motion load-balance; the direct-manipulation load — the interaction-model effects: the direct-manipulation advantage (the visible-object continuous-action feedback loop reducing the command-memory load — the dragging-brushing-linking versus menu-command comparison, the interface-object mental-model simplicity), the manipulation-precision costs (the fine-motor targeting load — the Fitts-law target-size-and-distance mechanics as the motor-load model, the touch-versus-pointer precision variance), and the mode-confusion risks (the modal-interface state-memory load — the which-mode-am-I-in problem, the modeless-alternative preference) as the manipulation-load engineering; the filter-and-query interaction load — the exploration mechanics: the filter-state tracking cost (the active-filter working-memory burden — the section-10 filter-amnesia load, the filter-combination complexity — the AND-OR-NOT mental-model demands), the incremental-feedback patterns (the live-result-count and preview updates reducing the guess-and-check cycles — the immediate-consequence design), and the reset-and-undo economics (the exploration-safety reducing the caution-load — the reversible-interaction freedom) as the query-load management; the multi-touch and gesture load — the input-modality variance: the gesture-discoverability problem (the invisible-affordance learning load — the swipe-pinch-rotate conventions requiring schema acquisition, the gesture-conflict costs with system and browser behaviors), the input-modality switching load (the pointer-keyboard-touch transitions — the modality-reconfiguration cost, the keyboard-shortcut expertise dividend — the automated-interaction schema benefit) as the modality-load considerations; the interaction-choreography — the sequence design: the task-flow interaction counting (the interaction-budget per common task — the click-and-wait inventory against the frequency-weighted optimization from section 10, the one-extra-click-at-scale cost accumulation), the interaction-sequencing patterns (the logical-order control arrangement, the anticipatory-affordance placement — the next-action proximity to the current focus), and the batch-versus-apply decisions (the incremental-live versus explicit-apply filter models — the feedback-immediacy versus control-load trade-off) as the choreography craft; the interaction-accessibility load — the inclusive mechanics: the assistive-technology interaction costs (the screen-reader navigation-load of complex widgets — the focus-order and landmark requirements, the keyboard-only interaction parity — the full-functionality-without-pointer standard, the cognitive-accessibility interaction simplification) as the course-9 load-integration with the universal-design dividend (the low-load accessible interactions benefiting all users); the interaction-measurement practice — the evidence layer: the interaction-telemetry load signals (the click-count and path-length distributions per task, the retry-and-abandonment patterns at interaction points, the latency-exposure measurements — the actual response-time distributions users experience), the controlled interaction-comparison protocols (the variant A-B testing on task-time, error, and workload measures), and the gesture-discovery testing (the affordance-finding rates for novel interactions) as the interaction-evidence system; the interaction anti-patterns — the failure catalog: the interaction-theater (the elaborate controls adding motion-and-clicks without information gain — the fidget-spinner interface), the latency-blind ambition (the heavy interactions on slow pipelines — the responsiveness-debt), the gesture-guessing game (the undiscoverable interaction vocabulary — the learned-only interface), and the modal-maze (the stacked dialog and mode states — the orientation-collapse through interaction) as the interaction-hygiene rules; and the interaction deliverable — the load-balanced responsive manipulation, feedback, and choreography system.
