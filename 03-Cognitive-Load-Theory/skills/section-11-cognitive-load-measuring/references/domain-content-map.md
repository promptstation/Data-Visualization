# Domain Content Map — Measuring Cognitive Load: Workload Scales, Performance, and Physiology

Read this during Phase 3 (architecture) and Phase 4 (writing) of the module
development workflow. Verify all factual claims and numbers against the
authoritative sources before publishing (Phase 2 evidence base); flag
anything disputed rather than repeating it.

## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Triangulate cognitive load across subjective (NASA-TLX, Paas scales with bias caveats), performance (completion, errors, dual-task residual-capacity probes, process indicators), and physiological-gaze (pupillometry, fixation patterns, HRV-EEG at research tier) measurement families.
2. Design protocols with within-subjects counterbalancing, task baselines, fatigue controls, and small-n effect-size statistics.
3. Interpret convergence and divergence patterns (effortless-errors complacency, high-effort-high-performance germane signatures).
4. Run dashboard-specific batteries (glance recall, comparison timing, exception-under-load, longitudinal sessions) with production telemetry proxies under inference caution, and report multi-method load evidence translated to user-cost and business-risk terms.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Builds the load-measurement capability: the measurement-challenge frame — the construct problem: cognitive load as a latent variable (the internal processing demand not directly observable) requiring triangulated proxies across the three measurement families — the subjective (the self-reported effort), the performance (the behavioral degradation under load), and the physiological (the bodily load correlates), each with documented strengths and failure modes demanding multi-method evidence; the subjective instruments — the self-report toolkit: the NASA-TLX (the six-dimension workload profile — mental demand, temporal demand, performance, effort, frustration, physical demand — with the weighted and unweighted scoring practices), the Paas mental-effort scale (the single-item nine-point invested-effort rating — the low-burden high-validity workhorse for variant comparisons), the task-specific difficulty ratings, and the subjective-measure caveats (the self-report biases — the effort-versus-performance confusion, the scale-interpretation variance, the post-hoc reconstruction problems) requiring the anchoring and triangulation discipline; the performance measures — the behavioral indicators: the primary-task metrics (the completion time, the error rate and type analysis, the comprehension-test accuracy as the direct load-consequence measures), the dual-task paradigm (the secondary-task degradation method — the residual-capacity probe: a simple concurrent task — the tone-response or digit-memorization — revealing spare capacity, the classic load-measurement instrument), and the process indicators (the interaction counts and path efficiency, the re-orientation behaviors — the back-navigation and re-scan rates, the hesitation patterns) as the objective load evidence; the eye-tracking load indicators — the gaze correlates: the documented load-associated gaze patterns (the fixation-duration increase under load, the pupil-dilation response — the pupillometry as the continuous load proxy with its luminance-confound controls, the scanpath-entropy and regression rates — the disorganized-search signatures, the blink-rate changes) integrating the course-1 and 2 gaze methods into load measurement with the interpretation-caution (gaze-load correlations moderate and context-dependent); the physiological measures — the body correlates: the research-grade instruments (the heart-rate-variability suppression under load, the galvanic-skin-response arousal indicators, the EEG frontal-theta and parietal-alpha load markers — the neuroscience literature status) with the practical-access reality (the wearable-grade approximations, the webcam-pupillometry emerging tools, the consumer-EEG limitations) making physiology a research-tier complement rather than a routine design tool; the measurement-design practice — the protocol craft: the variant-comparison structure (the same task and data across design conditions — the within-subjects counterbalanced protocols maximizing sensitivity per participant), the load-baseline establishment (the task-only baselines separating the display-attributable load from the task-intrinsic load), the order-and-fatigue controls (the counterbalancing and break scheduling — the session-length discipline preventing measurement-induced load), and the sample-size realities (the small-n design-research statistics — the effect-size focus, the bootstrap intervals, the directional-evidence standards from the course-19 discipline adapted to design contexts); the triangulation framework — the evidence combination: the convergent-validity logic (the load conclusions requiring agreement across families — the high-TLX plus slow-performance plus pupil-dilation convergence versus the single-method claims), the divergence-diagnosis (the subjective-objective disagreement patterns: the felt-effortless errors — the automation-complacency signature, the high-effort-high-performance — the germane-load signature distinguishing productive strain from waste) as the interpretation sophistication; the dashboard-specific load batteries — the applied protocols: the glance-comprehension tests (the short-exposure recall as the first-parse load probe), the comparison-task timing (the cross-panel and cross-series comparison speeds by design variant), the exception-detection-under-load trials (the seeded-anomaly detection with concurrent demands — the realistic monitoring simulation), and the session-longitudinal protocols (the repeated-exposure tracking — the learning-curve and fatigue-accumulation measurement connecting to section 12) as the BI-measurement toolkit; the analytics-proxy layer — the production telemetry: the behavioral load signals at scale (the task-abandonment rates, the help-and-definition lookups as the confusion markers, the filter-thrashing patterns — the repeated trial-and-error interactions, the session-duration outliers) with the telemetry-load-inference caution (the correlation-not-measurement discipline — the telemetry suggesting load questions the controlled tests must answer); the measurement anti-patterns — the methodology failures: the single-scale fallacy (the TLX-alone as sufficient evidence), the designer-participant contamination (the author-tested load claims — the course-1 naive-viewer principle applied), the task-difficulty confound (comparing variants on different tasks — the baseline absence), and the measurement-load paradox (the instrument burden adding the load it measures — the protocol-minimalism requirement) as the hygiene rules; the reporting practice — the evidence communication: the load-evidence presentation (the multi-method exhibits — the workload-profile charts, the performance-delta tables, the convergence narrative), the stakeholder translation (the load findings as user-cost and business-risk statements — the error-rate and abandonment implications) connecting to the executive-communication discipline (course 15) as the actionable-evidence craft; and the measurement deliverable — the triangulated load-assessment capability with protocols, telemetry proxies, and reporting practice.
