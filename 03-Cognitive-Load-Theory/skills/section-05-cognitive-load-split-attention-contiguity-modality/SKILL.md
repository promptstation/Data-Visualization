# Split Attention, Contiguity, and Modality: The Mayer Effect Toolkit I — Professional Training

## Mission

Produce professional-grade learning modules and training materials on **split Attention, Contiguity, and Modality: The Mayer Effect Toolkit I** within The science of working-memory limits applied to information design — managing intrinsic, extraneous, and germane load so complex dashboards inform users without overwhelming or fatiguing them.

Subject scope: Covers the experimentally tested load-reduction effects: the effects-tradition frame — the evidence base: the Mayer multimedia-learning research program's documented effects (the controlled experiments isolating presentation-caused load with transfer-and-retention outcomes) as the tested toolkit for visualization adaptation, each effect a designer-usable pattern with a known mechanism and measured benefit; the split-attention effect — the separation cost: the documented finding that integrating separated information sources reduces load and improves comprehension (the text-inside-diagram superiority over text-beside-diagram, the mental-integration tax: holding location A's label while processing location B's graphic — the visual-search plus working-memory double cost), with the visualization applications (the direct-labeling of series replacing legend lookup, the in-chart annotations replacing footnote references, the integrated value-labels on bars replacing axis-tracing, the co-located tooltips replacing separated detail panels) as the contiguity-first layout discipline; the spatial-contiguity principle — the proximity rule: the related-words-and-graphics-together requirement (the documented effect sizes among the largest in the multimedia literature) with the quantitative design standard (the integration distance — the label-to-mark gap minimizing the eye-travel and binding cost, the Gestalt-proximity mechanism from course 1 as the perceptual basis) and the layout audit (the separated-source inventory — every legend, footnote, and detached label assessed for integration); the temporal-contiguity principle — the synchronization rule: the simultaneous-presentation requirement for related animation and narration (the documented cost of sequential presentation of conceptually-linked events) with the visualization applications (the synchronized transition-and-explanation timing in animated and scrollytelling contexts — the course-13 preview, the coordinated-update timing in linked views — the cross-panel change simultaneity) as the time-domain contiguity; the modality principle — the channel distribution: the documented pictures-plus-narration superiority over pictures-plus-on-screen-text (the dual-channel exploitation from section 2 — the verbal load routed to the auditory channel freeing visual capacity) with the dashboard applications and limits (the audio-annotation value in presentation and kiosk contexts, the open-office and accessibility counter-constraints — the modality principle's context dependence, the caption-obligation) making modality a situational tool not a universal rule; the signaling principle — the cue effect: the documented comprehension gains from attention-guiding cues (the arrows, highlights, and emphasis marking the relevant information — the preattentive course-2 channels in load service: signaling reducing the search-and-select extraneous load), with the signal-design discipline (the cue-relevance requirement — signals on decision-critical elements only, the cue-restraint budget — every signal competing with every other) as the guided-attention load relief; the segmenting principle — the pace control: the documented benefit of learner-paced chunked presentation over continuous flow (the complex-animation and long-explanation segmentation at logical boundaries) with the dashboard applications (the progressive-disclosure sequencing — the section-9 preview, the stepped-comparison tools — the animation-controls and step-through interfaces) as the user-controlled-pacing load management; the pre-training principle — the component-first strategy: the documented advantage of teaching component names and characteristics before the combined process (the vocabulary-first comprehension) with the dashboard applications (the legend-and-encoding orientation before complex comparison, the onboarding glossary and metric-definition access, the annotation-defined terms) as the schema-priming load investment; the effect-combination practice — the pattern stacking: the documented compatible combinations (the contiguity-plus-signaling integration, the segmenting-plus-modality pairing in guided tours) with the interaction cautions (the over-cuing redundancy — signals on everything signaling nothing, the segment-fragmentation cost — too-small segments breaking coherence) as the composition discipline; the effect-limitations honesty — the boundary conditions: the documented moderators (the expertise-reversal of several effects — the section-6 preview, the content-complexity dependence, the medium and context constraints) with the local-validation requirement (the effect sizes from laboratory multimedia learning transferring imperfectly to dense professional dashboards — the test-don't-assume discipline) as the evidence maturity; and the toolkit deliverable — the contiguity, modality, signaling, segmenting, and pre-training patterns applied with combination and boundary discipline.

Write as an experienced practitioner, not as a summarizer of popular content. Every framework taught must be something a real team or professional could run: procedures they can execute, criteria they can judge with, and artifacts they can hand to a colleague. ## Anti-Patterns

Avoid: Treating load as only-about-quantity — element interactivity, not element count, drives intrinsic load: four interacting series can cost more than forty parallel ones; Optimizing extraneous load to zero while ignoring germane load — frictionless displays that teach nothing, leaving users permanently dependent on the designer's pre-chewing; Expertise-reversal blindness — scaffolding that helps novices (guides, annotations, step-by-step layering) becoming redundant load for experts, one design serving neither; Density panic — stripping legitimate information to reduce felt complexity, when the real fix is structure: chunking, alignment, and progressive disclosure carrying density at low load; Measuring load by asking designers — self-report from the display's authors is worthless; load evidence requires naive users, workload scales, and performance triangulation; exercises without evaluation criteria; modules that stop at inspiration without a single decision the learner can now make better; and any content that overstates certainty beyond what the evidence supports.

## Decision Heuristic

When allocating depth under time or length limits, ask:

1. Where will this audience actually stall in practice?
2. Which unit protects the most value if taught well?
3. Can the learner run the framework tomorrow without me?
4. Is every example doing work a plain sentence could not?
5. What would a skeptical domain expert say about this material?

If two topics compete for space, keep the one that changes a decision.
