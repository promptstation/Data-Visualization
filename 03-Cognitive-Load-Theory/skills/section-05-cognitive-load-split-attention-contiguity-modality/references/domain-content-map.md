## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the experimentally tested load-reduction effects: the effects-tradition frame — the evidence base: the Mayer multimedia-learning research program's documented effects (the controlled experiments isolating presentation-caused load with transfer-and-retention outcomes) as the tested toolkit for visualization adaptation, each effect a designer-usable pattern with a known mechanism and measured benefit; the split-attention effect — the separation cost: the documented finding that integrating separated information sources reduces load and improves comprehension (the text-inside-diagram superiority over text-beside-diagram, the mental-integration tax: holding location A's label while processing location B's graphic — the visual-search plus working-memory double cost), with the visualization applications (the direct-labeling of series replacing legend lookup, the in-chart annotations replacing footnote references, the integrated value-labels on bars replacing axis-tracing, the co-located tooltips replacing separated detail panels) as the contiguity-first layout discipline; the spatial-contiguity principle — the proximity rule: the related-words-and-graphics-together requirement (the documented effect sizes among the largest in the multimedia literature) with the quantitative design standard (the integration distance — the label-to-mark gap minimizing the eye-travel and binding cost, the Gestalt-proximity mechanism from course 1 as the perceptual basis) and the layout audit (the separated-source inventory — every legend, footnote, and detached label assessed for integration); the temporal-contiguity principle — the synchronization rule: the simultaneous-presentation requirement for related animation and narration (the documented cost of sequential presentation of conceptually-linked events) with the visualization applications (the synchronized transition-and-explanation timing in animated and scrollytelling contexts — the course-13 preview, the coordinated-update timing in linked views — the cross-panel change simultaneity) as the time-domain contiguity; the modality principle — the channel distribution: the documented pictures-plus-narration superiority over pictures-plus-on-screen-text (the dual-channel exploitation from section 2 — the verbal load routed to the auditory channel freeing visual capacity) with the dashboard applications and limits (the audio-annotation value in presentation and kiosk contexts, the open-office and accessibility counter-constraints — the modality principle's context dependence, the caption-obligation) making modality a situational tool not a universal rule; the signaling principle — the cue effect: the documented comprehension gains from attention-guiding cues (the arrows, highlights, and emphasis marking the relevant information — the preattentive course-2 channels in load service: signaling reducing the search-and-select extraneous load), with the signal-design discipline (the cue-relevance requirement — signals on decision-critical elements only, the cue-restraint budget — every signal competing with every other) as the guided-attention load relief; the segmenting principle — the pace control: the documented benefit of learner-paced chunked presentation over continuous flow (the complex-animation and long-explanation segmentation at logical boundaries) with the dashboard applications (the progressive-disclosure sequencing — the section-9 preview, the stepped-comparison tools — the animation-controls and step-through interfaces) as the user-controlled-pacing load management; the pre-training principle — the component-first strategy: the documented advantage of teaching component names and characteristics before the combined process (the vocabulary-first comprehension) with the dashboard applications (the legend-and-encoding orientation before complex comparison, the onboarding glossary and metric-definition access, the annotation-defined terms) as the schema-priming load investment; the effect-combination practice — the pattern stacking: the documented compatible combinations (the contiguity-plus-signaling integration, the segmenting-plus-modality pairing in guided tours) with the interaction cautions (the over-cuing redundancy — signals on everything signaling nothing, the segment-fragmentation cost — too-small segments breaking coherence) as the composition discipline; the effect-limitations honesty — the boundary conditions: the documented moderators (the expertise-reversal of several effects — the section-6 preview, the content-complexity dependence, the medium and context constraints) with the local-validation requirement (the effect sizes from laboratory multimedia learning transferring imperfectly to dense professional dashboards — the test-don't-assume discipline) as the evidence maturity; and the toolkit deliverable — the contiguity, modality, signaling, segmenting, and pre-training patterns applied with combination and boundary discipline.
