## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Builds the cognitive-systems model: the modal architecture — the processing pipeline: sensory registers (the icon and echo buffers — the brief high-capacity perceptual holds feeding selection), working memory (the capacity-limited active-processing workspace — the bottleneck), and long-term memory (the effectively unlimited organized store — the knowledge resource), with the information-flow view of dashboard reading (retinal input through preattentive filtering — the course-2 layer — into working-memory encoding, comparison, and integration against long-term schema knowledge) as the complete processing chain designers engineer within; Baddeley's working-memory model — the workspace anatomy: the central executive (the attention controller — the allocation and switching mechanism), the phonological loop (the verbal-rehearsal channel — the inner-voice holding of labels and numbers), the visuospatial sketchpad (the visual-spatial channel — the mental-canvas holding of chart shapes and positions, with the documented visual-versus-spatial subsystem distinction), and the episodic buffer (the multimodal integration workspace binding features into coherent episodes) as the four-component model; the channel-capacity implications — the multi-channel opportunity: the documented partial-independence of verbal and visual working memory (the mixed-modality advantage — pictures-plus-words exceeding words-alone capacity per the Paivio dual-coding and Baddeley cross-channel findings), making well-designed dashboards distribute load across channels (the numeric label and the bar shape carrying the same fact through two buffers) while poorly designed ones collide (the narration-plus-text duplication jamming the verbal channel — the redundancy-effect preview); the visual-persistence data — the icon-to-sketchpad flow: the documented icon capacity and decay (the Sperling partial-report findings — the large-capacity sub-second visual buffer), its role as the pre-attention supply (the course-2 preattentive extraction from the icon), and the design implication (the glance-window reality: what the icon-to-sketchpad transfer captures in one fixation is the display's pre-attentive yield); long-term memory structures — the knowledge side: the schema networks (the organized chart-reading, category, and domain knowledge), the automatization process (the practiced interpretations moving from executive-effort to schema-trigger — the reading-a-bar-height without deliberation progression), and the activation dynamics (the context-cued retrieval — the annotation and title priming relevant schemas) making long-term memory the load-relief reservoir the designer primes; the executive-function load — the control costs: the documented switching and inhibition costs (the task-switch latency, the interference suppression effort — the ignoring-distractions expense), the attention-control fatigue (the executive-resource depletion under sustained demand — the self-control and vigilance literature connections), and the design translation (every modality switch, distractor suppression, and re-orientation event charging executive load — the interaction-cost accounting); the capacity-variance reality — the individual differences: the documented working-memory span variation across individuals (the low-span and high-span populations with the design implications — the low-span-user accommodation as the inclusive default), the age and clinical variance (the older-adult working-memory changes, the ADHD and anxiety load-sensitivity), and the expertise compensation (the schema-based capacity expansion from section 1) as the audience-capacity distribution designers serve; the load-leakage points — the failure analysis: where dashboard reading loses capacity (the split attention across separated sources, the redundant re-processing of duplicated information, the extraneous decoding of unclear encodings, the re-orientation costs after interaction) previewing the effect catalog (sections 5-6) as the leakage map; the architecture-to-design principles — the translation table: each architectural property yielding a design rule (the four-chunk limit → chunking and hierarchy; the dual channels → mixed-modality encoding; the icon window → glanceable first-parse; the executive costs → switch minimization; the LTM reservoir → schema-priming titles and annotations) as the mechanism-grounded practice bridge; the model-honesty note — the science status: the working-memory model debates (the resource-pool versus slot accounts, the attention-versus-storage distinction, the embedded-processes alternatives) with the design-pragmatism stance (the models agree on the bottlenecks and costs that matter for layout and encoding decisions — the engineering-usable consensus); and the architecture deliverable — the memory-systems model grounding every load-management decision.
