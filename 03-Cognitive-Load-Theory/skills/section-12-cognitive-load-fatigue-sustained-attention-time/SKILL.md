# Fatigue, Sustained Attention, and the Time-on-Task Dimension — Professional Training

## Mission

Produce professional-grade learning modules and training materials on **fatigue, Sustained Attention, and the Time-on-Task Dimension** within The science of working-memory limits applied to information design — managing intrinsic, extraneous, and germane load so complex dashboards inform users without overwhelming or fatiguing them.

Subject scope: Covers the chronic-load dimension — the source's fatigue concern: the acute-versus-chronic load distinction — the temporal framing: the single-interaction load (the sections 1-11 domain — the working-set and effect management per task) versus the sustained-session load (the accumulation dynamics: the resource depletion over minutes and hours producing the fatigue failure mode the source names — the informed-without-fatiguing second requirement) as the course's temporal extension; the vigilance-decay science — the sustained-attention findings: the documented time-on-task degradation (the vigilance decrement — the detection-rate decline and response-time drift over sustained monitoring, the Mackworth-clock tradition through modern analytics-operations studies), the decrement causes (the resource-depletion and expectation-habituation accounts, the event-rarity effects — the low-signal-rate degradation) with the dashboard translation (the monitoring-analyst performance curve as the design target); the mental-fatigue mechanisms — the depletion dynamics: the sustained-executive-function costs (the continuous inhibition-of-distraction and task-maintenance expenditure — the attention-control fatigue from section 2), the decision-fatigue accumulation (the repeated-judgment degradation — the quality drift across session decisions), and the recovery dynamics (the micro-break restoration findings, the task-variety relief — the switching-as-rest paradox within bounds) as the fatigue-mechanism model; the load-accumulation design — the session architecture: the pacing structures (the rhythmic task variation — the monitoring-analysis-review rotation patterns, the natural-breakpoint design — the session-segment boundaries with summary moments), the attention-restoration supports (the calm-state displays — the low-arousal default views, the nature-and-palette rest considerations at awareness level, the reduced-stimulation night and long-shift modes) as the temporal-composition craft; the display-fatigue factors — the visual-comfort layer: the sustained-viewing strain contributors (the luminance and contrast extremes — the glare and dark-mode strain considerations, the dense-small-type reading fatigue — the typography endurance factors, the animation-and-flicker accumulation — the continuous-motion tax from course 2, the color-saturation overload — the vivid-field fatigue findings) with the comfort-design standards (the ambient-light-adaptive brightness, the endurance-typography minimums, the calm-motion defaults) as the perceptual-ergonomics layer; the expertise-and-fatigue interaction — the adaptation dimension: the documented fatigue-expertise relationship (the experts' automated processing resisting fatigue longer — the schema-efficiency dividend, the novice double-cost — the higher per-task load AND faster fatigue accumulation), with the fatigue-aware adaptation (the novice-session-length limits, the expert-endurance designs, the time-of-day and shift-context adjustments) as the audience-temporal planning; the fatigue anti-patterns — the failure catalog: the vigilance-assumption (designing for constant alertness — the undecayed-attention fallacy), the interruption-flood (the ungoverned notification streams compounding residue costs), the endurance-blind styling (the vivid-dense displays fine for glances toxic for shifts), and the fatigue-invisible telemetry (the degradation patterns unmonitored — the chronic-load measurement absence) as the temporal-hygiene rules; Write as an experienced practitioner, not as a summarizer of popular content. Every framework taught must be something a real team or professional could run: procedures they can execute, criteria they can judge with, and artifacts they can hand to a colleague. ## Core Output Requirements

- Deliverables are Markdown documents: the module, exercise sets with model solutions, and checklists. No placeholders, no "TODO" sections.
- Ground content in authoritative sources:
- Sweller's cognitive load theory — the instructional-design foundation (intrinsic, extraneous, and germane load with the element-interactivity basis)
- Baddeley's working-memory model (phonological loop, visuospatial sketchpad, central executive) and Cowan's revised capacity findings (the four-chunk limit)
- Miller's magical number seven lineage with the chunking and recoding tradition
- Mayer's multimedia learning principles (coherence, signaling, spatial-contiguity, temporal-contiguity, modality, redundancy) as the experimentally tested load-management toolkit
- Paas-van Merrienboer load measurement tradition (subjective rating scales, task-performance triangulation) and NASA-TLX workload assessment
- Stephen Few's dashboard design doctrine (glanceability, information density, chart-selection for monitoring) as the applied BI synthesis

- Maintain an evidence ledger while writing: every factual claim or number is either sourced, flagged as disputed/popular account, or omitted. Never invent statistics, studies, or citations.
- Distinguish established research findings from professional conventions and informed recommendations, and say which is which.
- Explain each specialized term in clear language on first use.

## Writing Standards

Throughout the material, prioritize language that is:

* Precise without becoming jargon-heavy
* Practical without discarding rigor
* Honest about limitations and contested findings without being defeatist
* Concrete — anchored in real cases, real artifacts, and verifiable numbers
* Progressive from fundamentals to expert judgment

Where a recommendation depends on context, explain the trade-off rather than presenting an absolute rule.

## Anti-Patterns

Avoid: Treating load as only-about-quantity — element interactivity, not element count, drives intrinsic load: four interacting series can cost more than forty parallel ones; Optimizing extraneous load to zero while ignoring germane load — frictionless displays that teach nothing, leaving users permanently dependent on the designer's pre-chewing; Expertise-reversal blindness — scaffolding that helps novices (guides, annotations, step-by-step layering) becoming redundant load for experts, one design serving neither; Density panic — stripping legitimate information to reduce felt complexity, when the real fix is structure: chunking, alignment, and progressive disclosure carrying density at low load; Measuring load by asking designers — self-report from the display's authors is worthless; load evidence requires naive users, workload scales, and performance triangulation; exercises without evaluation criteria; modules that stop at inspiration without a single decision the learner can now make better; and any content that overstates certainty beyond what the evidence supports.

## Decision Heuristic

When allocating depth under time or length limits, ask:

1. Where will this audience actually stall in practice?
2. Which unit protects the most value if taught well?
3. Can the learner run the framework tomorrow without me?
4. Is every example doing work a plain sentence could not?
5. What would a skeptical domain expert say about this material?

If two topics compete for space, keep the one that changes a decision.
