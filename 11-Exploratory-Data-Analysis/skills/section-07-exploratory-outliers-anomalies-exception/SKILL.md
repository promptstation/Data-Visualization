# Outliers, Anomalies, and the Exception Investigation — Professional Training

## Mission

Produce professional-grade learning modules and training materials on **outliers, Anomalies, and the Exception Investigation** within The investigative core of the data visualization specialist discipline — Tukey-lineage exploratory practice where visualization drives discovery: profiling, distribution reading, relationship hunting, outlier detection, and interactive exploration workflows that turn raw data into questions, patterns, and evidence.

Subject scope: Covers the exception-hunting discipline where EDA's most consequential findings often live: the outlier concept — definitions and distinctions: the definitional landscape (the deviation-from-expectation core reading: the outlier-as-model-relative concept where expectations come from distributions, relationships, or temporal patterns, the outlier-versus-anomaly-versus-error taxonomy: the legitimate-extreme-value versus suspicious-pattern versus data-quality-fault distinctions requiring different responses, the contextual-versus-global outliers: the values-extreme-only-within-their-subgroup or season from the anomaly-detection literature); the attitude discipline (the outliers-as-discoveries-not-nuisances stance from the Tukey tradition: the unexpected-observations-as-most-informative principle, the deletion-reflex resistance: the never-remove-before-investigation rule connecting to section 4's flag-investigate-decide protocol, the domain-meaning-first investigation: the business-and-physical-explanation search before statistical treatment); the outlier-generation mechanisms (the heavy-tail natural generation from section 3's distribution families: the power-law-and-lognormal contexts where extremes are expected, the mixture-population generation: the other-group-members appearing extreme in pooled views from section 4's bimodality readings, the error-and-fraud generation: the sentinel-values, entry-errors, and genuine-misbehavior from section 2's quality audits, the rare-event generation: the once-a-decade occurrences that are real but infrequent); and the expectation-dependence honesty (the no-model-free-outlier truth: the every-detection-embeds-assumptions acknowledgment, the method-plurality principle: the multiple-detection-agreement confidence building) as the conceptual foundation; the univariate detection methods — the single-variable screens: the fence-based flagging (the Tukey 1.5-IQR inner and 3-IQR outer fences from section 4's boxplot systems: the mild-versus-extreme classification conventions, the fence-arbitrariness awareness: the normal-distribution-calibration origins and their breakdown on skewed data, the adjusted-fence practices for skewed distributions: the log-transform-then-fence workflow from section 3's transformation discipline); the z-score family (the classical z-score with its mean-sd contamination vulnerability: the outlier-masking-its-own-detection problem, the robust modified-z-score using median and MAD from section 4's resistant family: the standard 3.5-threshold convention, the small-sample caveats: the z-score instability below thirty observations); the quantile-and-percentile approaches (the extreme-percentile flagging: the top-and-bottom-half-percent conventions for large data, the distribution-model comparison: the observed-versus-expected-extreme-frequency diagnostics from section 3's Q-Q traditions); the multivariate and relational detection — the joint-context methods: the leverage-and-influence reading (the x-extreme-versus-y-extreme position distinctions from section 5's scatter vocabulary: the high-leverage-points-shaping-fits awareness, the residual-versus-leverage displays: the influence-diagnostic scatterplots from the regression graphics tradition, the single-point-removal sensitivity: the recompute-without-suspect verification protocol); the Mahalanobis-distance approach (the covariance-weighted multivariate distance from the metric traditions of the geometry course: the accounting-for-correlation-structure advantage, the chi-square-reference comparison: the distance-versus-expected-distribution diagnostics, the covariance-robustness issues: the contaminated-covariance masking problem and the robust-estimation alternatives at awareness); the relationship-deviation detection (the residual-outliers from smoothing overlays: the points-far-from-LOESS-curves from section 5's nonlinear methods, the conditional-outlier logic: the values-normal-marginally-but-extreme-given-predictors, the local-density anomalies at awareness: the LOF-family neighbor-density comparisons from the anomaly-detection literature); Write as an experienced practitioner, not as a summarizer of popular content. Do not present claims as settled when the field treats them as contested — the training must model evidence discipline.

## Writing Standards

Throughout the material, prioritize language that is:

* Precise without becoming jargon-heavy
* Practical without discarding rigor
* Honest about limitations and contested findings without being defeatist
* Concrete — anchored in real cases, real artifacts, and verifiable numbers
* Progressive from fundamentals to expert judgment

Where a recommendation depends on context, explain the trade-off rather than presenting an absolute rule.

## Anti-Patterns

Avoid: Confirmatory masquerading as exploratory — arriving with the conclusion, cherry-picking the slices and scales that support it, and dressing the result in EDA language instead of running honest open-ended investigation; Summary-statistic blindness — trusting means and correlations before looking at distributions, so bimodality, skew, outliers, and Anscombe-quartet-style structural differences pass undetected; Missing-data amnesia — profiling values while ignoring absence: the NaN patterns, collection gaps, and silent nulls that carry more signal than the numbers themselves; Tool-driven exploration — letting dashboard defaults and library conveniences decide the questions, producing the same views every time instead of fitting the investigation to the data's structure; The undiscovered discovery — finding real patterns but losing them to unreproducible notebook chaos, unannotated screenshots, and workflows no colleague can rerun or trust; exercises without evaluation criteria; modules that stop at inspiration without a single decision the learner can now make better; and any content that overstates certainty beyond what the evidence supports.

## Decision Heuristic

When allocating depth under time or length limits, ask:

1. Where will this audience actually stall in practice?
2. Which unit protects the most value if taught well?
3. Can the learner run the framework tomorrow without me?
4. Is every example doing work a plain sentence could not?
5. What would a skeptical domain expert say about this material?

If two topics compete for space, keep the one that changes a decision.

## Final Principle

The goal is not more content about Exploratory data analysis. The goal is that a practitioner could take this material and perform: Hunt and handle exceptions through conceptual grounding (outlier-anomaly-error taxonomy with contextual-global distinctions, discovery-stance attitude discipline, generation-mechanism understanding, expectation-dependence honesty with method plurality), screen univariates (Tukey fences with skew adjustment, classical-versus-robust z-scores with masking awareness, percentile and Q-Q approaches, categorical rarity, exception visualization conventions), run investigation protocols (triage classification with domain consultation, provenance tracing, impact sensitivity assessment, keep-flag-transform-remove decision matrices with documentation and parallel analysis, narrative communication), and avoid deletion-reflex, z-masking, fence-misapplication, marginal-only, seasonal-blindness, tunnel-vision, undocumented-treatment, and suppression failures — with the same evidence discipline the field's best practitioners use. The module must function simultaneously as a learning resource, a practical reference, and a working methodology.
