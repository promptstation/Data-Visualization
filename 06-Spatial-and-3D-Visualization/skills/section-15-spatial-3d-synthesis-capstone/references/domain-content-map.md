# Domain Content Map — Synthesis and Capstone: The Spatial Visualization System

Read this during Phase 3 (architecture) and Phase 4 (writing) of the module
development workflow. Verify all factual claims and numbers against the
authoritative sources before publishing (Phase 2 evidence base); flag
anything disputed rather than repeating it.

## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Execute the complete spatial-system arc from requirement and data-quality assessment through pipeline engineering, technology selection.
2. Design architecture, implementation, performance hardening, governance, and delivery.
3. Demonstrate three-pillar spatial judgment (data-engineering rigor, perception-design science, platform-performance fluency) across the consolidated coordinate, data, mapping, thematic, point-scale, analysis, 3D, interaction, performance.
4. Design knowledge.
5. Apply 2D-first 3D-when-justified and map-as-argument ethics at system level.
6. Integrate perception, load, graphics, D3, IA, latency, accessibility, math, EDA, embedding, narrative, and semiographic disciplines into spatial systems.
7. Prevent canonical failures (projection mixing, normalization failures, 3D excess, attribution violations, MAUP blindness, privacy cascades) through documented systems.
8. And uphold honest-representation, inclusion-floor, privacy-equity.
9. Map-power-stewardship ethics climbing toward spatial-systems leadership.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Prepares expert integration: the consolidated capability map — the spatial foundations (spatial thinking, cartographic lineage, coordinate and projection mastery with geodesy precision), the data engineering (format ecosystem, preparation pipelines, tiling and scale strategies, spatial joins and aggregations), the mapping stacks (Leaflet lightweight mapping, MapLibre-Mapbox vector styling, deck.gl massive overlays), the thematic design (choropleth-classification rigor, symbol and density maps, honest multivariate and cartogram techniques), the point-scale systems (clustering, density, GPU, and indexing toolkit), the spatial analysis (buffer, isochrone, routing computation integration), the 3D capability (Three.js foundations, honest 3D representation, terrain-globe-building-twin rendering), the interaction systems (navigation, picking, query, temporal, multiview, accessible interaction), the performance engineering (reduction, streaming, culling, GPU, memory, device, network optimization with governance), and the design human-factors (cartographic craft, perception science, uncertainty visualization, accessibility, ethics, design systems) as one spatial-visualization discipline; the capstone workflow — the master arc: requirement and context analysis (the spatial question, audience, data availability and quality, scale and device fleet, accessibility and ethics requirements defining the system class), data-pipeline engineering (the source acquisition and assessment, format conversion and projection normalization, simplification-tiling-and-aggregation preparation, join and enrichment workflows with quality validation), technology selection (the Leaflet versus MapLibre versus deck.gl versus Three.js versus hybrid decision through the capability-scale-interaction matrices with simplest-sufficient discipline), design architecture (the thematic encoding and classification decisions, the visual hierarchy and composition, the interaction and navigation model, the accessibility and uncertainty-disclosure architecture), implementation (the base and data layer rendering, the interaction and analysis integration, the responsive and mobile adaptation, the 3D elements where justified), performance hardening (the budget-compliance verification, the reduction and streaming optimization, the device-tier adaptation with degradation chains), quality and governance (the design-review audit execution, the accessibility testing, the performance and accuracy validation, the attribution and license compliance, the documentation and reproducibility package), and delivery (the deployment with monitoring, the user feedback and iteration cycle) as the complete professional cycle; the integration thesis — the spatial-systems judgment: spatial mastery as pipeline plus perception plus platform (the data-engineering rigor preventing garbage geography in, the perception and design science preventing misleading or illegible maps out, the platform and performance fluency enabling production-scale delivery — the three-pillar competency), the 2D-first 3D-when-justified principle (the honesty-framework application at system level — the discipline restraint pairing the source mandate for 3D and map skills with the documented distortion evidence), and the map-as-argument awareness (every spatial visualization advancing a thesis — the ethical design responsibility from the ethics layer) as the mature spatial judgment; cross-course integration — the curriculum position: Gestalt and preattentive science (courses 1-2) governing the layer-hierarchy, clustering-display, and salience decisions in maps, cognitive load (course 3) bounding the navigation and disclosure complexity, graphics APIs (course 4) as the GPU-rendering foundation under MapLibre, deck.gl, and Three.js, D3-SVG craft (course 5) powering the geo-projection rendering, contour and force layouts, and hybrid chrome layers, information architecture (course 7) shaping the multi-view and drill-down structures, latency tuning (course 8) setting the interaction-responsiveness budgets, accessibility (course 9) as the inclusion floor implemented in spatial specifics, linear algebra (course 10) as the projection-transform and camera mathematics, EDA (course 11) supplying the spatial-pattern discovery informing map design, dimensionality reduction (course 12) as the embedding-projection-into-display partner, scrollytelling (course 13) as the narrative-map application, semiographics (course 14) informing the symbol and icon design systems, and executive communication (course 15) as the decision-support map context; case synthesis — the worked systems (the national public-health access dashboard — the choropleth, isochrone, and facility-point multilayer system with privacy protections and accessibility, the urban digital-twin operations view — the terrain, building, and sensor 3D system with 2D fallbacks and real-time streams, the climate-risk explorer — the multi-scenario field rendering with uncertainty visualization and narrative tours, the logistics-fleet monitor — the million-point streaming map with clustering, trajectory, and viewport performance engineering) decomposed through the capstone workflow; failure post-mortems — the canonical errors (projection-mixing offsets, choropleth-normalization failures, 3D-decoration excess, tile-attribution violations, scale-generalization mismatch, point-overplotting collapse, MAUP blindness, geocoding-accuracy cascade ignorance, memory-leak long-session crashes, inaccessible-map shipping) with the prevention systems from sections 1-14; the maturity model — stage one (coordinate and format literacy with basic Leaflet mapping), stage two (vector-tile styling and thematic-design rigor with analysis integration), stage three (3D and massive-scale systems with performance and accessibility engineering), stage four (spatial-systems leadership — the digital-twin and platform architecture, design governance, ethics stewardship, cross-discipline team direction) as the capability roadmap; the professional-ethics position — the standard: the honest-representation obligation (projection, classification, and uncertainty transparency, the 3D-justification discipline, the aggregation and generalization disclosure), the inclusion-floor commitment (accessible spatial experiences as architecture, the 2D and non-visual equivalents), the privacy-and-equity responsibility (location-data protection, the underrepresented-geography awareness, the community-harm-prevention review), and the stewardship of map power (the persuasion-integrity boundary, the source and method transparency building public trust) as the spatial ethics; and the capstone — a complete spatial-visualization system: select a real spatial question and audience, run the requirement and data assessment, engineer the complete pipeline (acquisition through tiling), select and justify the technology stack, design and implement the system (thematic layers, interactions, analysis integration, 3D where justified) with the accessibility and uncertainty architecture, harden performance within budgets across device tiers, pass the design-review audit and testing gates, and deliver with documentation, attribution compliance, monitoring, and ethics-review evidence.

## Unit 2 — Capstone integration

**Purpose:** integrate every capability above on one realistic problem end to end.

**Structure:** the learner takes a single problem in Spatial and 3D through each unit's framework in sequence, producing a coherent deliverable that mirrors real professional documentation. Evaluation criteria: internal consistency across artifacts, evidence discipline, honest trade-off statements, and demonstrable use of each unit's framework.
