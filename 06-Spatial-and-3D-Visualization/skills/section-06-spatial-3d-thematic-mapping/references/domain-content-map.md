# Domain Content Map — Thematic Mapping: Choropleths, Symbol Maps, and Classification Design

Read this during Phase 3 (architecture) and Phase 4 (writing) of the module
development workflow. Verify all factual claims and numbers against the
authoritative sources before publishing (Phase 2 evidence base); flag
anything disputed rather than repeating it.

## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Design thematic maps through the family taxonomy (choropleth, proportional-symbol, dot-density, isopleth, flow, cartogram) with base-map subordination.
2. Build choropleths through normalization imperatives, distribution-first classification selection (quantile-Jenks-interval methods, 4-7 classes), and ColorBrewer-CVD-verified ramps while preventing MAUP, ecological-fallacy, and boundary-emphasis distortions via multi-scale presentation and uncertainty disclosure.
3. Construct area-proportional symbol maps with overlap management, dot-density dasymetric refinements, contour-field renderings, OD-flow and trajectory maps, bivariate grids under variable-count restraint, and Dorling-cartogram corrections with base-geography references, and complete communication through legend-design matching, furniture audits, extent-inset composition, and responsive legibility adaptation.

the choropleth-critique literacy — the known distortions: the MAUP problem (the modifiable-areal-unit issue — the boundary-and-scale dependence of aggregated patterns: the same data telling different stories at county-state-regional levels), the ecological-fallacy risk (the area-average misreading as individual behavior), the unequal-area perception bias (the large-geography visual dominance in raw patterns — the cartogram alternative awareness), the boundary-emphasis artifact (the hard-edge classification implying precision between classes — the smooth-gradient alternatives for continuous phenomena) and the mitigation practices (the multi-scale presentation, the uncertainty-and-margin annotations, the underlying-distribution disclosure alongside classified views) as the honest-choropleth discipline; the proportional-symbol system — the point-magnitude mapping: the symbol-scaling mathematics (the area-proportional scaling — the radius-as-square-root-of-value rule preventing the visual-magnitude overstatement, the Flannery-compensation awareness), the symbol-design choices (the circles versus squares versus pictograms, the graduated-versus-continuous scaling decisions), the overlap-and-occlusion management (the symbol-sorting large-behind-small ordering, the transparency and offset strategies for dense clusters), and the bivariate extensions (the two-variable symbol encodings — the color-plus-size dual channels with the legend complexity cost) as the symbol-map craft; the dot-density and dasymetric techniques — the distribution rendering: the dot-density mechanics (the dot-value and dot-size calibration — the one-dot-per-N-units convention, the random-within-polygon placement with the inhabited-area masking), the dasymetric refinement (the ancillary-data redistribution — the landcover-and-building-footprint weighting moving dot-density beyond administrative uniformity toward realistic distributions), and the technique-fit analysis (the dot-density for count-distribution perception versus choropleth for rate-comparison) as the distribution toolkit; the isopleth-and-field mapping — the continuous surfaces: the contour-and-isoline conventions (the elevation-isopleth cartographic tradition, the interpolation-from-samples mechanics — the IDW-kriging concepts at awareness, the smooth-generalization surface rendering), the field-rendering implementations (the raster-hillshade composites, the contour-line generation via d3-contour-turf from the D3 discipline, the heat-map density surfaces — the kernel-density estimation rendering), and the field-honesty discipline (the interpolation-uncertainty acknowledgment — the estimated-between-samples transparency) as the surface-mapping layer; the flow-and-movement mapping — the relational dynamics: the OD-flow representation (the origin-destination line-and-arc patterns — the width-encoded magnitude flows, the curved-arc aesthetics versus straight-line accuracy), the movement-trajectory rendering (the GPS-track and animation paths — the time-encoded trail rendering, the animated-flow-particle techniques from the advanced-rendering discipline), the network-structure mapping (the transit-and-route-system display conventions) and the flow-clutter management (the aggregation-and-filtering of dense OD matrices, the top-N flow selection) as the movement toolkit; the bivariate-and-multivariate mapping — the multi-encoding layer: the bivariate-choropleth system (the two-variable color-mixture grids — the 3x3-4x4 class matrices with the legend design challenge, the perceptual-interaction caution — the blended-hue readability limits), the multivariate alternatives (the small-multiple map series, the classified-dot-and-symbol combinations, the interactive layer-toggle approaches), and the variable-count restraint (the two-to-three variable practical ceiling for static comprehension — the interactive exploration extension) as the multivariate craft; the cartogram family — the geometry-distorted maps: the area-cartogram concept (the geography-rescaled-by-value transformation — the Dorling circular and contiguous cartogram approaches, the topology-preservation versus recognizability trade-off), the use-case justification (the population-and-electoral-vote equal-visibility problems where area-cartograms correct choropleth dominance), the implementation tooling (the cartogram-generation libraries and preprocessing), and the distortion-disclosure obligation (the base-geography reference provision — the recognizable-shape loss mitigation) as the corrective technique; the legend-and-annotation design — the map-furniture craft: the legend-design patterns (the continuous-gradient versus discrete-class legends matching the classification scheme, the unit-and-source annotation, the interactive-legend filtering integration), the in-map labeling strategy (the direct-label versus legend-lookup decisions, the annotation-callout patterns for highlighted features), and the furniture-completeness audit (the scale-bar, north-arrow-or-orientation-cue, attribution, date, and source-credit requirements) as the communication-completion layer; the map-composition practice — the layout design: the extent-and-scale selection (the framing decision — the data-coverage versus context-geography balance, the inset-map patterns for disconnected territories and detail zooms), the multi-map layouts (the small-multiple map grids for comparison, the before-after and change-detection pairings), and the responsive-map-design (the mobile-legibility — the class-count and label reductions, the touch-target sizing for interactive elements) as the composition craft;
