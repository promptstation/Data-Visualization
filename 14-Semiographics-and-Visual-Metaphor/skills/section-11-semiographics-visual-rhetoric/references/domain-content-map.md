# Domain Content Map — Visual Rhetoric: Persuasion, Argument, and the Ethics of Graphic Influence

Read this during Phase 3 (architecture) and Phase 4 (writing) of the module
development workflow. Verify all factual claims and numbers against the
authoritative sources before publishing (Phase 2 evidence base); flag
anything disputed rather than repeating it.

## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Practice visual rhetoric through foundations (ethos-pathos-logos mapping onto graphics, visual argument concepts with claims-warrants-evidence, audience adaptation, rhetorical figure traditions from metaphor through visual schemes, persuasion research on framing-narrative-uncertainty effects).
2. Build argument architecture (claim-evidence structures with counter-evidence treatment and warrant visualization, framing devices across scale-reference-temporal-aggregation-salience dimensions, genre conventions from advocacy through scientific figures, multimodal and interactive argumentation), master critical literacy (complete distortion toolkit inventory from truncated axes through dual-axis deception.
3. Audit protocols with counterfactual rendering and adversarial review, manipulation-versus-craft boundary reasoning with disclosure standards, platform viral contexts with standalone honesty, defense literacy curricula).
4. Operate the ethical framework (truthfulness-harm-autonomy-justice foundations, professional standards landscape, organizational review-disclosure-correction practice, personal intent examination with reversibility tests and refusal capability), and avoid framing manipulation, pseudo-neutrality, distortion laundering, cherry-picked narratives, decontextualization neglect, ethics afterthoughts, and literacy resignation failures.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the persuasive dimension where visualizations function as arguments and the ethical discipline governing that power: the visual rhetoric foundations — graphics as persuasion: the rhetorical tradition applied to visuals (the classical ethos-pathos-logos triad mapped onto data graphics where ethos travels through craft quality, source transparency, and institutional style, pathos through human-scale imagery, color emotion, and narrative stakes, and logos through evidence structure, statistical validity, and logical visual argumentation, from the rhetorical analysis traditions; the visual argument concept where graphic compositions make claims requiring warrant and backing like verbal arguments, with data marks serving as evidence and annotations as warrant statements; the audience-adaptation principle where rhetorical strategies calibrate to audience values, knowledge, and expectations, extending section 10's cultural analysis into persuasion design); the figure traditions in visual form (the visual analogs of rhetorical figures including metaphor from sections 5 and 6 as the master figure, synecdoche where part-of-data stands for whole-phenomena in selected examples and case studies, hyperbole through scale and emphasis exaggeration, litotes through deliberate understatement, and irony through expectation-subverting juxtapositions, from the visual rhetoric literature; the scheme figures where visual arrangement patterns function like parallelism and antithesis, including small-multiple parallel structures and juxtaposition contrasts; the trope awareness as design literacy where recognizing figures in others' graphics enables both critical reading and deliberate ethical use); the persuasive visualization research (the experiment traditions measuring how design choices shift beliefs, decisions, and risk perception, where identical data supports different conclusions under different framings; the framing effects documentation including reference point selection, aggregation level, and time window choices systematically moving audiences; the narrative persuasion findings where story-embedded data resists counter-arguing more than standalone statistics, connecting to the narrative course traditions; the uncertainty communication research where displaying confidence intervals and probability formats affects trust and decision quality in measurable directions) as the foundations layer; the argument architecture — building graphic cases: the claim-evidence structure (the thesis-driven composition where a central claim organizes supporting graphics in logical sequence, the argument-first design method contrasted with exploration-first presentation; the evidence hierarchy arranging data by probative strength with the strongest support positioned by rhetorical strategy, opening or closing per the serial-position research; the counter-evidence treatment where acknowledging and addressing contrary data builds ethos while omission risks credibility collapse under scrutiny; the warrant visualization where the reasoning connecting evidence to claim is made explicit through annotation, comparison baselines, and methodology disclosure); the framing devices (the scale and axis framing where range selection, baseline choice, and unit selection shape magnitude perception, the classic manipulation surface analyzed critically below; the reference class framing where comparison targets determine whether values read as large or small, from the benchmarking traditions; the temporal framing where start-end point selection, window length, and resolution choices manufacture trends and hide them, connecting to the temporal analysis traditions; the aggregation framing where grouping levels change which patterns exist, the Simpson's paradox surface from the exploratory analysis traditions; the visual salience framing where emphasis systems from section 7 of the narrative traditions direct attention to claim-supporting features); the genre conventions of argument (the advocacy infographic tradition where cause organizations build visual cases with explicit persuasive intent and the transparency norms that keep them honest; the explanatory journalism genre where persuasion serves understanding with verification obligations from the editorial traditions; the business case deck where data graphics support resource requests with the stakeholder-adaptation craft from the executive communication contexts; the scientific figure tradition where persuasion operates through rigor display and the conventions that signal methodological care); and the multimodal argument integration (the text-image argument division where captions, headlines, and body text carry claims while graphics carry evidence, applying section 2's relay function; the sequence arguments where multi-panel and scroll-based orderings build cumulative cases, from the narrative traditions; the interactive argumentation where exploration affordances let audiences verify claims themselves, the persuasion-through-transparency strategy from the interactivity traditions of section 8 in the narrative course) as the architecture layer; the dark patterns and manipulation — the critical literacy: the distortion toolkit inventory (the truncated-axis exaggeration where nonzero baselines multiply apparent differences, the area-volume misrepresentation where pictorial scaling violates psychophysics per section 7, the cherry-picked window and comparison selection where framing devices serve predetermined conclusions, the correlation-causation visual juxtaposition implying causal claims from co-occurrence, the pseudo-precision display where spurious decimal places and exact-looking graphics overstate certainty, the misleading smoothing where trend lines erase relevant volatility, the dual-axis deception where independent scales manufacture correlation, and the selective omission where absent data categories reshape totals and proportions, each documented in the critical visualization literature); the detection and audit methods (the manipulation audit protocol examining every framing device against the alternative choices and their conclusions, extending section 3's construction audit into rhetorical space; the counterfactual rendering test asking whether the graphic's claim survives honest encoding alternatives; the source-completeness check verifying that selection decisions are disclosed and reconstructable; the peer adversarial review where reviewers actively attempt to extract unsupported claims, from the review traditions); the manipulation-versus-legitimate-craft boundary (the emphasis-versus-distortion distinction where directing attention to genuine patterns differs from manufacturing apparent patterns, applying the expressiveness criterion of section 4; the simplification-versus-omission boundary where abstraction for comprehension differs from exclusion of inconvenient data; the disclosure standard where persuasive choices survive publication of method and framing decisions, the transparency test from the ethics traditions; the audience-competence consideration where expert audiences tolerate conventional shorthand that misleads novice audiences, from section 1's literacy concept); the platform and viral manipulation contexts (the social media infographic ecosystem where engagement algorithms reward distortion, from the media literacy traditions; the screenshot decontextualization risk where graphics circulate stripped of anchorage and caveats, demanding standalone-honesty design; the AI-generated persuasive graphics at awareness where synthetic imagery and automated chart generation scale manipulation risks, connecting to the emerging tooling contexts); and the defense literacy (the critical reading curriculum where audiences learn the distortion toolkit, from the data literacy education traditions; the journalist and analyst verification protocols for third-party graphics before republication; the correction and accountability norms where published distortions face retraction and correction obligations from the editorial ethics traditions) as the critical layer; the ethical framework — responsible persuasion: the ethics foundations (the truthfulness obligation where graphics may not assert what data does not support, the expressiveness criterion as ethical minimum from section 4; the harm-minimization principle weighing persuasive impact on vulnerable audiences, financial decisions, health behaviors, and political participation against communication goals, from the applied ethics traditions; the autonomy respect where audiences receive the information and framing transparency needed for independent judgment, the anti-manipulation commitment; the justice dimension asking whose interests graphic persuasion serves and whose data stories get told by whom, extending section 10's representation ethics); the professional standards landscape (the journalism ethics codes covering data and visual accuracy from the press traditions; the statistical graphics integrity standards from the professional statistics associations; the design ethics manifestos and professional codes addressing persuasion responsibility from the design community traditions; the regulatory contexts where financial, medical, and political advertising graphics face legal accuracy requirements from the compliance traditions); the organizational ethics practice (the review and approval workflows where consequential persuasive graphics face ethics review alongside accuracy review, from the editorial process traditions; the disclosure conventions publishing data sources, methods, framing choices, and funding contexts; the correction policies and public accountability mechanisms for published graphics; the team ethics literacy where distortion-toolkit knowledge and boundary reasoning are trained rather than assumed); the personal practice framework (the intent examination asking what the designer wants the audience to believe, feel, and do, and whether that outcome serves the audience's interests alongside the client's; the reversible-test discipline of imagining the graphic republished with framing choices exposed to hostile expert scrutiny; the advocacy transparency where persuasive intent is acknowledged rather than hidden behind neutrality performance; and the refusal capability where professionals recognize and decline projects requiring distortion, supported by portfolio and career planning realities) as the ethics layer; and the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the framing manipulation, deploying scale-window-aggregation choices to manufacture conclusions the full data rejects, remedied by the manipulation audit and counterfactual testing; the pseudo-neutrality performance, hiding persuasive intent behind unmarked advocacy, remedied by the transparency and disclosure standards; the distortion laundering, wrapping manipulated graphics in institutional style to borrow undeserved credibility, remedied by the craft-quality-ethos separation analysis; the cherry-pick narrative, sequencing evidence to support predetermined claims while suppressing contrary data, remedied by the counter-evidence treatment obligations; the viral decontextualization neglect, publishing graphics that mislead once screenshotted without anchorage, remedied by the standalone-honesty design; the ethics-as-afterthought, reviewing persuasion only after publication complaints arrive, remedied by the workflow integration; and the literacy resignation, assuming audiences cannot learn to detect manipulation so detection is pointless, remedied by the defense literacy and correction norms with detection methods as the diagnostic.
