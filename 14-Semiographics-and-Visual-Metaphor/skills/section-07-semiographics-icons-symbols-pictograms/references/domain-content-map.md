# Domain Content Map — Icons, Symbols, and Pictograms: Designing the Visual Vocabulary

Read this during Phase 3 (architecture) and Phase 4 (writing) of the module

authoritative sources before publishing (Phase 2 evidence base); flag

## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Design visual vocabulary through the Isotype and pictogram tradition (unit-reduction-repetition principles with workshop method legacy, Olympic-DOT systematic construction programs, effectiveness research on concreteness-familiarity-unit-chart perception), master icon construction craft (silhouette tests with grid-keyline systems and detail budgets, style system decisions across outline-filled-duotone families, optical correction for sizing-overshoot-weight, semantic clarity engineering against ambiguity with affordance signaling), architect icon systems at scale (inventory-coverage-priority methodology, consistency DNA with family relationships and state variants, governance with contribution standards-versioning-documentation, licensing landscape navigation).
2. Apply data-specific icons (unit chart conventions with array arrangements and scaling limits.
3. Map symbol systems with legend contracts and density management, dashboard status-trend-metric-alert iconography with interactive states, annotation and narrative pictograms), and avoid ad hoc accumulation, silhouette failure, optical sloppiness, ambiguous glyphs, array overflow, stereotype import, and unlicensed borrowing failures.

the governance and maintenance (the contribution standards where new icons pass construction-rule review before library admission, from the design-system governance traditions; the deprecation and migration management retiring ambiguous or inconsistent icons with replacement mapping; the versioning and distribution through icon fonts, sprite systems, and component libraries with their rendering tradeoffs, from the front-end traditions; and the usage documentation showing correct and incorrect applications, sizing rules, clear-space requirements, and color treatments); the licensing and sourcing landscape (the open-source icon ecosystems and their license obligations; the commercial library evaluation for coverage, consistency, and style fit; the custom-versus-sourced decision framework weighing brand distinctiveness against cost and coverage;
