# Metaphor Theory and Cognition: How Metaphor Structures Thought and Graphics — Professional Training

## Mission

Produce professional-grade learning modules and training materials on **metaphor Theory and Cognition: How Metaphor Structures Thought and Graphics** within The meaning-making layer of the data visualization specialist discipline — semiotics, Bertin's graphical semiology, and metaphor theory mastered as design instruments: the science of how marks, icons, colors, and visual figures carry meaning, and the craft of building coherent visual languages that communicate instantly and survive cultural contact.

Subject scope: Covers the cognitive science of metaphor that grounds visual metaphor design in how human understanding actually works: the conceptual metaphor revolution — Lakoff and Johnson: the metaphor-we-live-by thesis (the 1980 finding that metaphor pervades ordinary thought and language rather than decorating poetry, where everyday reasoning runs on structures like argument-is-war, time-is-money, and ideas-are-food; the conceptual mapping definition where understanding one domain, the target, in terms of another, the source, with systematic correspondences between their elements; the entailment principle where each metaphor licenses inferences that shape what we notice and ignore about the target); the primary metaphor foundations (the embodied origins of basic mappings from sensorimotor experience, where more-is-up arises from observing piles grow upward, affection-is-warmth from being held, and important-is-big from interacting with larger things, from the Grady primary-metaphor analysis; the cross-cultural partial-universality of primary metaphors grounded in shared embodiment versus cultural variation in complex metaphors built from them, previewing section 10; the natural-mapping advantage in interface design where primary-metaphor-aligned controls require less learning, connecting to section 1's motivated-sign concept); the orientational and ontological categories (the orientational metaphors organizing concepts spatially, where up-down, in-out, and front-back structures carry value judgments such as happy-is-up and control-is-up; the ontological metaphors treating abstractions as entities and substances, where inflation-is-an-entity permits measuring, causation, and action upon it, the cognitive license behind every gauge, container, and quantity graphic; the container schema specifically, where bounded regions with interiors and exteriors structure understanding of categories, states, and groups, explaining the intuitive power of treemaps, Venn diagrams, and enclosed regions); and the metaphor critique capacity (the hiding and highlighting mechanism where every metaphor illuminates some aspects of the target while concealing others, as time-is-money hides gift economies and argument-is-war hides cooperative inquiry; the design consequence that choosing a visual metaphor chooses what the audience can and cannot think about the data, an ethical and rhetorical responsibility previewing sections 6 and 11) as the conceptual layer; the conceptual blending theory — Fauconnier and Turner: the mental spaces framework (the input spaces as partial structures brought together in understanding, the generic space holding their shared skeleton, and the blended space where the inputs combine with emergent structure not present in either input, from the way-we-think tradition; the running-the-blend dynamics where the mind manipulates the blend forward and backward, simulating scenarios and drawing inferences, the mechanism behind interactive visualization exploration); the application to visual metaphor (the diagram as blend where data structure and visual source domain combine, producing emergent readings that neither the raw numbers nor the source image carries alone; the double-scope integration where both inputs contribute topology, as in flow diagrams blending water hydraulics with process logic; the blend quality criteria of the theory including good external relations to inputs, no clashes between input logics, and useful emergent structure, providing evaluation principles for visual metaphor design); the material anchors and cognitive role (the anchor concept where physical artifacts such as drawings and diagrams serve as stable external structures enabling repeated blend construction, making visualizations literally extended cognition; the compression patterns that make blends humanly tractable, where the blend compresses relations of time, identity, cause, and change into manageable form, explaining why small well-designed graphics convey what tables cannot); and the blend failure modes (the clash condition where source and target logics contradict inside the blend, producing confusion rather than insight; the over-compression errors where blends hide important structure by over-simplifying; and the runaway-emergence risk where blend-generated inferences outstrip data support, the cognitive root of chart over-interpretation) as the blending layer; the visual metaphor research — the design evidence: the visual metaphor definitions and types (the juxtaposition, fusion, and replacement classifications from the visual-communication research tradition, where elements combine pictorially rather than verbally; the simile-metaphor continuum in visual form where explicit comparison markers soften or absence hardens the identification claim; the concrete-abstract mapping direction where visual sources are overwhelmingly concrete domains representing abstract data targets, following the embodiment logic); the effectiveness findings (the comprehension and memory research where moderate visual metaphors improve recall and engagement relative to literal presentation, with the inverted-U pattern where obscure or overly complex metaphors degrade understanding; the expertise and familiarity moderators where metaphor benefits depend on source-domain familiarity and audience knowledge, connecting to section 1's competence concept; the affect and persuasion findings where metaphorical framing influences attitudes and emotional response, the power that demands the ethical discipline of section 11); the domain application studies (the business communication research on metaphor in reports and presentations, the science communication studies of metaphor in explaining complex systems, and the data visualization specific experiments comparing literal charts with metaphorical infographics on accuracy and engagement tradeoffs; the accuracy-engagement tension where decorative metaphor sources can cost quantitative precision, the core tradeoff of infographic design analyzed in section 6); and the design guidance synthesis (the source-domain selection criteria of familiarity, structural fit, and connotation appropriateness; the mapping completeness principle where systematic correspondence beats arbitrary decoration, from the conceptual mapping logic; and the anchorage requirements where text and context prevent metaphor misfires, from the Barthes anchorage tradition of section 2) as the evidence layer; the metaphor families in data representation — the inherited structures: the spatial and container family (the data-as-space mappings underlying scatter plots as landscapes, density as terrain, and clusters as regions; the container metaphors of boxes, bins, and buckets in box plots, histograms, and treemaps, each importing part-whole and boundary inferences; the layer and depth metaphors of stacked charts and z-ordering with their hidden-item implications); the physical and substance family (the liquid metaphors of flows, fills, and streams in Sankey diagrams and streamgraphs, importing conservation and volume intuitions; the force and physics metaphors of spring layouts, gravity, and collision in force-directed graphs, importing energy and equilibrium reasoning; the temperature and heat metaphors of heatmaps and hotspots, importing intensity and danger connotations); the journey and narrative family (the path metaphors of trajectories, funnels, and timelines importing progress, destination, and obstacle reasoning; the growth metaphors of trees, roots, and branches in hierarchy displays importing organic development; the race and competition metaphors of rankings and race charts importing winner-loser framing with its ideological entailments previewing section 11); the body and face family (the anthropomorphic mappings of pictograms, unit charts as people, and face-basedglyphs like Chernoff faces importing human-scale empathy and pattern recognition; the health and disease metaphors of dashboards as vital signs, importing diagnosis and monitoring frames from the medical tradition); and the family analysis discipline (the inherited-metaphor audit where designers identify which metaphor families their standard charts already run on, revealing that literal-looking charts are frozen metaphors; the entailment examination asking what each inherited family licenses and hides about the data at hand) as the families layer; the metaphor design reasoning — the operational method: the source selection procedure (the structural fit analysis comparing relational patterns between candidate source and data target, following the systematicity principle from conceptual metaphor theory; the audience familiarity assessment for the source domain across the intended readership from the effectiveness research; the connotation screening of candidate sources for unwanted cultural, emotional, and ideological associations, connecting to section 9's color and section 10's cultural analysis); the mapping construction (the element correspondence table listing target entities, relations, and quantities against source counterparts, applying the blend construction logic; the entailment inventory documenting which source-licensed inferences the design intends, tolerates, and must block; the gap identification where target structure has no source counterpart, requiring invention, hybridization, or anchorage); the clash and failure prevention (the logic consistency check ensuring source and target rules do not contradict within the blend, from the blend failure modes; the emergence audit examining what readings the blend invites and whether data supports them, preventing runaway inference; the fallback comprehension design providing literal alternatives where metaphor might fail, from the progressive disclosure traditions); and the evaluation integration (the metaphor comprehension testing with target audiences measuring intended versus actual readings, from the research methods; the accuracy-versus-engagement measurement tracking whether the metaphor costs quantitative precision, from the tradeoff literature; the iterative refinement cycles where mapping tables and test results drive source adjustment or anchorage strengthening) as the method layer; and the section anti-patterns — the failure library: the decorative-metaphor fallacy, adding visual source imagery with no systematic mapping to the data, remedied by the mapping construction discipline; the clash-blind design, combining source and target logics that contradict inside the blend, remedied by the consistency check; the entailment negligence, shipping metaphors whose hidden inferences mislead without audit, remedied by the hiding-highlighting analysis; the obscure-source selection, choosing sources unfamiliar to the audience so the blend cannot be built, remedied by the familiarity assessment; the runaway-emergence acceptance, letting blend-invited readings exceed data support unexamined, remedied by the emergence audit; the frozen-metaphor amnesia, forgetting that standard charts are metaphors with entailments, remedied by the inherited-metaphor audit; and the metaphor-maximalism, layering multiple competing metaphors in one display until blends collapse, remedied by the single-source discipline and blend quality criteria with detection methods as the diagnostic.

Write as an experienced practitioner, not as a summarizer of popular content. Every framework taught must be something a real team or professional could run: procedures they can execute, criteria they can judge with, and artifacts they can hand to a colleague. Do not present claims as settled when the field treats them as contested — the training must model evidence discipline.

## Writing Standards

Throughout the material, prioritize language that is:

* Precise without becoming jargon-heavy
* Practical without discarding rigor
* Honest about limitations and contested findings without being defeatist
* Concrete — anchored in real cases, real artifacts, and verifiable numbers
* Progressive from fundamentals to expert judgment

Where a recommendation depends on context, explain the trade-off rather than presenting an absolute rule.

## Anti-Patterns

Avoid: Private symbolism — inventing visual metaphors that make sense only to their designer, shipping graphic languages readers must decode by trial, legend, and guesswork; Cultural blindness — assuming colors, gestures, icons, and metaphors carry universal meaning, then discovering red means mourning in one audience and danger in another; Decoration creep — layering visual richness until metaphor and ornament compete with data, reviving the chartjunk failures the Tufte tradition documented; Jargon without craft — reciting signifier-signified-icon-index-symbol vocabulary in design reviews while producing no better encodings than intuition alone; Mixed-metaphor systems — combining incompatible visual languages within one product (liquid fills beside bar stacks beside physical gauges) so readers relearn semantics per chart; exercises without evaluation criteria; modules that stop at inspiration without a single decision the learner can now make better; and any content that overstates certainty beyond what the evidence supports.

## Decision Heuristic

When allocating depth under time or length limits, ask:

1. Where will this audience actually stall in practice?
2. Which unit protects the most value if taught well?
3. Can the learner run the framework tomorrow without me?
4. Is every example doing work a plain sentence could not?
5. What would a skeptical domain expert say about this material?

If two topics compete for space, keep the one that changes a decision.
