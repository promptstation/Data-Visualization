## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the modeling-and-prediction layer: the computational-saliency tradition — the engineering lineage: the Itti-Koch bottom-up saliency model (the multi-scale feature-map extraction, the center-surround differencing, the map normalization and combination into the saliency map, the winner-take-all attention shifting) as the canonical architecture translating the section-2 neuroscience into implementable prediction; the feature-map mechanics — the model internals: the channel decomposition (the intensity, orientation, color implementations mirroring the biological channels, the motion extension for video), the across-scale differencing (the center-surround pyramids computing local contrast at multiple resolutions — the spatial-frequency structure from section 2 operationalized), and the normalization politics (the per-map scaling determining channel weights — the combination-rule sensitivity) as the model anatomy; the bottom-up-only limitation — the scope boundary: the pure-salience models predicting fixation-attractiveness of images without task context (the documented predictive validity for free-viewing — the ROC and NSS metric family, the human-fixation-agreement benchmarks) versus the task-blindness failure (the same image's salience map useless for predicting where a analyst looking for an anomaly will fixate — the guided-search top-down gap) making bottom-up models the attention-floor predictor not the full model; the task-aware extensions — the hybrid models: the relevance-weighted saliency approaches (the task-feature up-weighting — the Wolfe-guided-search computational implementations, the conditional-saliency frameworks), the deep-learning saliency generation (the convolutional and transformer saliency predictors trained on fixation datasets — the documented benchmark improvements and the interpretability opacity), and the visualization-specific models (the chart-saliency research — the models trained on dashboard and chart eye-tracking corpora predicting analyst attention) as the prediction-state-of-art; the practical prediction tooling — the applied layer: the saliency-prediction services and libraries (the available attention-heatmap tools for designs and web pages, the design-tool plugins rendering predicted-salience overlays) with the use-discipline (predictions as audit heuristics — does the intended emphasis region dominate the predicted map? — not as ground truth, the model-error awareness); the salience-competition analysis — the diagnostic application: using models to find unintended competitors (the chrome elements, logos, and images out-salienting the data — the reversed-hierarchy detection, the accidental-salience hotspots from clustering artifacts) as the automated composition audit complementing the course-1 manual squint protocols; the map-based design iteration — the workflow integration: the design-predict-compare-adjust loop (rendering the saliency prediction for candidate layouts, comparing against the intended attention hierarchy, adjusting channel weights — the dim-saturate-resize interventions — and re-predicting) as the rapid pre-user-test screening reducing expensive participant studies to genuine ambiguities; the graph-saliency research frontier — the domain science: the documented visualization-perception research on saliency in charts (the mark-type salience differences, the legend-and-annotation capture costs, the density effects on predicted versus actual attention) as the emerging evidence base with the transfer-caution (most saliency research uses natural photographs — the chart-domain gap requiring local validation); the normative-versus-predictive distinction — the philosophy layer: saliency models describing where eyes go (prediction) versus design theory prescribing where eyes should go (the decision-relevance hierarchy — the normative salience design: making the decision-critical element the salience winner even against the model's natural bias), with the reconciliation practice (when data-important and salience-natural diverge — reposition, re-encode, or accept the guided-search top-down support through task framing and annotation); the ethics-of-prediction layer — the manipulation boundary: the attention-prediction power in persuasive contexts (the advertising and feed-design saliency optimization — the engagement-engineering adjacency) with the analyst-honesty standard (in data visualization, salience serves finding truth in the data, not steering conclusions — the emphasis-integrity principle connecting to the course-1 honesty boundary); the model-limitations summary — the calibration knowledge: the documented failure modes of saliency prediction (the semantic-blindness — meaningful elements not predicted when visually quiet, the text-content ignorance — most models not reading labels, the individual-variance erasure — population-average predictions) as the humility checklist bounding model trust; and the computational-salience deliverable — the prediction-augmented composition audit and iteration workflow with normative-design discipline.
