# Salience Maps and Computational Models: Predicting Where Eyes Go — Professional Training

## Mission

Produce professional-grade learning modules and training materials on **salience Maps and Computational Models: Predicting Where Eyes Go** within The science of visual attributes (color hue, size, orientation, motion) processed in milliseconds before conscious attention — engineered to direct the viewer's eye instantly in data visualization.

Subject scope: Covers the modeling-and-prediction layer: the computational-saliency tradition — the engineering lineage: the Itti-Koch bottom-up saliency model (the multi-scale feature-map extraction, the center-surround differencing, the map normalization and combination into the saliency map, the winner-take-all attention shifting) as the canonical architecture translating the section-2 neuroscience into implementable prediction; the feature-map mechanics — the model internals: the channel decomposition (the intensity, orientation, color implementations mirroring the biological channels, the motion extension for video), the across-scale differencing (the center-surround pyramids computing local contrast at multiple resolutions — the spatial-frequency structure from section 2 operationalized), and the normalization politics (the per-map scaling determining channel weights — the combination-rule sensitivity) as the model anatomy; the bottom-up-only limitation — the scope boundary: the pure-salience models predicting fixation-attractiveness of images without task context (the documented predictive validity for free-viewing — the ROC and NSS metric family, the human-fixation-agreement benchmarks) versus the task-blindness failure (the same image's salience map useless for predicting where a analyst looking for an anomaly will fixate — the guided-search top-down gap) making bottom-up models the attention-floor predictor not the full model; the task-aware extensions — the hybrid models: the relevance-weighted saliency approaches (the task-feature up-weighting — the Wolfe-guided-search computational implementations, the conditional-saliency frameworks), the deep-learning saliency generation (the convolutional and transformer saliency predictors trained on fixation datasets — the documented benchmark improvements and the interpretability opacity), and the visualization-specific models (the chart-saliency research — the models trained on dashboard and chart eye-tracking corpora predicting analyst attention) as the prediction-state-of-art; the practical prediction tooling — the applied layer: the saliency-prediction services and libraries (the available attention-heatmap tools for designs and web pages, the design-tool plugins rendering predicted-salience overlays) with the use-discipline (predictions as audit heuristics — does the intended emphasis region dominate the predicted map? — not as ground truth, the model-error awareness); the salience-competition analysis — the diagnostic application: using models to find unintended competitors (the chrome elements, logos, and images out-salienting the data — the reversed-hierarchy detection, the accidental-salience hotspots from clustering artifacts) as the automated composition audit complementing the course-1 manual squint protocols; the map-based design iteration — the workflow integration: the design-predict-compare-adjust loop (rendering the saliency prediction for candidate layouts, comparing against the intended attention hierarchy, adjusting channel weights — the dim-saturate-resize interventions — and re-predicting) as the rapid pre-user-test screening reducing expensive participant studies to genuine ambiguities; the graph-saliency research frontier — the domain science: the documented visualization-perception research on saliency in charts (the mark-type salience differences, the legend-and-annotation capture costs, the density effects on predicted versus actual attention) as the emerging evidence base with the transfer-caution (most saliency research uses natural photographs — the chart-domain gap requiring local validation); the normative-versus-predictive distinction — the philosophy layer: saliency models describing where eyes go (prediction) versus design theory prescribing where eyes should go (the decision-relevance hierarchy — the normative salience design: making the decision-critical element the salience winner even against the model's natural bias), with the reconciliation practice (when data-important and salience-natural diverge — reposition, re-encode, or accept the guided-search top-down support through task framing and annotation); the ethics-of-prediction layer — the manipulation boundary: the attention-prediction power in persuasive contexts (the advertising and feed-design saliency optimization — the engagement-engineering adjacency) with the analyst-honesty standard (in data visualization, salience serves finding truth in the data, not steering conclusions — the emphasis-integrity principle connecting to the course-1 honesty boundary); the model-limitations summary — the calibration knowledge: the documented failure modes of saliency prediction (the semantic-blindness — meaningful elements not predicted when visually quiet, the text-content ignorance — most models not reading labels, the individual-variance erasure — population-average predictions) as the humility checklist bounding model trust; and the computational-salience deliverable — the prediction-augmented composition audit and iteration workflow with normative-design discipline.

Write as an experienced practitioner, not as a summarizer of popular content. ## Anti-Patterns

Avoid: Salience inflation — highlighting everything until nothing pops; preattentive power is relative and budget-limited, diluted by overuse (the cry-wolf effect on the salience map); Confusing salience with meaning — a popped element directs the eye but carries no interpretation; preattentive capture without deliberate-support structure leaves viewers looking at the right place without understanding; Conjunction blindness — expecting combinations of features (red AND large AND tilted) to pop when only single features do, producing serial-search tasks mislabeled as glances; Habituation neglect — persistent highlights and badges losing capture power through neural adaptation, the alert-fatigue dynamic killing always-on emphasis; Accessibility-assumed pop — preattentive effects varying with color vision (hue channels degraded for CVD viewers) and age, single-channel salience failing for part of the audience; exercises without evaluation criteria; modules that stop at inspiration without a single decision the learner can now make better; and any content that overstates certainty beyond what the evidence supports.

## Decision Heuristic

When allocating depth under time or length limits, ask:

1. Where will this audience actually stall in practice?
2. Which unit protects the most value if taught well?
3. Can the learner run the framework tomorrow without me?
4. Is every example doing work a plain sentence could not?
5. What would a skeptical domain expert say about this material?

If two topics compete for space, keep the one that changes a decision.
