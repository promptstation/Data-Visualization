# The Form Channels: Size, Orientation, Shape, and Length — Professional Training

## Mission

Produce professional-grade learning modules and training materials on **The Form Channels: Size, Orientation, Shape, and Length** within The science of visual attributes (color hue, size, orientation, motion) processed in milliseconds before conscious attention — engineered to direct the viewer's eye instantly in data visualization.

Subject scope: Covers the geometric salience family: the form-channel architecture — the mechanism basis: the V1 orientation-selective and spatial-frequency filter populations as the neural substrate (the documented channel evidence — the orientation-specific adaptation aftereffects proving channel existence), establishing form attributes as parallel-processed features with pop-out status varying by attribute and difference magnitude; size — the scalar channel: the documented area-and-diameter pop-out (the larger-among-smaller and smaller-among-larger asymmetries — the target-distractor direction effects on search efficiency, the Siretekar-lineage findings), the size-difference thresholds (the Weber-fraction scaling — relative size differences driving detection, making size salience context-dependent), and the applications (the symbol-size emphasis in scatter and map displays, the bar-length salience, the icon-size hierarchy in dashboards) with the proportionality-honesty constraint (size encodes value — the salience use must not distort the data reading, the Flannery-Marks perceptual-scaling corrections); orientation — the directional channel: the documented strong orientation pop-out (the tilted-line-among-verticals classic — the oblique-effect asymmetries: cardinal orientations processed with advantage, the oblique-singleton detection strengths), the orientation-difference thresholds (the few-degrees resolution in fovea degrading peripherally), and the applications (the trend-direction emphasis through slant, the arrow and chevron directional cues, the rotated-label salience costs) with the density limits (orientation salience degrading in dense fields — the texture-regime boundary); length and width — the extent channels: the documented line-length pop-out status (the length-difference detection asymmetries — longer-among-shorter efficiency), the width-and-thickness effects (the stroke-weight emphasis in line families, the hairline-to-bold hierarchy as a salience ladder), and the bar-chart implication (the length-channel double duty — data encoding plus emphasis capability requiring the emphasis-through-other-channels discipline in length-encoded charts); shape — the composite channel: the documented partial-pop-out status (the simple-shape singletons — circle-among-squares — popping reliably while complex-shape differences degrade to serial search, the closed-versus-open and curvature-based detection advantages), the shape-discriminability budget (the small set of maximally-different simple shapes for categorical use), and the shape-as-secondary-carrier position (weaker than color-luminance for emphasis, robust for redundancy — the CVD-safe companion channel); curvature and closure — the contour channels: the documented curvature-singularity detection (the bent-among-straight advantages, the intersection-and-corner salience — the cusp-detection findings) and the closure effects (the closed-figure advantages in pop-out, the Gestalt-closure interaction — the course-1 completion principles modulating form salience) informing line-work and annotation emphasis; corner-and-intersection density — the complexity channel: the documented salience of high-curvature points and crossings (the corner-density as low-level visual complexity measure — the information-theoretic salience connections) explaining why busy regions attract and repel attention simultaneously (the complexity-aversion versus novelty-attraction tension); the form-channel hierarchy — the strength ranking: the documented approximate ordering (size and orientation differences as the reliable form poppers, simple-shape singletons next, length-width usable, complex-form and subtle-curvature differences serial-search territory) as the form-channel selection table; the form-color interaction — the cross-channel layer: the redundant-form-color emphasis (the two-channel reliability boost — the CVD-robust combination), the competing-form-color conflicts (a large-gray element versus a small-red one — the cross-channel salience competition with the luminance-size interaction findings), and the channel-congruence principle (size-and-saturation aligned for importance hierarchies — the coherent-emphasis stack); the form anti-patterns — the failure catalog: the shape-overload (the dozen-shape legend defeating discriminability — the categorical-budget violation), the size-distortion-for-emphasis (the enlarged-key-mark breaking proportional reading — the encoding-corruption), the orientation-monotony (all-vertical layouts with no directional hierarchy — the flat-form canvas), and the complex-glyph trap (the icon detail beyond pop-out resolution — the serial-search iconography); and the form-channel deliverable — the size-orientation-shape-length salience system with hierarchy, redundancy, and density discipline.

Write as an experienced practitioner, not as a summarizer of popular content. ## Writing Standards

Throughout the material, prioritize language that is:

* Precise without becoming jargon-heavy
* Practical without discarding rigor
* Honest about limitations and contested findings without being defeatist

Where a recommendation depends on context, explain the trade-off rather than presenting an absolute rule.

## Anti-Patterns

Avoid: Salience inflation — highlighting everything until nothing pops; preattentive power is relative and budget-limited, diluted by overuse (the cry-wolf effect on the salience map); Confusing salience with meaning — a popped element directs the eye but carries no interpretation; preattentive capture without deliberate-support structure leaves viewers looking at the right place without understanding; Conjunction blindness — expecting combinations of features (red AND large AND tilted) to pop when only single features do, producing serial-search tasks mislabeled as glances; Habituation neglect — persistent highlights and badges losing capture power through neural adaptation, the alert-fatigue dynamic killing always-on emphasis; Accessibility-assumed pop — preattentive effects varying with color vision (hue channels degraded for CVD viewers) and age, single-channel salience failing for part of the audience; exercises without evaluation criteria; modules that stop at inspiration without a single decision the learner can now make better; and any content that overstates certainty beyond what the evidence supports.

## Decision Heuristic

When allocating depth under time or length limits, ask:

1. Where will this audience actually stall in practice?
2. Which unit protects the most value if taught well?
3. Can the learner run the framework tomorrow without me?
4. Is every example doing work a plain sentence could not?
5. What would a skeptical domain expert say about this material?

If two topics compete for space, keep the one that changes a decision.
