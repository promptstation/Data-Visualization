# Domain Content Map — The Form Channels: Size, Orientation, Shape, and Length

## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Engineer form salience through the filter-channel architecture with documented pop-out asymmetries (larger-among-smaller, tilted-among-verticals, simple-shape singletons), deploy size emphasis with proportional-honesty constraints and perceptual-scaling corrections.
2. Operate the form-channel strength ranking selecting reliable channels (size, orientation, simple shape) over serial-search territory (complex forms, subtle curvature).
3. Combine form-color channels for CVD-robust redundancy with congruence discipline.
4. Manage density, peripheral-acuity, and screen-scale limits preventing shape-overload, size-distortion, and complex-glyph failures.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the geometric salience family: the form-channel architecture — the mechanism basis: the V1 orientation-selective and spatial-frequency filter populations as the neural substrate (the documented channel evidence — the orientation-specific adaptation aftereffects proving channel existence), establishing form attributes as parallel-processed features with pop-out status varying by attribute and difference magnitude; size — the scalar channel: the documented area-and-diameter pop-out (the larger-among-smaller and smaller-among-larger asymmetries — the target-distractor direction effects on search efficiency, the Siretekar-lineage findings), the size-difference thresholds (the Weber-fraction scaling — relative size differences driving detection, making size salience context-dependent), and the applications (the symbol-size emphasis in scatter and map displays, the bar-length salience, the icon-size hierarchy in dashboards) with the proportionality-honesty constraint (size encodes value — the salience use must not distort the data reading, the Flannery-Marks perceptual-scaling corrections); orientation — the directional channel: the documented strong orientation pop-out (the tilted-line-among-verticals classic — the oblique-effect asymmetries: cardinal orientations processed with advantage, the oblique-singleton detection strengths), the orientation-difference thresholds (the few-degrees resolution in fovea degrading peripherally), and the applications (the trend-direction emphasis through slant, the arrow and chevron directional cues, the rotated-label salience costs) with the density limits (orientation salience degrading in dense fields — the texture-regime boundary); length and width — the extent channels: the documented line-length pop-out status (the length-difference detection asymmetries — longer-among-shorter efficiency), the width-and-thickness effects (the stroke-weight emphasis in line families, the hairline-to-bold hierarchy as a salience ladder), and the bar-chart implication (the length-channel double duty — data encoding plus emphasis capability requiring the emphasis-through-other-channels discipline in length-encoded charts); shape — the composite channel: the documented partial-pop-out status (the simple-shape singletons — circle-among-squares — popping reliably while complex-shape differences degrade to serial search, the closed-versus-open and curvature-based detection advantages), the shape-discriminability budget (the small set of maximally-different simple shapes for categorical use), and the shape-as-secondary-carrier position (weaker than color-luminance for emphasis, robust for redundancy — the CVD-safe companion channel); curvature and closure — the contour channels: the documented curvature-singularity detection (the bent-among-straight advantages, the intersection-and-corner salience — the cusp-detection findings) and the closure effects (the closed-figure advantages in pop-out, the Gestalt-closure interaction — the course-1 completion principles modulating form salience) informing line-work and annotation emphasis; corner-and-intersection density — the complexity channel: the documented salience of high-curvature points and crossings (the corner-density as low-level visual complexity measure — the information-theoretic salience connections) explaining why busy regions attract and repel attention simultaneously (the complexity-aversion versus novelty-attraction tension); the form-channel hierarchy — the strength ranking: the documented approximate ordering (size and orientation differences as the reliable form poppers, simple-shape singletons next, length-width usable, complex-form and subtle-curvature differences serial-search territory) as the form-channel selection table; the form-color interaction — the cross-channel layer: the redundant-form-color emphasis (the two-channel reliability boost — the CVD-robust combination), the competing-form-color conflicts (a large-gray element versus a small-red one — the cross-channel salience competition with the luminance-size interaction findings), and the channel-congruence principle (size-and-saturation aligned for importance hierarchies — the coherent-emphasis stack); the scale-and-density limits — the operating envelope: the form-salience degradation with mark density (the overplotting destruction of size and orientation channels — the course-1 aggregation remedies as salience recovery), the peripheral-vision form-resolution falloff (the acuity gradient making form channels fovea-dependent while color and motion retain peripheral reach — the channel-geometry map), and the display-size interactions (the small-screen form-salience compression) as the boundary conditions; the form anti-patterns — the failure catalog: the shape-overload (the dozen-shape legend defeating discriminability — the categorical-budget violation), the size-distortion-for-emphasis (the enlarged-key-mark breaking proportional reading — the encoding-corruption), the orientation-monotony (all-vertical layouts with no directional hierarchy — the flat-form canvas), and the complex-glyph trap (the icon detail beyond pop-out resolution — the serial-search iconography); and the form-channel deliverable — the size-orientation-shape-length salience system with hierarchy, redundancy, and density discipline.
