## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Engineer color salience through the opponent-channel architecture with the documented reliability hierarchy (luminance ≥ hue > saturation), deploy the saturated-focus-desaturated-context and monochrome-plus-one-hue patterns on neutral canvases with dark-mode recalibration.

3. Validate color emphasis through search-task benchmarks, A/B testing, and telemetry while preventing rainbow-default, highlight-inflation, and washout failures.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the strongest salience family: the color-opponent architecture — the channel basis: the documented opponent-process organization (the red-green and blue-yellow chromatic channels plus the achromatic luminance channel — the retinal-ganglion and LGN opponency, the Hering lineage confirmed physiologically), establishing color as three semi-independent preattentive systems with different strengths and failure modes (the architecture explaining both color's power and the CVD vulnerabilities); lightness (luminance) — the achromatic workhorse: the documented strongest-and-most-robust color channel (luminance contrast driving edge detection, figure-ground, and pop-out with the highest reliability across viewers — the CVD-immunity property), the contrast-ratio mechanics (the Weber-fraction and luminance-ratio sensitivity, the WCAG contrast thresholds as the accessibility codification — the course-9 integration), and the applications (the emphasis-through-darkening-lightening, the dimming-backgrounds technique, the monochrome-plus-one-hue pattern as the highest-reliability salience design); hue — the categorical-signaling channel: the documented hue pop-out (the unique-hue singletons — red among green-gray fields — with the hue-difference magnitude effects: larger hue distances popping faster, the categorical-perception findings — across-category hue differences detected faster than within-category equal-distances), the hue-count limits (the discriminable-hue budget for categorical sets — the working-memory and channel-crosstalk constraints), and the hue-semantics collision (the red-green and cultural-meaning overlays — the traffic-light conventions competing with data encoding, the double-duty problem); saturation/chroma — the intensity channel: the documented weaker-but-useful status (the saturation singletons popping less reliably than hue or luminance but serving the emphasis-vividness role — the saturated-focus-desaturated-context technique as the workhorse highlighting pattern), the saturation-lightness interaction (the perceptual independence failures — chroma changes reading as lightness changes at extremes), and the pastel-versus-vivid family semantics (the saturation gradient as importance hierarchy); the color-channel hierarchy — the strength ranking: the documented approximate ordering for pop-out reliability (luminance contrast ≥ hue difference > saturation difference, with motion and orientation competing at the top for temporal and form contexts) as the channel-selection decision table for emphasis design; color-combination effects — the interaction layer: the simultaneous-contrast dynamics (surrounding colors shifting perceived color — the salience-context dependence), the color-bleeding and assimilation effects at small scales (the thin-line and small-mark color perception shifts — the optical-mixing regime where preattentive color fails), and the dense-color-field behaviors (the texture-like reading of high-density color variation — the point where color stops being per-element signal) as the scale-and-context limits; the background-canvas discipline — the relativity application: the neutral-background requirement (the quiet-canvas principle from section 2 specialized: chromatic backgrounds competing with data colors for channel capacity, the white-and-gray ground conventions as channel-preservation), the brand-color accommodation strategies (the brand palette as chrome layer separated from data-encoding palette — the two-palette architecture), and the dark-mode specifics (the luminance-channel inversion effects, the saturated-color bloom on dark grounds — the dark-theme salience recalibration); the temporal-color dynamics — the change dimension: the color-change detection advantages (the documented onset-and-change capture — color transitions popping harder than static color, the change-blindness mitigation through color-transients), the flash-and-pulse conventions (the alert-color animation patterns with the seizure-safety limits — the WCAG flash thresholds, the course-9 obligation), and the persistence-decay (the color-highlight fade strategies preventing habituation — the section-12 preview); CVD and color-salience equity — the reliability layer: the CVD population prevalence and channel losses (the deuteranopia-protanopia red-green channel collapse, the tritanopia rarity, the luminance-channel preservation) with the design consequences (hue-only salience failing for a substantial audience — the redundant-carrier mandate: color emphasis paired with luminance, position, or form differences), and the CVD-simulation verification workflow; the measurement practice — the color-salience validation: the search-task protocols for color emphasis (the detection-time benchmarks for candidate palettes — the section-7 method preview), the A/B highlight testing (the color-variant comparison on real dashboards), and the production telemetry (the click-and-hover response to color alerts as the revealed-salience data) as the evidence loop; the color-salience anti-patterns — the failure catalog: the rainbow-default error (the categorical palette applied to continuous data destroying the lightness channel — the jet-colormap critique as salience science), the highlight-inflation (multiple loud colors competing into mutual cancellation — the salience-budget violation), the semantic-collision (red for 'high value' where audiences read 'danger' — the convention-conflict cost), and the dark-mode-unchecked (the screen-designed salience failing in bright control-room environments — the ambient-light contrast washout); and the color-channel deliverable — the luminance-hue-saturation salience system with reliability ranking, redundancy, and validation.
