# Synthesis and Capstone: The Salience-Engineered Display System — Professional Training

## Mission

Produce professional-grade learning modules and training materials on **synthesis and Capstone: The Salience-Engineered Display System** within The science of visual attributes (color hue, size, orientation, motion) processed in milliseconds before conscious attention — engineered to direct the viewer's eye instantly in data visualization.

Subject scope: Prepares expert integration: the consolidated framework — the millisecond-visual-system foundations (the two-stage architecture, the parallel-channel processing), the mechanism model (feature maps, salience maps, guided search, contrast relativity), the channel families with strength hierarchies (luminance-hue-saturation color, size-orientation-shape-length form, motion-onset-flicker temporal, position-depth-enclosure-whitespace spatial), the measurement science (search slopes, singleton thresholds, signal detection, gaze metrics), the top-down determinants (task sets, expertise, expectation, vigilance), the application systems (dashboard emphasis tiers, alert design, glanceability, data-encoding channel selection), the limits and hygiene (conjunctions, distractors, density, habituation, budgets, cry-wolf dynamics), the streaming dynamics (transients, continuity, rate adaptation), and the validation stack as one salience-engineering discipline; the capstone workflow — the master arc: task-and-audience analysis (the viewer's questions and modes — monitoring, analysis, lookup; the expertise mix and accessibility requirements), the salience-specification (the intended attention hierarchy — what must pop first, second, never; the channel-allocation plan from the strength tables with redundancy for CVD), the composition execution (the quiet-canvas construction, the tier-one-two-three emphasis build, the encoding-channel selection per data type), the limits-audit (the conjunction, distractor, density, and budget checks plus the habituation-decay plan for persistent elements), the validation (the tier-matched evidence — seeded-anomaly tests, first-fixation verification, telemetry baselines), and the documentation (the salience-spec record with cue-channel rationale and evidence) as the complete professional cycle; the salience-as-service principle — the integration insight: preattentive channels as the display's steering system serving the viewer's decision tasks (the normative-salience discipline from section 8 — decision-relevance setting the hierarchy, capture-power allocated not by data loudness or designer taste but by the cost of missing each signal), making salience engineering an act of respect for the viewer's finite attention; cross-course integration — the curriculum position: Gestalt grouping (course 1) as the structural binding that salience rides on (the quiet-canvas and grouping-hierarchy preconditions), cognitive load (course 3) as the capacity economics salience serves (the attention-budget connection), the rendering APIs (courses 4-5) as the implementation layer where channel decisions become shader and style properties, the latency discipline (course 8) as the transient and feedback timing constraint, accessibility (course 9) as the redundant-channel mandate, EDA (course 11) as the data-truth check before salience-trust, scrollytelling (course 13) as the narrative-directed attention sequence, and executive display (course 15) as the glanceability-stakes context; case synthesis — the worked exemplars (the operations-monitoring wall redesign from everything-loud to tiered-salience with detection-time evidence, the trading-dashboard transient-grammar overhaul defeating alert fatigue, the public-health live dashboard balancing glanceability and accessibility, the analytics-tool emphasis system adapting to expert-novice divergence) decomposed through the capstone workflow; failure post-mortems — the canonical errors (salience inflation and the flatline dashboard, conjunction mislabeling as glance design, habituation-blind persistent alerts, single-channel CVD-fragile emphasis, silent-jump streaming, heatmap-theater validation) with the prevention systems from sections 12-14; ## Writing Standards

Throughout the material, prioritize language that is:

* Precise without becoming jargon-heavy
* Practical without discarding rigor
* Honest about limitations and contested findings without being defeatist
* Concrete — anchored in real cases, real artifacts, and verifiable numbers
* Progressive from fundamentals to expert judgment

Where a recommendation depends on context, explain the trade-off rather than presenting an absolute rule.

## Anti-Patterns

Avoid: Salience inflation — highlighting everything until nothing pops; preattentive power is relative and budget-limited, diluted by overuse (the cry-wolf effect on the salience map); Confusing salience with meaning — a popped element directs the eye but carries no interpretation; preattentive capture without deliberate-support structure leaves viewers looking at the right place without understanding; Conjunction blindness — expecting combinations of features (red AND large AND tilted) to pop when only single features do, producing serial-search tasks mislabeled as glances; Habituation neglect — persistent highlights and badges losing capture power through neural adaptation, the alert-fatigue dynamic killing always-on emphasis; Accessibility-assumed pop — preattentive effects varying with color vision (hue channels degraded for CVD viewers) and age, single-channel salience failing for part of the audience; exercises without evaluation criteria; modules that stop at inspiration without a single decision the learner can now make better; and any content that overstates certainty beyond what the evidence supports.

## Decision Heuristic

When allocating depth under time or length limits, ask:

1. Where will this audience actually stall in practice?
2. Which unit protects the most value if taught well?
3. Can the learner run the framework tomorrow without me?
4. Is every example doing work a plain sentence could not?
5. What would a skeptical domain expert say about this material?

If two topics compete for space, keep the one that changes a decision.
