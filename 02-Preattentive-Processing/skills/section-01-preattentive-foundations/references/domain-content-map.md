## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Introduces the discipline and its governing phenomenon: preattentive processing defined — the visual operations completed across the entire visual field in a single fixation, before and beneath conscious attention (the documented sub-200-to-250-millisecond regime, some effects faster), discovered through the experimental tradition (the Neisser dichoptic-search and Treisman feature-search findings: certain property differences detected in parallel regardless of display size — search time flat as item count grows, the operational definition of pop-out) making preattentivity measurable rather than mystical; the source thesis operationalized — visual attributes like color hue, size, orientation, and motion are processed in milliseconds before conscious attention kicks in, allowing the designer to direct the viewer's eye instantly: the design claim restated as engineering (emphasis is not decoration applied to importance but a pre-conscious attention-steering mechanism with measurable channel strengths and budgets); the two-stage vision model — the architecture preview: the early parallel stage (feature extraction and salience computation across the whole field — the preattentive layer this course dissects) versus the serial attentive stage (the focused, capacity-limited, object-by-object processing where comprehension and comparison live — the Gestalt-grouping course 1 as the binding layer between them), with the design implication: preattentive channels decide where attention goes, attentive processing decides what it means; the why-it-exists logic — the evolutionary-engineering view: the preattentive system as the biological early-warning and foraging scanner (the motion-onset priority for threat detection, the color-singularity sensitivity for ripe-fruit finding — the ecological rationale explaining channel priorities), making the channel hierarchy inherited hardware the designer programs against; the visualization stakes — the dashboard reality: modern displays exceed deliberate-processing capacity (hundreds of marks, dozens of panels — the course-3 load problem), so the fraction of information actually seen is determined largely by preattentive steering, making salience design the difference between a dashboard that gets read and one that gets glanced at and abandoned; the channel inventory previewed — the attribute families the course dissects: color (hue, saturation/chroma, lightness), form (size, length, width, orientation, shape, curvature), motion (direction, onset, flicker), and spatial (position, depth/ordering, enclosure) each with documented pop-out status and strength ranking; the search-paradigm foundation — the measurement instrument: visual-search experiments as the preattentivity test (the target-among-distractors task, the search-slope metric — flat slopes signaling parallel pop-out, steep slopes signaling serial attention, the set-size manipulation logic) as the evidence culture the course inherits; the salience-versus-salience-of-meaning distinction — the pitfall named early: preattive capture directs the eye but carries no interpretation (a red mark pops; why it matters requires attentive reading), making preattentive design the pointer layer that must be paired with deliberate-support structure (the two-layer design contract); the individual-difference reality — the audience variance: documented preattentive-strength variation across color-vision status (the CVD population's degraded hue channels — the course-9 integration), age (the attentional-capture decline findings), expertise (the top-down modulation — experts' task-guided search partially overriding bottom-up salience per the Wolfe model), making single-channel salience a reliability risk; the ethics-and-comfort frame introduced — capture as a finite resource: the habituation and fatigue dynamics (persistent highlights losing power — the alert-fatigue pattern), the dark-pattern adjacency (attention-capture techniques from persuasion design — the behavioral-course boundary awareness), and the comfort constraints (flicker limits, the motion-sensitivity obligations) as the responsibility layer; the practitioner identity — the salience-engineer profile (the designer who budgets pre-conscious capture deliberately: which channel, how much contrast, for which decision, verified by search-style testing) as the capability built; and the course map from the visual architecture through the color, form, motion, and spatial channel families into search measurement, salience-map models, top-down modulation, dashboard and data-encoding applications, limits and habituation, streaming displays, validation methods, and the capstone salience system.
