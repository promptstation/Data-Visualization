## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Design streaming displays with update-transient grammars (change-type signatures, parameter-tuned flashes, batching and priority queuing) defeating change blindness at data rate, render motion continuity through scroll conventions, tweening, and frame-rate decoupling with rate-adaptation (aggregation windows, burst triage, staleness signaling), support sustained vigilance through change-only signaling, alert coalescence, severity grading, and ambient peripheral channels, compose multi-stream layouts with priority hierarchies and synchronized temporal scaffolds.
2. Validate through seeded-event trials and flood stress tests preventing silent-jump, strobe, hypnosis, and frozen-display failures.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the live-data environment: the streaming-display problem — the temporal context: real-time visualizations (the monitoring walls, the trading and operations dashboards, the live-event graphics, the IoT sensor fields) where the display never settles — data arrives continuously, elements enter-exit-shift, and the viewer's task is sustained vigilance over a moving field, making static-salience science insufficient without the temporal dynamics; the update-transient design — the change-signaling craft: the transient-on-update patterns (the flash-highlight conventions — the brief luminance or color pulse marking changed values, the documented transient-capture advantage making unannounced changes invisible per the change-blindness findings from section 5), the transient-parameter tuning (the duration-magnitude decay curves — the flash length and intensity against update frequency, the overlap-management when multiple elements change simultaneously — the update-batching and priority-queuing), and the transient-type grammar (the value-change versus new-element versus threshold-crossing versus connection-loss distinct transient signatures — the change-type vocabulary); the motion-continuity craft — the flow rendering: the scrolling time-series conventions (the left-shift streaming with the motion-parallax continuity — the documented smooth-scroll versus jump-update comprehension trade-offs, the anchor-point stabilization keeping recent history readable), the interpolation-and-tweening of updating marks (the position-morph continuity preserving object identity — the course-1 animated-transition principle applied at data rate), and the frame-rate discipline (the 30-60fps rendering smoothness versus data-arrival rate decoupling — the render-loop aggregation preventing motion-judder); the rate-adaptation architecture — the speed governance: the data-rate-to-visual-rate mapping (the aggregation windows at high arrival rates — the sub-frame changes invisible anyway: the perceptual-aliasing prevention, the sampling-and-binning for display stability), the rate-spike handling (the burst-mode degradation strategies — the salience-triage under flood: exception-only rendering, the detail-suspension patterns), and the rate-communication (the data-freshness and lag indicators — the staleness-signaling preventing false-confidence in frozen displays) as the throughput-perception interface; the vigilance-support design — the human-factors layer: the sustained-monitoring degradation countermeasures (the section-9 vigilance-decay findings applied: the change-only-signaling imperative, the periodic summary-refreshes recapping state since last glance, the anomaly-history rails showing what already fired), the alarm-fatigue management at stream scale (the alert-coalescence — grouping related exceptions, the severity-graded channel allocation, the acknowledged-state visual retirement), and the ambient-mode design (the peripheral-vision monitoring channels — the gross luminance, motion, and large-form signals surviving divided attention, the glance-and-return workflow support); the multi-stream composition — the parallel-channels problem: the simultaneous-feed layouts (the multi-panel streaming walls with the cross-panel salience coordination — the section-10 control-room patterns at data-rate, the global-exception-beacon aggregating alerts across panels), the stream-priority hierarchies (the primary-focus stream with full detail versus secondary streams at reduced-salience summary — the attention-allocation architecture), and the cross-stream correlation support (the synchronized-time-axis alignment enabling the cross-panel pattern detection — the temporal common-fate scaffold); the latency-perception coupling — the responsiveness layer: the end-to-end latency budget (the sensor-to-pixel chain — the data pipeline, computation, and rendering latencies composing the perceived freshness, the sub-100ms interaction-feedback rule from the latency-tuning discipline connecting to course 8), the latency-visibility design (the timestamp and lag indicators, the interpolated-versus-confirmed value styling — the uncertainty-in-stream signaling) as the temporal-honesty layer; the historical-context integration — the now-and-then display: the streaming-with-context patterns (the recent-window detail plus historical-baseline overlay — the anomaly-versus-normal-band comparison enabling the expectation-violation salience from section 9, the time-shift comparison views — the today-versus-yesterday overlays), and the replay-and-scrub interfaces (the temporal-navigation controls with the salience-continuity during scrubbing) as the context-preserving stream design; the testing-streaming-salience — the validation practice: the seeded-event detection trials (the injected anomalies at realistic rates — the detection-time and miss-rate measurement across transient designs), the sustained-session testing (the time-on-task performance decay observation — the vigilance-decay verification of countermeasures), and the update-flood stress tests (the burst-mode comprehension checks) as the streaming-evidence system; the streaming anti-patterns — the failure catalog: the silent-jump stream (updates without transients — the change-blindness trap at data scale), the strobe-effect (update rates producing flicker-flood — the transient-overlap failure and seizure-risk adjacency), the scroll-hypnosis (persistent smooth motion capturing attention with no information — the ambient-motion tax), and the frozen-display-blindness (stale data indistinguishable from calm data — the staleness-invisibility failure) as the live-display hygiene; and the streaming deliverable — the transient-grammared, rate-adapted, vigilance-supporting live-display salience system.
