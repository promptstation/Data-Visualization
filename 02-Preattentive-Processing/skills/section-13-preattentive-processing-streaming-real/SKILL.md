# Preattentive Processing in Streaming and Real-Time Displays — Professional Training

## Mission

Produce professional-grade learning modules and training materials on **preattentive Processing in Streaming and Real-Time Displays** within The science of visual attributes (color hue, size, orientation, motion) processed in milliseconds before conscious attention — engineered to direct the viewer's eye instantly in data visualization.

Subject scope: Covers the live-data environment: the streaming-display problem — the temporal context: real-time visualizations (the monitoring walls, the trading and operations dashboards, the live-event graphics, the IoT sensor fields) where the display never settles — data arrives continuously, elements enter-exit-shift, and the viewer's task is sustained vigilance over a moving field, making static-salience science insufficient without the temporal dynamics; the update-transient design — the change-signaling craft: the transient-on-update patterns (the flash-highlight conventions — the brief luminance or color pulse marking changed values, the documented transient-capture advantage making unannounced changes invisible per the change-blindness findings from section 5), the transient-parameter tuning (the duration-magnitude decay curves — the flash length and intensity against update frequency, the overlap-management when multiple elements change simultaneously — the update-batching and priority-queuing), and the transient-type grammar (the value-change versus new-element versus threshold-crossing versus connection-loss distinct transient signatures — the change-type vocabulary); the motion-continuity craft — the flow rendering: the scrolling time-series conventions (the left-shift streaming with the motion-parallax continuity — the documented smooth-scroll versus jump-update comprehension trade-offs, the anchor-point stabilization keeping recent history readable), the interpolation-and-tweening of updating marks (the position-morph continuity preserving object identity — the course-1 animated-transition principle applied at data rate), and the frame-rate discipline (the 30-60fps rendering smoothness versus data-arrival rate decoupling — the render-loop aggregation preventing motion-judder); the rate-adaptation architecture — the speed governance: the data-rate-to-visual-rate mapping (the aggregation windows at high arrival rates — the sub-frame changes invisible anyway: the perceptual-aliasing prevention, the sampling-and-binning for display stability), the rate-spike handling (the burst-mode degradation strategies — the salience-triage under flood: exception-only rendering, the detail-suspension patterns), and the rate-communication (the data-freshness and lag indicators — the staleness-signaling preventing false-confidence in frozen displays) as the throughput-perception interface; the vigilance-support design — the human-factors layer: the sustained-monitoring degradation countermeasures (the section-9 vigilance-decay findings applied: the change-only-signaling imperative, the periodic summary-refreshes recapping state since last glance, the anomaly-history rails showing what already fired), the alarm-fatigue management at stream scale (the alert-coalescence — grouping related exceptions, the severity-graded channel allocation, the acknowledged-state visual retirement), and the ambient-mode design (the peripheral-vision monitoring channels — the gross luminance, motion, and large-form signals surviving divided attention, the glance-and-return workflow support); the multi-stream composition — the parallel-channels problem: the simultaneous-feed layouts (the multi-panel streaming walls with the cross-panel salience coordination — the section-10 control-room patterns at data-rate, the global-exception-beacon aggregating alerts across panels), the stream-priority hierarchies (the primary-focus stream with full detail versus secondary streams at reduced-salience summary — the attention-allocation architecture), and the cross-stream correlation support (the synchronized-time-axis alignment enabling the cross-panel pattern detection — the temporal common-fate scaffold); the latency-perception coupling — the responsiveness layer: the end-to-end latency budget (the sensor-to-pixel chain — the data pipeline, computation, and rendering latencies composing the perceived freshness, the sub-100ms interaction-feedback rule from the latency-tuning discipline connecting to course 8), the latency-visibility design (the timestamp and lag indicators, the interpolated-versus-confirmed value styling — the uncertainty-in-stream signaling) as the temporal-honesty layer; ## Anti-Patterns

Avoid: Salience inflation — highlighting everything until nothing pops; preattentive power is relative and budget-limited, diluted by overuse (the cry-wolf effect on the salience map); Confusing salience with meaning — a popped element directs the eye but carries no interpretation; preattentive capture without deliberate-support structure leaves viewers looking at the right place without understanding; Conjunction blindness — expecting combinations of features (red AND large AND tilted) to pop when only single features do, producing serial-search tasks mislabeled as glances; Habituation neglect — persistent highlights and badges losing capture power through neural adaptation, the alert-fatigue dynamic killing always-on emphasis; Accessibility-assumed pop — preattentive effects varying with color vision (hue channels degraded for CVD viewers) and age, single-channel salience failing for part of the audience; exercises without evaluation criteria; modules that stop at inspiration without a single decision the learner can now make better; and any content that overstates certainty beyond what the evidence supports.

## Decision Heuristic

When allocating depth under time or length limits, ask:

1. Where will this audience actually stall in practice?
2. Which unit protects the most value if taught well?
3. Can the learner run the framework tomorrow without me?
4. Is every example doing work a plain sentence could not?
5. What would a skeptical domain expert say about this material?

If two topics compete for space, keep the one that changes a decision.
