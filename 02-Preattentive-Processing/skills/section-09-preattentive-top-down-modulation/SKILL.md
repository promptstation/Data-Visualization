# Top-Down Modulation: Task Sets, Expertise, and Guided Attention — Professional Training

## Mission

Produce professional-grade learning modules and training materials on **Top-Down Modulation: Task Sets, Expertise, and Guided Attention** within The science of visual attributes (color hue, size, orientation, motion) processed in milliseconds before conscious attention — engineered to direct the viewer's eye instantly in data visualization.

Subject scope: Covers the viewer-side determinants of what pops: the task-set principle — the goal-directed filter: the documented top-down modulation of salience (the feature-attention weighting — observers prioritizing the channel matching their search goal: looking for red makes red pop harder and blue pop less, the contingent-capture findings — salience capturing attention only when matching the current attentional set), making the viewer's question a co-author of the display's effective salience; the dashboard-task taxonomy — the question types: the monitoring task (the exception-scan — what changed, what breached: the ambient-vigilance mode favoring motion-luminance channels and peripheral reach), the analysis task (the comparison-and-pattern mode: the deliberate foveal work where top-down structure and grouping dominate over raw salience), the lookup task (the find-specific-item mode: the guided-search with known-target templates) as the task-mode triad each demanding different salience architecture; the known-target versus unknown-target distinction — the search-mode fork: the feature-template guidance (knowing the target is 'the red spike' enables channel-weighted search — the fast guided mode) versus the category-definition targets ('find anything anomalous' — the no-template slow mode requiring the display to define anomaly salience itself) with the design implication (support both modes: engineered anomaly salience for the template-less scan plus searchable features for the targeted lookup); expertise modulation — the trained-attention reality: the documented expert-novice gaze differences (the experts' economical scanpaths — fewer fixations on informative regions, the schema-driven chunking, the reduced bottom-up capture — expertise partially immunizing against salience while increasing sensitivity to domain-meaningful patterns; the radiologist and chess-master paradigms transferred to analyst dashboards), with the dual-audience design problem (novices need stronger bottom-up steering; experts need it out of the way — the adaptive-emphasis solutions: progressive disclosure, role-based views, the dismissible-highlight patterns); expectation and priming — the context effects: the documented priming of feature channels (recent exposure to a feature enhancing its salience — the repetition-priming effects), the expectation-violation capture (the schema-disconfirmation salience — the surprising element in a predictable pattern popping beyond its feature contrast — the course-1 anomaly-science integration), and the narrative-context modulation (the story-framing directing attention — the scrollytelling preview for course 13: text primes setting the salience weights for the following chart); the vigilance-decay dynamics — the temporal top-down failure: the documented vigilance decrement (sustained-monitoring performance declining over time-on-task — the classic vigilance research, the rare-event signal-detection degradation), the attentional-resource depletion under cognitive load (the course-3 integration: high load increasing bottom-up capture susceptibility — tired viewers more distractible), and the monitoring-design responses (the alarm-rate management, the change-only signaling, the rotation-and-break patterns for human monitors); the multi-tasking reality — the divided-attention context: the documented interruption and task-switching costs (the salience-of-return problem — re-finding position after interruption, the bookmark-and-orientation design patterns), the ambient-display mode (the peripheral-monitoring design — the glanceable degradation hierarchy: what survives divided attention — motion, gross luminance, large-form channels only) as the attention-scarcity design; the individual-difference stack — the viewer-variability summary: the CVD and age effects (the section-3 channel losses, the documented older-viewer capture decline — the larger-contrast requirements), the cognitive-style variation (the field-dependence-independence findings, the need-for-cognition modulation of top-down effort), and the cultural scan-pattern variation (the reading-direction effects on spatial primacy — the course-17 cross-cultural caveat) as the audience-model inputs; the top-down-design levers — the steering toolkit: the task-framing devices (the titles and questions as attention-set primers — the interrogative-headline pattern, the annotation-as-task-guide — the arrow-and-callout directing deliberate attention), the progressive-disclosure sequencing (the layered salience — overview emphasis first, detail emphasis on interaction), and the training-and-documentation channel (the dashboard onboarding teaching the salience conventions — the learned-signal vocabulary) as the guided-search design practices; the modulation-measurement — the assessment layer: the task-condition search experiments (the same display tested under different instructed tasks — the salience-by-task interaction measurement), the expertise-comparison studies (the analyst-versus-novice gaze divergence quantification), and the telemetry segmentation (the production attention data split by user role and tenure) as the modulation-evidence methods; and the top-down deliverable — the task-mode-aware, expertise-calibrated, expectation-managed salience design.

Write as an experienced practitioner, not as a summarizer of popular content. Every framework taught must be something a real team or professional could run: procedures they can execute, criteria they can judge with, and artifacts they can hand to a colleague. Do not present claims as settled when the field treats them as contested — the training must model evidence discipline.

The module deepens this section's capabilities for a learner progressing from competent beginner toward expert practitioner, and connects them to the surrounding discipline rather than teaching them in isolation.

## Writing Standards

Throughout the material, prioritize language that is:

* Precise without becoming jargon-heavy
* Practical without discarding rigor
* Honest about limitations and contested findings without being defeatist
* Concrete — anchored in real cases, real artifacts, and verifiable numbers
* Progressive from fundamentals to expert judgment

Where a recommendation depends on context, explain the trade-off rather than presenting an absolute rule.

## Anti-Patterns

Avoid: Salience inflation — highlighting everything until nothing pops; preattentive power is relative and budget-limited, diluted by overuse (the cry-wolf effect on the salience map); Confusing salience with meaning — a popped element directs the eye but carries no interpretation; preattentive capture without deliberate-support structure leaves viewers looking at the right place without understanding; Conjunction blindness — expecting combinations of features (red AND large AND tilted) to pop when only single features do, producing serial-search tasks mislabeled as glances; Habituation neglect — persistent highlights and badges losing capture power through neural adaptation, the alert-fatigue dynamic killing always-on emphasis; Accessibility-assumed pop — preattentive effects varying with color vision (hue channels degraded for CVD viewers) and age, single-channel salience failing for part of the audience; exercises without evaluation criteria; modules that stop at inspiration without a single decision the learner can now make better; and any content that overstates certainty beyond what the evidence supports.

## Decision Heuristic

When allocating depth under time or length limits, ask:

1. Where will this audience actually stall in practice?
2. Which unit protects the most value if taught well?
3. Can the learner run the framework tomorrow without me?
4. Is every example doing work a plain sentence could not?
5. What would a skeptical domain expert say about this material?

If two topics compete for space, keep the one that changes a decision.
