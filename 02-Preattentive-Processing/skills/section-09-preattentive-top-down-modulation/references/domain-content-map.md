## Absorbed capabilities this module must produce
1. Design for top-down modulation through task-set awareness (monitoring, analysis, lookup modes with channel-matched architectures), support known-target and template-less anomaly search simultaneously, calibrate dual-audience emphasis (novice steering versus expert efficiency) through progressive and dismissible patterns.
2. Manage vigilance decay, interruption costs, and ambient-divided-attention contexts with change-only and glanceable-degradation design, prime expectations through titles, annotations, and narrative context.
3. Measure modulation through task-condition experiments and role-segmented telemetry.

## Unit 1 — Unit 1

**Scope:** Covers the viewer-side determinants of what pops: the task-set principle — the goal-directed filter: the documented top-down modulation of salience (the feature-attention weighting — observers prioritizing the channel matching their search goal: looking for red makes red pop harder and blue pop less, the contingent-capture findings — salience capturing attention only when matching the current attentional set), making the viewer's question a co-author of the display's effective salience; the dashboard-task taxonomy — the question types: the monitoring task (the exception-scan — what changed, what breached: the ambient-vigilance mode favoring motion-luminance channels and peripheral reach), the analysis task (the comparison-and-pattern mode: the deliberate foveal work where top-down structure and grouping dominate over raw salience), the lookup task (the find-specific-item mode: the guided-search with known-target templates) as the task-mode triad each demanding different salience architecture; the known-target versus unknown-target distinction — the search-mode fork: the feature-template guidance (knowing the target is 'the red spike' enables channel-weighted search — the fast guided mode) versus the category-definition targets ('find anything anomalous' — the no-template slow mode requiring the display to define anomaly salience itself) with the design implication (support both modes: engineered anomaly salience for the template-less scan plus searchable features for the targeted lookup); expertise modulation — the trained-attention reality: the documented expert-novice gaze differences (the experts' economical scanpaths — fewer fixations on informative regions, the schema-driven chunking, the reduced bottom-up capture — expertise partially immunizing against salience while increasing sensitivity to domain-meaningful patterns; the radiologist and chess-master paradigms transferred to analyst dashboards), with the dual-audience design problem (novices need stronger bottom-up steering; experts need it out of the way — the adaptive-emphasis solutions: progressive disclosure, role-based views, the dismissible-highlight patterns); expectation and priming — the context effects: the documented priming of feature channels (recent exposure to a feature enhancing its salience — the repetition-priming effects), the expectation-violation capture (the schema-disconfirmation salience — the surprising element in a predictable pattern popping beyond its feature contrast — the course-1 anomaly-science integration), and the narrative-context modulation (the story-framing directing attention — the scrollytelling preview for course 13: text primes setting the salience weights for the following chart); the vigilance-decay dynamics — the temporal top-down failure: the documented vigilance decrement (sustained-monitoring performance declining over time-on-task — the classic vigilance research, the rare-event signal-detection degradation), the attentional-resource depletion under cognitive load (the course-3 integration: high load increasing bottom-up capture susceptibility — tired viewers more distractible), and the monitoring-design responses (the alarm-rate management, the change-only signaling, the rotation-and-break patterns for human monitors); the multi-tasking reality — the divided-attention context: the documented interruption and task-switching costs (the salience-of-return problem — re-finding position after interruption, the bookmark-and-orientation design patterns), the ambient-display mode (the peripheral-monitoring design — the glanceable degradation hierarchy: what survives divided attention — motion, gross luminance, large-form channels only) as the attention-scarcity design; the individual-difference stack — the viewer-variability summary: the CVD and age effects (the section-3 channel losses, the documented older-viewer capture decline — the larger-contrast requirements), the cognitive-style variation (the field-dependence-independence findings, the need-for-cognition modulation of top-down effort), and the cultural scan-pattern variation (the reading-direction effects on spatial primacy — the course-17 cross-cultural caveat) as the audience-model inputs; the top-down-design levers — the steering toolkit: the task-framing devices (the titles and questions as attention-set primers — the interrogative-headline pattern, the annotation-as-task-guide — the arrow-and-callout directing deliberate attention), the progressive-disclosure sequencing (the layered salience — overview emphasis first, detail emphasis on interaction), and the training-and-documentation channel (the dashboard onboarding teaching the salience conventions — the learned-signal vocabulary) as the guided-search design practices; the modulation-measurement — the assessment layer: the task-condition search experiments (the same display tested under different instructed tasks — the salience-by-task interaction measurement), the expertise-comparison studies (the analyst-versus-novice gaze divergence quantification), and the telemetry segmentation (the production attention data split by user role and tenure) as the modulation-evidence methods; and the top-down deliverable — the task-mode-aware, expertise-calibrated, expectation-managed salience design.
